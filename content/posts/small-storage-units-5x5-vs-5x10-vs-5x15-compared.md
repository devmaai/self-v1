**Introduction**

Small storage units usually mean 5x5, 5x10 and 5x15 spaces, which cost about $35 to $110 a month in most US cities before climate control. A 5x5 holds a closet's worth of boxes, a 5x10 fits the contents of a studio or small one-bedroom, and a 5x15 handles a one-bedroom apartment with room to spare. This guide compares the three sizes side by side, shows the price gap between each step, and helps you pick the smallest unit that truly fits your things.

## What counts as a small storage unit?

Most facilities call any unit under 100 square feet a small storage unit. The three common sizes share a 5-foot width and differ only in depth, so you can compare them easily.

| Size | Floor space | Roughly the size of | Common access type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | 25 sq ft | A large hall closet | Indoor, often upper floor |
| 5x10 | 50 sq ft | A walk-in closet | Indoor or drive-up |
| 5x15 | 75 sq ft | A small bathroom | Indoor or drive-up |

A few things to keep in mind:

- Ceiling heights usually run 8 feet or more, so you can stack boxes well above head height.

- The 5x15 is less common than the other two, so not every facility lists one.

- The smallest storage unit at many facilities is a locker of 4x4 or smaller, often stacked in a row.

For a full picture of a single size, see our guides to the 5x5 storage unit and the 5x10 storage unit. This post focuses on choosing between the small sizes.

## How much do small storage units cost?

A 5x5 averages about $35 to $55 a month, a 5x10 about $60 to $80, and a 5x15 about $80 to $110 in a typical US market. Climate control adds roughly 20% to 40% on top.

| Size | Typical monthly cost | With climate control | Cost per sq ft per month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $35 to $55 | $50 to $85 | About $1.40 to $2.20 |
| 5x10 | $60 to $80 | $80 to $125 | About $1.20 to $1.60 |
| 5x15 | $80 to $110 | $100 to $150 | About $1.07 to $1.47 |

What the numbers tell you:

- Each 5-foot step up costs about $20 to $30 more per month.

- Small units cost more per square foot than large ones, so a 5x10 can be better value than a packed 5x5.

- Prices in dense cities such as New York City, Los Angeles and San Francisco can run 30% to 60% above these ranges, while smaller inland markets often come in lower.

Large national operators such as Public Storage and Extra Space Storage list small units online with live rates, so you can compare sizes in your area in a few minutes. For a wider view of rates and fees across every size, read how much is a storage unit.

## 5x5 vs 5x10 vs 5x15: which size fits your load?

Choose a 5x5 for boxes and seasonal items, a 5x10 for a studio or a dorm room, and a 5x15 for a one-bedroom apartment or a small home office.

| If you are storing | Best fit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 to 15 boxes and holiday decor | 5x5 | Enough room to stack boxes to the ceiling |
| A dorm room or a few pieces of furniture | 5x10 | Fits a twin mattress, a desk and boxes |
| A studio apartment | 5x10 | Room for a queen mattress, a dresser and boxes |
| A one-bedroom apartment | 5x15 | Adds space for a sofa and a small table |
| Business files, samples or stock | 5x10 or 5x15 | Space for a row of shelving and an aisle |
| Bikes, sports gear and camping kit | 5x10 | Long items fit along the back wall |

A simple rule: if your list fits in a 5x5 only when every inch is packed, step up to a 5x10. You will save time and avoid damage when you need to reach something at the back. Our storage size guide shows each size with sample layouts.

## What fits in a 5x15 storage unit?

A 5x15 storage unit fits the contents of a one-bedroom apartment without large appliances, which makes it the most flexible of the small sizes.

You can usually fit:

- A queen or full mattress and bed frame

- A loveseat or small sofa

- A dresser and a nightstand

- A small dining table and two to four chairs

- A TV, a desk and 15 to 25 medium boxes

Because fewer facilities build 5x15 units, they can be harder to find in busy summer months. Reserve early if you have a move date, and ask the facility to confirm the exact dimensions, since some list a 5x12 or a 6x15 under a similar label.

## How to choose the right small storage unit

Choose the smallest unit that holds everything with a little room to reach your items, then confirm access, floor level and climate needs before you sign.

Follow these steps:

- List your bulky items first. Mattresses, sofas and dressers decide the size more than boxes do.

- Measure anything over 5 feet. A 5-foot-wide unit cannot fit a long sofa sideways, so check lengths against the depth.

- Use uniform boxes. Same-size boxes stack higher and safer, which can let you drop a size.

- Pick the floor and access type. Ground-floor and drive-up units cost more but save effort if you visit often.

- Decide on climate control. Wood, leather, electronics and paper do better in a climate controlled unit. Our climate controlled storage guide explains when it is worth the extra cost.

- Try the calculator. The storage unit size calculator estimates the right size from your list of items.

If a small unit will not hold everything, compare the next sizes up in our guide to large storage units.

## How to save money on small storage units

You can often cut the cost of small storage units by 10% to 30% by sizing down where you can, booking online and renting in the off-season.

- Rent in fall or winter: demand drops between October and February, and so do rates.

- Book online: web rates and move-in deals are often lower than walk-in prices. Learn how first month free storage offers work before you sign.

- Choose an upper floor: indoor units reached by elevator usually cost less than ground-floor or drive-up units.

- Skip climate control for sturdy items: plastic bins, tools and sports gear rarely need it in mild areas.

- Compare a few facilities nearby: rates for the same size can vary widely across one city. You can find storage near you and compare options in one place.

- Ask about the rate after the promo: introductory prices often rise after a few months.

## Frequently asked questions

What is the smallest storage unit size? The smallest standard self storage unit is usually a 5x5, at 25 square feet. Some facilities also rent lockers of 4x4 or smaller for a few boxes.

How much is a 5x15 storage unit? A 5x15 storage unit typically costs about $80 to $110 a month, or $100 to $150 with climate control, depending on your city.

Is a 5x10 big enough for a one-bedroom apartment? A 5x10 can hold a small one-bedroom if you pack tightly and skip large appliances. A 5x15 gives you more room and an easier way to reach your things.

What is the difference between a 5x5 and a 5x10? A 5x10 is twice as deep and adds 25 square feet of floor space. It usually costs about $20 to $30 more per month.

Can a mattress fit in a 5x5 storage unit? A twin mattress can stand on its side in a 5x5, but a queen or king usually needs at least a 5x10.

Are small storage units more expensive per square foot? Yes, in most cases. Small units cost more per square foot than large units, so moving up one size can be better value when you will use the space.

Can I store a motorcycle in a small storage unit? Many motorcycles fit in a 5x10 or 5x15 drive-up unit. Check the facility rules, since some require the tank to be nearly empty and the battery disconnected.

How far ahead should I reserve a small storage unit? Reserve one to two weeks ahead in spring and summer, and earlier near college move-out dates, when small units fill fast.
