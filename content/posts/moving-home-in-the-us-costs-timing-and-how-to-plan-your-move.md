**Introduction**

Moving home in the US usually costs $800 to $2,500 for a local move with professional movers and $2,500 to $7,500 or more for a long-distance move, while a DIY move with a rental truck can come in under $600 locally. Your final bill depends on how much you own, how far you go, when you move, and how much of the work you hand off. This guide covers typical moving costs by home size, the cheapest and most expensive times to move, how to build a realistic budget, an 8-week timeline, packing advice, when to hire help, and how to use a storage unit to make the whole move easier.

## How much does it cost to move home in the US?

Most local moves with a moving company cost $800 to $2,500, and most long-distance moves cost $2,500 to $7,500, with large homes crossing the country running $10,000 or more. Local moves are usually billed by the hour, while long-distance moves are priced by weight or volume and distance.

| Type of move | DIY truck | Moving container | Full-service movers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local (under 100 miles) | $200 to $600 | $300 to $1,500 | $800 to $2,500 |
| Long distance (500 to 1,000 miles) | $1,200 to $3,000 | $2,000 to $5,500 | $2,500 to $7,500 |
| Cross country (2,000+ miles) | $1,800 to $4,000 | $3,500 to $7,000 | $4,000 to $10,000+ |

Quick take:

- Local labor typically runs $80 to $130 an hour for two movers and a truck, and $110 to $170 an hour for three movers.

- Most companies set a minimum, often two to four hours, even for a small job.

- Long-distance quotes depend on the weight of your shipment, the miles, and the services you add, such as packing or storage.

These are typical ranges. Get at least three written quotes for your own move, since prices for the same job can vary widely between companies.

## How much does it cost to move by home size?

Home size is the biggest driver of what you pay, because more rooms mean more weight, more boxes, and more hours of labor. Here is what full-service moves typically cost.

| Home size | Local move | Long distance (about 1,000 miles) | Typical crew |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Studio or 1 bedroom | $400 to $1,200 | $1,500 to $3,500 | 2 movers |
| 2 bedrooms | $800 to $2,200 | $2,500 to $5,500 | 2 to 3 movers |
| 3 bedrooms | $1,200 to $3,200 | $3,500 to $7,500 | 3 to 4 movers |
| 4 bedrooms | $1,800 to $4,500 | $5,000 to $10,000 | 4 movers |
| 5+ bedrooms | $2,500 to $6,000+ | $7,000 to $14,000+ | 4 to 5 movers |

The fastest way to cut these numbers is to move less. Every box you sell, donate, or toss before moving day saves weight on a long-distance quote and time on a local one. A good rule is to declutter room by room at least four weeks before your move.

## DIY move vs movers vs moving containers: which is cheapest?

A DIY move with a rental truck is almost always the cheapest option, a moving container sits in the middle, and full-service movers cost the most but save you the most time and effort.

- DIY truck rental: local truck rentals often start around $20 to $90 a day plus mileage, depending on truck size. One-way long-distance rentals usually cost $600 to $3,200 before fuel. You do all the packing, loading, and driving.

- Moving containers: a company drops a portable container at your home, you load it on your schedule, and they drive it to your new place or hold it in storage. Our guide to how moving containers work covers costs, sizes, and how to compare companies.

- Hybrid move: you rent the truck or container and hire moving help for loading and unloading, which often costs a few hundred dollars for a two or three hour job.

- Full-service movers: the crew packs, loads, drives, and unloads. This is the easiest option for families, busy schedules, and long-distance moves with lots of furniture.

| Option | Best for | Main trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DIY truck | Small homes, short distances, tight budgets | Your time, effort, and fuel |
| Moving container | Flexible timing, moves that need storage in between | Needs space for the container at both ends |
| Hybrid (truck plus labor) | Saving money without heavy lifting | You still pack and drive |
| Full-service movers | Large homes, long distances, busy households | Highest cost |

## What hidden moving costs should you budget for?

Extra fees and one-off expenses commonly add $1,000 to $3,000 to a family move, so plan for them from the start. These are the costs people forget most often.

- Packing supplies: $100 to $300 for boxes, tape, bubble wrap, and markers if you pack yourself.

- Professional packing: about $300 to $1,500 depending on home size. See our upcoming guide to what packing services cost.

- Access fees: stairs, elevators, and long walks from the truck to your door can add $50 to $150 each.

- Bulky or specialty items: pianos, safes, pool tables, and large artwork can cost $200 to $1,000 or more to move.

- Valuation coverage: basic carrier liability is limited, so full value protection often adds $100 to $500 or more.

- Tips: $20 to $60 per mover is common, more for long or difficult days.

- Cleaning: a move-out clean usually costs $100 to $400.

- Deposits and utilities: a rental security deposit, plus setup or transfer fees for power, internet, and water.

- Temporary housing and storage: if your old and new move dates do not line up, budget for a hotel or short-term rental and a storage unit.

- Vehicle shipping: $800 to $1,500 if you are not driving your car to the new home.

## When is the best time of year to move?

The cheapest time to move is usually mid-September through April, with January and February often offering the lowest rates. Summer, from about May through September, is peak moving season, and moves then commonly cost 20% to 30% more.

| Timing | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| May to September | Highest demand, highest prices, crews and trucks book up fast |
| October and November | Lower rates and mild weather in much of the country |
| December to February | Lowest rates, but winter weather can slow things down in the north |
| March and April | Prices start rising as spring demand builds |

A few more timing tips:

- Move midweek. Monday through Thursday is usually cheaper than Friday through Sunday, and Tuesday and Wednesday are often the best value.

- Avoid the first and last days of the month, when many leases start and end. The middle two weeks are usually easier to book and cheaper.

- Book early in peak season. For a summer move, reserve movers or a truck four to eight weeks ahead.

- Think about your climate. Summer heat in places like Phoenix or Houston and winter snow in places like Minneapolis or Buffalo can make moving day harder, so plan an early start or a flexible backup date.

## How do you make a moving budget?

Start with a written estimate for the move itself, add every extra cost on your list, then add a buffer of 15% to 20% for surprises. A clear budget stops small costs from piling up into a big bill.

Here is a simple way to build yours:

- Get three quotes. Ask for written estimates based on an in-home or video survey, not a quick phone guess.

- List your extras. Supplies, packing help, access fees, specialty items, tips, cleaning, deposits, and utilities.

- Add travel costs. Fuel, meals, hotels, and pet travel for long-distance moves.

- Add storage if you need it. A unit for a month or two during the move usually costs $75 to $300 a month depending on size and city. Our guide to how much a storage unit costs breaks down prices by size.

- **Add a 15% to 20% buffer.**

| Example: 2-bedroom local move | Typical cost |
| --- | --- |
| Movers (3 movers, about 5 hours) | $700 to $1,000 |
| Packing supplies | $100 to $200 |
| Tips | $60 to $150 |
| Move-out cleaning | $150 to $300 |
| One month of storage (10x10) | $100 to $200 |
| Buffer (15%) | $165 to $280 |
| Estimated total | $1,275 to $2,130 |

Some moving costs tied to a new job can be reimbursed by an employer, and active-duty military members have separate rules for moving expenses. Check with your employer or a tax professional about your own situation.

## What is a good moving timeline?

Give yourself about eight weeks to plan a move, and more for a large home or a long-distance move. Here is a simple week-by-week timeline.

**8 weeks before**

- Set your moving date and budget.

- Request quotes from movers, truck rental companies, or container companies.

- Start decluttering one room at a time.

**6 weeks before**

- Book your movers, truck, or container.

- Order packing supplies.

- Reserve a storage unit if your dates do not line up.

**4 weeks before**

- Start packing items you rarely use, such as books, décor, and seasonal clothes.

- Submit a change of address with USPS and update banks, insurance, and subscriptions.

- Schedule utility shutoff at the old home and setup at the new one.

**2 weeks before**

- Confirm dates and arrival times with your movers.

- Use up food in the freezer and pantry.

- Arrange childcare or pet care for moving day.

**1 week before**

- Pack everything except daily essentials.

- Defrost the fridge and freezer.

- Pack an essentials box with chargers, medicine, toiletries, documents, and a change of clothes.

**Moving day**

- Walk through each room with the crew lead.

- Check the inventory list before you sign.

- Do a final sweep of closets, cabinets, and the garage.

## How should you pack for a move?

Pack room by room, use sturdy boxes of a few standard sizes, and label every box with its contents and the room it goes to. Good packing protects your things and speeds up both loading and unloading.

- Use small boxes for heavy items like books and kitchenware, and large boxes for light items like bedding and pillows.

- Fill empty space with towels, clothes, or packing paper so items do not shift.

- Wrap fragile items one by one and mark those boxes "Fragile" on the top and sides.

- Take photos of electronics before unplugging them so you can reconnect cables later.

- Disassemble furniture and keep screws in labeled bags taped to each piece. For dressers, sofas, and appliances, our guide on how to move heavy furniture without damage covers safe lifting and protection.

- Keep valuables with you, including jewelry, passports, and important documents.

- Color-code by room with stickers or tape so movers know where each box belongs.

If you pack yourself, plan for roughly one to two days per room for a typical family home. If time is tight, many moving companies offer partial packing for the kitchen and fragile items only, which costs less than a full pack.

## Should you hire movers or moving help?

Hire full-service movers if you have a large home, a long-distance move, or little time, and hire hourly moving help if you want to save money and are happy to pack and drive yourself. Either way, check the company before you book.

**What to check before you hire:**

- Licensing: interstate movers must be registered with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration and have a USDOT number you can look up.

- Written estimates: ask whether the quote is binding, non-binding, or binding not to exceed.

- Valuation coverage: understand what is covered and how claims work.

- Reviews: read recent reviews and look at how the company responds to problems.

- Deposits: be cautious of large upfront deposits, especially for local moves.

## How can a storage unit help during a move?

A storage unit gives you a safe place for your belongings when your move-out and move-in dates do not match, when you are staging a home for sale, or when you are downsizing. Most people use storage for one to three months during a move.

**Common ways to use storage during a move:**

- Bridging a gap between closing on one home and getting the keys to the next.

- Staging your home for sale by clearing out extra furniture and personal items.

- Downsizing while you decide what to keep, sell, or give to family.

- Moving to a new city before finding a permanent home, so you can rent short term first.

- Renovating your new home before you move everything in.

## How do moving and storage prices differ by city?

Moving and storage prices follow local real estate and labor costs, so the same move or unit can cost far more in a big coastal city than in a smaller inland one. Hourly mover rates and storage rents in places like New York City, San Francisco, Los Angeles, Boston, and Seattle often sit well above the national average. Parking permits, elevator bookings, and stairs can add more in dense cities. Movers and storage in cities like Kansas City, St. Louis, Salt Lake City, Sacramento, Columbus, and Indianapolis are usually more affordable.

**How to choose a storage facility during a move:**

- Location first. Pick a facility near your new home if you will unload it within weeks, or near your old home if you are staging a sale.

- Easy truck access. Wide driveways, drive-up units, and a loading area save hours on moving day.

- Access hours that fit your schedule. Early starts and late finishes are common on moving day, so 24-hour access storage can help.

- Security: gated entry, cameras, good lighting, and on-site staff.

- Compare three or four facilities, since prices for the same size can vary by 30% or more within a few miles.

## Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to move a 2-bedroom home? A local move for a 2-bedroom home usually costs $800 to $2,200 with professional movers. A long-distance move of about 1,000 miles typically runs $2,500 to $5,500.

What is the cheapest month to move? January and February usually have the lowest moving rates. October and November are also good value, with milder weather in much of the country.

How far in advance should I book movers? Book four to eight weeks ahead for a summer move and two to four weeks ahead in the off-season. Long-distance moves need more notice than local ones.

Is it cheaper to rent a truck or hire movers? Renting a truck and doing it yourself is usually the cheapest option. Hiring hourly loading help with a rental truck is a good middle ground if you want to avoid heavy lifting.

How much should I tip movers? A common range is $20 to $60 per mover for a standard day. Tip more for long hours, stairs, or very heavy items.

How long does it take to plan a move? Give yourself about eight weeks for most moves. Large homes and long-distance moves benefit from 10 to 12 weeks of planning.

Do I need a storage unit when I move? You need one if your move-out and move-in dates do not line up, or if you are staging, downsizing, or renovating. Many people rent a unit for one to three months.

What size storage unit do I need for a 3-bedroom house? Most 3-bedroom homes fit in a 10x20 unit. If you also have a garage full of tools or large patio furniture, consider a 10x25 or 10x30.
