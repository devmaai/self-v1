**Introduction**

A temperature controlled storage unit usually costs about 20% to 50% more than a standard unit, and SelfStorage.help's storage cost guide puts a temperature controlled 10x10 at roughly $145 to $205 a month. These units are heated and cooled to stay inside a set range, often around 55 to 80 degrees, so your belongings avoid freezing winters and baking summers. In this guide you will see what temperature controlled storage is, how it compares with full climate control, what it costs by size, which items need it, and how to pick the right facility near you.

## What is temperature controlled storage?

Temperature controlled storage is a self storage unit that is heated and cooled to keep the air inside a set range all year. Most of these units sit inside an enclosed building with insulated walls and a shared HVAC system, so the temperature in your unit stays close to the hallway temperature whatever the weather is doing outside.

Here is what you can usually expect:

- **A set temperature range:** most facilities aim for roughly 55 to 80 degrees, though the exact range varies by operator.

- **Indoor access:** you reach the unit through a lobby or hallway, with no outside door on the unit itself.

- **Steadier conditions:** fewer swings between day and night, and between seasons.

- **Humidity control is not always included:** some facilities manage temperature only, while others also run dehumidifiers. Always ask.

If you want the full picture of how indoor climate systems work at storage facilities, our [complete guide to climate controlled storage in the US](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/climate-controlled-storage-in-the-us-everything-you-need-to-know>) covers the basics in depth.

## Temperature controlled vs climate controlled storage: what is the difference?

The main difference is humidity. A temperature controlled unit manages heat and cold, while a climate controlled unit manages heat, cold and moisture together. Many facilities use the two phrases interchangeably, so the only way to know what you are paying for is to ask how the building handles humidity.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Keeps units warm in winter | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Keeps units cool in summer | No | Yes | Yes |
| Manages humidity | No | Sometimes | Yes |
| Typical range | Above about 50 degrees | About 55 to 80 degrees | About 55 to 80 degrees, with steady humidity |
| Best for | Cold-winter areas, short stays | Most furniture, electronics, clothing | Wood, leather, paper, art, instruments |

If you only need protection from freezing, a heated unit may be enough. Our guide to [heated storage units and when you need one](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/heated-storage-units-what-they-are-costs-and-when-you-need-one>) explains that option.

Humidity matters because mold and mildew grow when moisture stays high. The [EPA's guide to mold and moisture](<https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home>) recommends keeping indoor relative humidity below 60%, and ideally between 30% and 50%. If you live somewhere humid, a unit that controls moisture as well as temperature is the safer choice.

## What should you keep in a temperature controlled storage unit?

You should keep anything that can crack, warp, melt or corrode in a temperature controlled unit. Heat and cold are the biggest risks for these items:

- **Wood furniture:** wood expands and contracts with temperature swings, which loosens joints and splits finishes. Our guide on [how to store furniture in a storage unit without damage](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/how-to-store-furniture-in-a-storage-unit-without-damage>) covers wrapping and placement.

- **Electronics and appliances:** TVs, computers and gaming consoles can suffer from condensation and extreme heat.

- **Documents and photos:** paper yellows and photos stick together in heat. See our guide to [storing paperwork and records safely](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/document-storage-units-how-to-store-paperwork-and-records-safely>).

- **Vinyl records, DVDs and media:** high heat can warp discs and records.

- **Musical instruments:** pianos, guitars and violins need stable conditions to stay in tune and intact.

- **Clothing, leather and fabrics:** steady conditions help prevent mildew, odors and cracking.

- **Candles, cosmetics and wax items:** these can melt in a summer storage unit.

**You can usually skip temperature control for:** metal tools, garden equipment, plastic patio furniture and items you are storing for a few weeks in a mild season.

## Is a temperature controlled self storage unit worth it?

A temperature controlled self storage unit is worth it when the cost of replacing your belongings is higher than the extra rent. For a 10x10, that premium is often $30 to $75 a month, which is small next to the cost of a ruined sofa, piano or laptop.

It tends to pay off when:

- You are storing for longer than one season.

- You live somewhere with hot summers, freezing winters or both.

- Your items have high or sentimental value.

- You plan to store wood, leather, paper or electronics.

It may not be worth it when you are storing durable items for a short stay in a mild part of the year.

## How do prices for storage units differ by city?

Prices for storage units follow local real estate, so the same temperature controlled 10x10 can cost far more in a dense coastal city than in a smaller inland market. Starting prices on SelfStorage.help show this spread clearly: climate controlled listings start from $41 a month in Los Angeles and from $10 a month in St. Louis.

When you choose a facility, think about location as well as price:

- **Distance from home:** a unit 10 minutes away is easier to visit than a cheaper one across town.

- **Access hours:** check whether the building is open when you need it.

- **Route and parking:** a loading area close to the entrance saves time on move-in day.

- **Neighborhood rates:** prices a few miles apart can differ by 30% or more, so compare a few nearby options.

## How to choose the right temperature controlled storage unit

To choose the right temperature controlled storage unit, confirm the conditions, the size and the true monthly cost before you sign. Use this checklist:

- **Ask for the exact temperature range** the facility maintains year round.

- **Ask whether humidity is managed,** and how, if you are storing wood, paper or leather.

- **Pick the right size** using our [storage size guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-size-guide>), so you are not paying to cool empty space.

- **Check the building:** clean hallways, sealed doors and no signs of leaks are good signs.

- **Confirm security:** gated access, cameras and good lighting.

- **Ask about the rate after any promotion** and how often rent can increase.

- **Review protection options** and whether your home or renter policy already covers stored items.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a temperature controlled storage unit?** It is an indoor self storage unit that is heated and cooled to stay within a set range, often about 55 to 80 degrees. It protects your belongings from extreme heat and cold.

**Is temperature controlled the same as climate controlled storage?** Not always. Temperature controlled units manage heat and cold, while climate controlled units also manage humidity. Many facilities use both terms, so ask what the building controls.

**How much more does temperature controlled storage cost?** It usually costs about 20% to 50% more than a standard unit. For a 10x10, that often means an extra $30 to $75 a month.

**What temperature are temperature controlled storage units kept at?** Most facilities keep units between about 55 and 80 degrees. The exact range depends on the operator, so ask before you rent.

**Do I need temperature controlled storage in winter?** If you live where temperatures drop below freezing, it helps protect electronics, liquids, wood and instruments. A heated unit can be a lower-cost option for short winter stays.

**Can I store furniture in a temperature controlled storage unit?** Yes. Wood, upholstered and leather furniture are some of the best items to keep in a temperature controlled unit, since steady temperatures help prevent warping and cracking.
