**Introduction**

The 10x10 is still the single most rented storage unit size in San Diego, but the 5x10 runs a much closer second here than it does in cheaper, less crowded metros. That pattern follows how San Diego actually lives: a huge population of renters and apartment dwellers packed into a coastline with very little room left to build, plus a massive military community that moves on a fixed government schedule every year. This guide ranks the sizes San Diego renters pick most, breaks down what each one costs across the metro, and helps you match a size to your own move, deployment, or apartment cleanout.

## What is the most popular storage unit size in San Diego?

The 10x10 holds the top spot in San Diego, the same as it does nationwide, because it fits a one to two-bedroom household's worth of furniture and boxes without forcing you to pay for square footage you will not use.

Ranked by how often San Diego renters choose each size:

- 10x10: the most rented size in the metro, the default pick for an apartment move or a basic household cleanout.

- 5x10: a close second in San Diego, more popular here than in many cities because the metro's apartment and condo stock is so large relative to single-family homes.

- 5x5: common in San Diego's expensive core neighborhoods, where renters often downsize to the smallest unit that will still hold their seasonal gear and overflow boxes.

- 10x15: used for full two to three-bedroom households, including renters combining homes or clearing out a garage.

- 10x20: less common than in sprawling metros, reserved mostly for full-home moves, vehicle storage, or small business stock.

- 10x30: the least common size, limited to whole-home storage, multiple vehicles, or commercial inventory.

This mirrors the nationwide pattern, where the 10x10 and 5x10 together make up roughly half of all rentals, but San Diego leans even harder toward the smaller end because land is scarce, real estate is expensive, and so many residents live in apartments and condos rather than houses with garages.

## Why smaller units do so well in San Diego

A 5x10 unit holds roughly the contents of a studio or one-bedroom apartment once boxes and a few pieces of furniture are stacked. That size, along with the 5x5, performs better in San Diego than in most major cities for a few reasons:

- Far more renters than homeowners. San Diego has one of the largest shares of apartment and condo dwellers of any major metro, so the typical move involves less furniture than a single-family home.

- Extremely limited, expensive land. Coastal and central San Diego have little room left for new facilities, so supply stays tight and every square foot costs more, which pushes renters toward the smallest unit that will do the job.

- A large, mobile military population. Naval Base San Diego, Naval Base Point Loma, and Marine Corps Base Camp Pendleton keep thousands of service members and their families moving every year, and most active-duty households store a modest amount of furniture rather than a full house.

If you only need to store the contents of a one-bedroom apartment, a 5x10 unit is usually enough. If you are clearing out a full house, size up to a 10x20 instead of renting two separate smaller units.

## Which size fits your situation in San Diego

- Studio or one-bedroom apartment: a 5x10 covers a typical studio, while a 5x5 can work if you are only storing boxes and small items.

- One or two-bedroom apartment or condo: a 10x10 gives enough room for furniture, boxes, and a mattress or two.

- Single-family home, 2 to 3 bedrooms: a 10x15 typically fits a full household, including a sofa, appliances, and bedroom sets.

- Full house, a garage's worth of items, or a vehicle: a 10x20 or 10x30 gives room for furniture from every room plus a car, boat, or small business's stock.

- PCS move or deployment: many military families land in a 5x10 or 10x10 depending on whether they are storing a full household or just the extras that will not fit in base housing.

- Business inventory or equipment: most small San Diego businesses start around a 10x10 to 10x15 and move up as stock grows.

## Climate control and size in San Diego

Climate control is less of a must-have in San Diego than in humid or extreme-temperature markets, since the city's mild, semi-arid climate rarely swings far from comfortable room temperature. Even so, plenty of renters choose it for specific reasons.

- Climate-controlled units typically add 20% to 35% to the base rate in San Diego, in line with the national average.

- Coastal neighborhoods deal with salt air and ocean humidity, which can corrode metal, warp wood, and damage electronics over time, even without extreme heat.

- Inland communities away from the coast see noticeably hotter summer temperatures, where a standard unit can still reach uncomfortable highs that stress sensitive items.

- It is worth paying for if you are storing wood or leather furniture, electronics, documents, photos, musical instruments, or anything staying in storage longer than a few months.

- You can usually skip the upgrade for tools, patio furniture, camping gear, and short stays of a few weeks.

## Tips to pick the right size and avoid overpaying in San Diego

- Measure your largest items first (sofas, mattresses, appliances, a vehicle) since they set the minimum size you need, not the number of boxes.

- Stack instead of sizing up. Vertical shelving can let a 5x10 or 10x10 do the job of a larger unit for most apartment moves.

- Ask about the rate after the intro period. San Diego facilities often lead with a promotional rate that climbs after a few months.

- Compare drive-up versus climate-controlled indoor units, since drive-up units in San Diego often run noticeably cheaper for the same square footage.
