**Introduction**

[Student storage](/student-storage) in the US usually costs $40 to $80 a month for a [5x5 unit](/blog/5x5-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside), $60 to $120 for a 5x10, and $100 to $180 for a 10x10 you can split with roommates, while per-item pickup services charge roughly $14 to $40 per item per month. For a typical summer break of about three months, most students spend $150 to $400 in total. This guide covers when you need storage, the options that fit a student budget, what size to rent, how to share a unit, when to book, and how to pack a dorm room so everything comes back in good shape.

## How much does student storage cost?

Most college students pay $40 to $120 a month for their own small unit, or about $35 to $60 each when three or four friends split a 10x10. Here are typical monthly prices across the US.

| Unit size | Standard unit | Climate controlled | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $40 to $80 | $50 to $100 | One dorm room of boxes, a mini fridge, a bike |
| 5x10 | $60 to $120 | $75 to $150 | One or two dorm rooms, a futon, a desk |
| 10x10 | $100 to $180 | $130 to $230 | A shared apartment with a bed, couch, and boxes |
| Pickup service (per item) | $14 to $40 per item | Usually included | A few boxes and a suitcase, no car needed |

Quick take:

- Facilities close to campus often charge more than those a few miles out, because demand spikes in May and June.

- Monthly billing is usually not prorated. If you move in May 15 and out August 20, you will likely pay for four months.

- Budget for move-in costs too, such as an admin fee of around $25 to $50, a lock, and a protection plan. Our guide to [how much a storage unit costs](/blog/how-much-is-a-storage-unit) breaks those down.

## When do college students need storage?

Most students need storage when they leave campus housing for more than a few weeks and cannot easily take everything home. The most common times are:

- Summer break: dorms close in May or June and reopen in August. Storing near campus saves you from hauling a carload of stuff across the country twice.

- Study abroad: a semester or a full year overseas means you need a safe place for your belongings for four to twelve months.

- Summer internships: if you are spending ten weeks in another city, you can store most of your things and take only what you need.

- Gaps between leases: off-campus leases often end in May and start in August, leaving a gap of a few weeks to a few months.

- Winter break and transfers: shorter stays, transferring schools, or graduating and waiting to start a job can all leave you between homes.

If your break is only a few weeks, our guide to [short-term storage](/blog/short-term-storage-options-costs-and-how-it-works) covers flexible options that suit shorter gaps.

## What are your student storage options?

You have four main options, and the right one depends on how much you own, whether you have a car, and how much you want to spend.

- Self storage unit: you rent a unit at a local facility, move your things in yourself, and pick them up whenever you like. It is the cheapest choice per square foot when you have furniture or several roommates sharing.

- Student pickup services: a company drops off boxes, collects them from your dorm, stores them in a warehouse, and delivers them back at move-in. You pay per item, so it works best for a handful of boxes and you do not need a car.

- Campus storage: some colleges offer summer storage rooms or partner with a storage company. Spaces are limited and often fill fast, so check your housing office early.

- [Storage lockers](/blog/what-is-a-storage-locker-sizes-costs-and-when-to-rent-one): small lockers of around 5x5 or smaller suit a few boxes and a suitcase. Our guide on what a storage locker is explains sizes and prices.

| Option | Best for | Pros | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Self storage unit | Furniture, roommates, longer stays | Lowest cost for lots of items, access any time | You need a vehicle and helpers to move in |
| Pickup service | A few boxes, no car | Door-to-door, little effort | Per-item pricing adds up fast with furniture |
| Campus storage | Light loads | Very close to your dorm | Limited space and fixed dates |
| Storage locker | A few boxes and a suitcase | Cheapest small option | Fits very little |

A simple rule: if you have fewer than about eight items, a pickup service often costs about the same as a unit and saves you a lot of effort. Once you add a mattress, a desk, or a couch, a shared self storage unit almost always wins on price.

## What size storage unit does a college student need?

Most single students need a 5x5 or 5x10, and groups of roommates sharing an apartment usually need a 10x10. Size up based on your bulkiest items, not your box count.

- 5x5 (25 sq ft): about 10 to 15 boxes, a mini fridge, a lamp, bedding, and a bike. Fits the contents of a typical dorm room. See our 5x5 storage unit guide.

- 5x10 (50 sq ft): a twin mattress, a desk and chair, a dresser, and 15 to 20 boxes. Good for one student with furniture, or two dorm rooms. See our [5x10 storage unit](/blog/5x10-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside) guide.

- 10x10 (100 sq ft): the furniture and boxes from a two or three bedroom student apartment, including a couch and full-size beds. See our [10x10 storage unit](/blog/10x10-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside) guide.

Before you book, lay everything out in one spot and picture it stacked. Paying for empty space is the most common way students overspend. For more help, try our [storage size guide](/storage-size-guide).

## Should you share a storage unit with roommates?

Sharing a unit is one of the easiest ways to cut your storage bill, as long as you agree on the rules up front. Four students splitting a $140 10x10 each pay about $35 a month, less than most solo 5x5 units.

| Setup | Typical monthly cost | Cost per person |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo 5x5 | $40 to $80 | $40 to $80 |
| Two people in a 5x10 | $60 to $120 | $30 to $60 |
| Three people in a 10x10 | $100 to $180 | $33 to $60 |
| Four people in a 10x10 | $100 to $180 | $25 to $45 |

To keep sharing smooth:

- Put one person on the lease and agree in writing how everyone pays them, with a payment app reminder set before each due date.

- Label every box with the owner's name and keep a shared inventory list.

- Agree on access. Decide who holds the key or gate code and how others can get in over the summer.

- Plan the move-out day so the unit is emptied before the next billing date.

- Check the facility rules, since some only let people named on the lease enter.

## When should you book student storage?

Book three to four weeks before your move-out date. Units near campus fill quickly in the last two weeks of the semester, and small sizes like 5x5 and 5x10 are the first to go.

- Spring semester ending in May: reserve by mid-April.

- Study abroad starting in August or January: book about a month before you leave, and choose a lease you can extend online.

- Move in a few days before finals if you can. Moving day crowds, full elevators, and booked rental trucks all get worse once exams end.

- Watch the billing cycle. Moving out a few days before a new month starts can save you a full month's rent.

Storage prices are highest in late spring and summer, so the best deals often come from booking early before rates rise. Our breakdown of [first month free](/blog/first-month-free-storage-how-the-deals-work) storage deals explains how move-in promotions work and what to check before you sign.

## Do storage facilities offer student discounts?

Some do. Discounts vary by location rather than by brand, so ask each facility directly and have your student ID or school email ready. Even without a formal student rate, you can usually save money in other ways.

**Ways to pay less for student storage:**

- Book online. Web rates and first-month offers are often lower than walk-in prices.

- Look a few miles from campus. Facilities outside the student zone often charge noticeably less for the same size.

- Split a unit with roommates or teammates.

- Prepay for the summer if the facility offers a discount for paying several months at once.

- Skip climate control for sturdy items in mild climates, but keep it for electronics, books, and anything you will store through a hot, humid summer.

- Use your parents' homeowners policy if it covers your belongings in storage, rather than buying the facility plan.

- Sell or donate what you will not use next year. Many campuses run end-of-year donation drives.

## How do you pack a dorm room for storage?

Pack in sturdy boxes of the same size, keep heavy items small, and label everything clearly. Good packing protects your things and helps you fit more into a smaller, cheaper unit.

**Packing checklist:**

- Use matching medium boxes or plastic bins. They stack evenly and are easier to carry up and down stairs.

- Put books in small boxes so they stay light enough to lift.

- Clean and defrost the mini fridge at least a day before. Dry it fully and leave the door slightly open in storage to prevent mold.

- Wash bedding and clothes before packing, and use vacuum bags to save space.

- Wrap electronics in towels or their original boxes, and keep laptops, passports, and valuables with you.

- Remove all food and drinks, including sealed snacks, which can attract pests.

- Take apart furniture such as bed risers, shelves, and desks, and tape screws to the pieces they belong to.

- Label every box on two sides with your name, the contents, and the room it goes to.

- **Load heavy items first and place boxes you will need in August at the front.**

Do not store anything flammable, perishable, or hazardous, such as aerosol cans, candles with lighters, or cleaning chemicals. Most facilities list these in the lease.

## How should you choose a storage facility near campus?

Choose a facility that is easy to reach on move-out and move-in day, has good security, and offers flexible month-to-month leases. Convenience matters most for students, since you may not have a car and will be moving on the busiest days of the year.

**What to check before you book:**

- Distance and transport. Can you get there on a bus line, by bike, or with a short rental truck trip? A facility five minutes from campus can save you a lot of hassle.

- Access hours. Late finals and early flights may need evening or [24-hour access](/blog/24-hour-access-storage-units-what-you-need-to-know-before-you-rent).

- Ground floor or elevator access so you are not carrying a mattress up several flights of stairs.

- Security: gated entry, cameras, good lighting, and on-site staff during office hours.

- Climate control if you are storing electronics, instruments, books, or anything sensitive to heat and humidity. Learn more about [climate controlled storage](/climate-controlled-storage).

- Free truck or move-in help. Some facilities include a free truck on move-in day, which is a big help if you do not drive.

- Lease terms. Look for month-to-month leases, online payments, and simple move-out notice rules.

How prices differ by city: student storage follows local real estate, so the same unit near a campus in [Los Angeles](/storage-search/california/los-angeles), [San Francisco](/storage-search/california/san-francisco), Boston, or [Manhattan](/storage-search/new-york/manhattan) can cost twice as much as one in a smaller college town. Students in places like [Provo](/storage-search/utah/provo), [Syracuse](/storage-search/new-york/syracuse), [St. Louis](/storage-search/missouri/st-louis), Austin, or Gainesville will usually find more choice and lower rates, especially a few miles from campus. In any city, compare three or four facilities, since prices for the same size can vary widely within a short drive.

## Do you need insurance for student storage?

Most facilities require some protection for the items you store, either through their own plan or proof of your own coverage. Facility plans usually cost about $8 to $40 a month.

- Check your family's homeowners policy. Many policies cover a dependent student's belongings stored away from home, up to a set limit.

- Check your renters policy if you rent off campus. Our guide on whether [renters insurance](/blog/does-renters-insurance-cover-storage-units) covers storage units explains how this usually works.

- Pickup services often include basic coverage per item, so ask about the limit and whether electronics need original packaging.

- Take photos of your belongings and keep your inventory list on your phone.

## How do you plan storage for study abroad or a summer internship?

For study abroad or an internship, choose a longer lease you can manage online, store items you will not need for months, and give someone at home a way to reach the unit. These stays usually run four to twelve months, so a little extra planning pays off.

- Choose autopay so you never miss a payment while you are in another time zone. Missed payments can lead to late fees and, eventually, a lien on your belongings.

- Add a trusted contact to your account so a parent or friend can access the unit if needed.

- Pick climate control for longer stays, especially for electronics, textbooks, and clothing.

- Pack a separate box of things you will want the day you return, and place it near the door.

- Confirm the rate after any promotion, since rents can rise during a long stay.

## Frequently asked questions

How much does student storage cost for the summer? Most students spend $150 to $400 for a summer of storage, depending on unit size, location, and whether they share. Splitting a 10x10 with roommates often brings the cost under $150 per person.

What size storage unit do I need for a dorm room? A 5x5 unit usually holds the contents of one dorm room, including boxes, bedding, and a mini fridge. If you have a mattress, desk, or dresser, choose a 5x10.

When should I book summer storage? Book three to four weeks before you move out, ideally by mid-April for a May move-out. Small units near campus sell out first.

Can college students share a storage unit? Yes. Most facilities allow sharing, but one person usually signs the lease. Agree on payments and access in writing before you move in.

Is a pickup service or a storage unit better for students? A pickup service is often better for a few boxes and no car. A self storage unit usually costs less once you have furniture or several people sharing.

Do storage facilities give student discounts? Some do, depending on the location. Ask with your student ID ready, and compare online rates and first-month offers, which can save more than a student discount.

Can I store a mini fridge in a storage unit? Yes. Clean and defrost it, dry it fully, and leave the door slightly open so mold and odors do not build up.

Do I need insurance for student storage? Most facilities require coverage. Check whether your family's homeowners policy or your renters policy covers stored items before buying the facility plan.
