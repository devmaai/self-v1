**Introduction**

A small storage unit is one of the most affordable ways to handle document storage: a [5x5 unit](/blog/5x5-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside) costs about $35 to $70 a month and holds roughly 50 to 80 standard file boxes, while a climate controlled 5x10 can hold well over 100 boxes for around $80 to $125. Paper is sensitive to heat, damp, and pests, so the way you pack and shelve your records matters as much as the unit you pick. This guide covers which unit size fits your files, what document storage costs, how long to keep common records, and a simple system for packing, labeling, and finding paperwork when you need it.

## Is a self storage unit a good place to store documents?

Yes, a self storage unit works well for paperwork you need to keep but rarely open, such as old tax returns, closed client files, medical and legal records, and archived business documents. It gives you more space than a home office and more access than a records center, at a lower monthly cost for most households and small businesses.

A storage unit suits you best when:

- You have more than a few boxes and they are crowding your home or office.

- You need to reach files a few times a year, often on short notice.

- You want to keep costs predictable with one flat monthly rent.

- You run a small business and want a dedicated place for archives. Our guide to storage units for business covers other ways business owners use a unit.

A professional records center can make more sense if you have thousands of boxes, need certified shredding, or must follow strict chain-of-custody rules in a regulated industry.

## What size storage unit do you need for documents?

Most people storing paperwork need a 5x5 or [5x10 unit](/blog/5x10-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside). A standard file box (about 15 x 12 x 10 inches) takes up roughly one cubic foot, and with sturdy shelving you can stack boxes safely to about five or six feet high.

| Unit size | Approximate file boxes | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small locker (5x3 to 4x4) | 15 to 35 | Personal tax records and family papers |
| 5x5 | 50 to 80 | A household archive or a sole trader's records |
| 5x10 | 120 to 180 | A small office, plus a filing cabinet or two |
| 10x10 | 250 to 350 | Several years of records for a growing business |
| 10x15 | 400 to 550 | Large archives, with room for an aisle and a desk |

Leave a walkway so you can reach the back rows. For more detail on the smallest options, see our 5x5 storage unit guide and 5x10 storage unit guide, or use the [storage size guide](/storage-size-guide).

## How much does document storage cost in self storage units?

Document storage in a self storage unit typically costs $35 to $250 a month, depending on size, location, and whether you add climate control. Larger cities sit at the top of that range, and smaller markets sit near the bottom.

| Option | Typical cost | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small locker | $25 to $60 a month | A handful of boxes |
| 5x5 self storage unit | $35 to $70 a month | Household or sole trader archives |
| 5x10 [climate controlled unit](/climate-controlled-storage) | $80 to $125 a month | Small business records |
| 10x10 climate controlled unit | $150 to $250 a month | Larger business archives |
| Records center (per box) | About $0.25 to $0.60 per box a month, plus retrieval fees | Very large or regulated archives |

Records centers can look cheap per box, but pickup, retrieval, and delivery fees add up if you need files often. With your own unit, you pay one rent and visit as often as you like. Our breakdown of [how much a storage unit costs](/blog/how-much-is-a-storage-unit) explains the fees to budget for, such as admin fees and protection plans.

## Do you need climate controlled storage for documents?

For anything you plan to keep longer than a season, climate controlled storage is the safer choice. Paper and photos last longest at about 60 to 70 degrees with relative humidity between 30% and 50%. High humidity leads to mold, curling, and ink transfer, while extreme heat makes paper brittle and yellow over time.

Climate control is especially worth it if:

- You live somewhere with hot summers, such as [Fresno](/storage-search/california/fresno) or [Sacramento](/storage-search/california/sacramento), humid summers, such as [New York City](/storage-search/new-york/new-york-city), or freezing winters, such as [Buffalo](/storage-search/new-york/buffalo) or [Syracuse](/storage-search/new-york/syracuse).

- You are storing original legal papers, deeds, photos, or artwork.

- You expect to keep the records for several years.

You can usually manage without it for short stays in a mild climate, as long as boxes sit off the floor in sealed containers. Our climate controlled storage guide covers how these units work, and you can compare options on the climate controlled storage page.

## How to store paper documents long term

Good packing protects paperwork from moisture, crushing, and pests. Follow these steps before anything goes into your unit.

### Choose the right containers

- Plastic bins with tight lids give the best protection against moisture and pests.

- Heavy-duty file boxes with lids work well in a climate controlled unit and are easier to label and stack.

- Acid-free folders and sleeves help originals and photos last for decades.

- Skip old grocery boxes, which crush easily and attract insects.

### Prepare the paperwork

- Remove rubber bands, paper clips, and staples where you can, since they rust and stain over time.

- Keep papers flat and upright in hanging folders rather than folded or rolled.

- Do not overfill boxes. A box you can lift comfortably, around 25 to 30 pounds, is easier to move and less likely to split.

### Set up the unit

- Use metal shelving to keep boxes off the floor and allow airflow.

- Put the files you reach most often at waist height near the door.

- Leave a few inches between boxes and walls.

- Never store food, liquids, or anything damp in the same unit.

## How to organize and label records so you can find them

A clear system turns a wall of boxes into an archive you can search in minutes.

- Number every box and write the number on at least two sides.

- Label by category and date range, for example "Taxes 2019 to 2021" or "Client files A to F."

- Color code by type, such as blue for taxes, green for property, and red for legal.

- Keep a master index in a spreadsheet or notes app that lists each box number, its contents, and its shelf location.

- Add a destroy-by date to each box so you know when it can be shredded.

Review the unit once a year, shred anything past its retention date, and update your index. If you plan to stop by after work or at weekends, look for a facility with extended hours. ## How long should you keep important documents?

Retention periods vary, and the right timeline for you depends on your situation. The table below shows common guidelines based on IRS recordkeeping guidance. Check with your tax professional or attorney for records that apply to your business or state.

| Record type | Common guideline |
| --- | --- |
| Tax returns and supporting records | At least 3 years after filing |
| Records for underreported income over 25% | 6 years |
| Claims for bad debt or worthless securities | 7 years |
| Employment tax records | At least 4 years |
| Property records (purchase and improvements) | Until you sell, plus the tax period after the sale |
| Birth certificates, deeds, wills, passports | Keep permanently |

Keep documents you might need urgently, such as passports, birth certificates, and original wills, in a fireproof safe at home or a bank safe deposit box. A storage unit is ideal for the bulky archives that support those records.

## How to keep stored documents secure

Paperwork often holds personal and financial details, so security matters as much as climate.

- Pick a facility with gated access, cameras, and good lighting. Indoor units add another layer of protection.

- Use a disc lock or cylinder lock that resists cutting.

- Scan sensitive records and keep a digital backup in a secure cloud service or encrypted drive.

- Check your coverage. Many facilities offer a protection plan, and your home or renters policy may already cover stored items. Our article on whether [renters insurance](/blog/does-renters-insurance-cover-storage-units) covers storage units explains what to ask.

When choosing a facility, convenience counts. A unit close to your home or office makes quick retrievals easy, and prices can differ a lot between neighborhoods, so compare a few nearby options before you sign. ## Frequently asked questions

Can you store documents in a storage unit? Yes. Self storage units are a common choice for tax records, business archives, and family papers. Pack them in sealed containers, keep them off the floor, and choose climate control for long-term storage.

What size storage unit do I need for file boxes? A 5x5 unit holds about 50 to 80 standard file boxes, and a 5x10 holds about 120 to 180. Shelving helps you use the full height safely.

Is climate controlled storage necessary for paperwork? It is strongly recommended for anything stored longer than a few months, or in hot, humid, or freezing climates. Paper lasts longest at about 60 to 70 degrees with 30% to 50% humidity.

How much does it cost to store documents? Most people pay $35 to $125 a month for a 5x5 or 5x10 unit, depending on location and climate control. Records centers charge per box plus retrieval fees.

What is the best way to store paper documents long term? Use plastic bins or sturdy lidded file boxes, acid-free folders, and metal shelving. Remove metal clips, keep papers flat, and store everything in a climate controlled unit.

Should I store original birth certificates or passports in a storage unit? It is better to keep these in a fireproof safe at home or a bank safe deposit box, so you can reach them quickly when you need them.

How long should a business keep records? Many tax records should be kept for at least 3 years, employment tax records for at least 4 years, and some records for 7 years or longer. Confirm the right timeline with your accountant.
