**Introduction**

[Vehicle storage](/car-storage) in the US typically costs $50 to $150 a month for an outdoor parking space, $75 to $250 for a covered spot, and $150 to $500 for a fully enclosed unit, depending on the size of the vehicle and where you live. A standard car fits in a [10x20 unit](/blog/10x20-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside), while most RVs and boats need an outdoor or covered parking space of 25 to 45 feet. This guide walks you through every option, what each one costs, how to prepare a car, an RV, or a boat so it starts right up when you come back, and how to pick a facility you can trust with something this valuable.

## How much does vehicle storage cost?

Vehicle storage costs depend on three things: how much protection you want, how long the vehicle is, and the local market. Here are typical monthly ranges across the US.

| Vehicle | Outdoor (uncovered) | Covered (canopy or carport) | Enclosed unit | Climate controlled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Car or small SUV | $50 to $150 | $75 to $200 | $150 to $350 | $200 to $450 |
| Motorcycle | $30 to $75 | $50 to $100 | $60 to $150 (5x10 or 10x10) | $90 to $200 |
| RV or camper (20 to 45 ft) | $30 to $200 | $50 to $250 | $100 to $450+ | $150 to $550+ |
| Boat on a trailer | $50 to $150 | $75 to $250 | $150 to $500 | $200 to $600 |

Quick take:

- Outdoor parking is the cheapest way to store any vehicle and works well for short stays.

- Longer vehicles pay more. A 40-foot Class A motorhome can cost two to three times as much as a pop-up camper in the same lot.

- Marinas usually price boats by the foot. Dry stack storage runs about $15 to $35 per foot per month, and seasonal shrink wrapping adds roughly $14 to $30 per foot.

- Budget for the usual move-in costs too, such as an admin fee and a protection plan. Our guide to [how much a storage unit costs](/blog/how-much-is-a-storage-unit) covers those fees in detail.

## What types of vehicle storage can you choose from?

You have four main choices, and each one trades price for protection.

- Outdoor parking: a marked space in a fenced, paved or gravel lot. It is the lowest-cost option and fine for vehicles built to live outside, such as trucks, trailers, and boats with a quality cover.

- Covered parking: a space under a roof or canopy with open sides. It blocks sun, hail, snow, and bird droppings, which protects paint, tires, and RV roof seals.

- Enclosed storage: a drive-up unit with a roll-up door, usually 10x20 or larger. Your vehicle is fully out of the weather and out of sight. Our comparison of drive-up vs indoor storage units explains how access works.

- [Climate controlled storage](/climate-controlled-storage): an enclosed space that holds temperature and humidity steady. It makes sense for classic cars, collector motorcycles, and boats with wood or leather interiors. Read more about climate controlled storage.

A simple rule: the more the vehicle is worth and the longer you plan to store it, the more protection pays for itself. A daily driver parked for one summer can sit outside under a good cover, while a restored classic you plan to keep for years belongs indoors.

## How do you store a car long term?

To store a car long term, clean it, top off the fluids, protect the battery and tires, and keep pests out. These steps take an afternoon and save you from dead batteries, flat-spotted tires, and stale fuel when you return. For a full checklist, see our guide on how to store a car long term.

**Before you park:**

- Wash and wax the car. Dirt, sap, and bird droppings can etch paint over time. Clean the interior and remove all food and wrappers.

- Change the oil. Used oil holds contaminants that can wear engine parts while the car sits.

- Fill the tank and add fuel stabilizer. A full tank limits moisture buildup, and stabilizer keeps gas fresh for up to a year or more. Run the engine for a few minutes so the treated fuel reaches the lines.

- Protect the battery. Use a battery tender if the facility offers power, or disconnect the negative cable. Electric vehicles do best stored at a partial charge, so check your owner's manual.

- Inflate the tires. Pump them to the pressure listed on the door jamb or slightly above it to help prevent flat spots.

- Skip the parking brake. Brake pads can stick to rotors over months. Use wheel chocks instead.

- Block pests. Place steel wool in the exhaust tip (with a reminder note on the steering wheel) and set rodent deterrents around the car.

- Use a breathable cover. Plastic tarps trap moisture. A fitted, breathable car cover keeps dust off and lets the paint breathe.

While it is stored: visit every few weeks if you can. Check tire pressure, look for leaks or signs of pests, and make sure the battery tender is working. Starting the engine for just a minute or two does more harm than good, so either drive it for 15 minutes or leave it off.

## How do you store an RV?

To store an RV, empty and clean the tanks, protect the plumbing from freezing, seal out pests and moisture, and choose a space that keeps the roof out of direct sun. RVs have more systems than a car, so give yourself a full day for prep. Our [RV storage](/rv-storage) guide goes deeper on each step.

**RV storage checklist:**

- Dump and flush the tanks. Empty the black and gray tanks at a dump station and rinse them well.

- Winterize the plumbing in cold climates. Drain the fresh water tank and water heater, bypass the heater, and run RV antifreeze through the lines. Skip this and a single hard freeze can crack pipes and fittings.

- Clear the fridge and pantry. Remove all food, wipe down the fridge, and prop the door open to stop mold and odors.

- Handle the batteries. Charge and disconnect house and chassis batteries, or keep them on a maintainer.

- Treat the fuel and generator. Fill the tank, add stabilizer, and run the generator briefly so treated fuel cycles through it.

- Protect the exterior. Inspect roof seams and window seals, close blinds to block UV, cover the tires, and use a breathable RV cover if you are parking outside.

- Keep critters out. Seal gaps around pipes and vents and set out rodent deterrents.

Covered storage is the sweet spot for most RV owners. It costs less than enclosed space and shields the roof membrane and seals, which are among the priciest parts of an RV to repair.

## How do you store a boat?

To store a boat, winterize the engine, treat the fuel, drain all water, and cover or shrink wrap the hull before parking it on its trailer or in a rack. Your choices range from an outdoor lot to indoor dry stack storage at a marina. Our guide on how to store a boat covers each option in more detail.

**[Boat storage](/boat-storage) checklist:**

- Clean the hull and deck. Remove salt, algae, and grime so they do not harden over the season.

- Winterize the engine. Change the oil and filter, flush the cooling system, and fog the cylinders on gas engines. Many owners have a marine shop do this.

- Fill and stabilize the fuel. A nearly full tank with stabilizer reduces condensation.

- Drain all water. Empty bilges, livewells, water systems, and the head, and pull the drain plug.

- Remove electronics and soft items. Take cushions, life jackets, and portable electronics home to avoid mildew and theft.

- Cover it properly. A fitted cover or shrink wrap keeps out rain, snow, and animals. Leave vents so moisture can escape.

- Check the trailer. Inflate the tires, grease the bearings, and lift the trailer tongue slightly so rainwater drains off the stern.

If you live near the water, dry stack storage at a marina is convenient because staff launch the boat for you. If you store inland, a self storage facility with outdoor or covered boat parking is usually the more affordable choice.

## What size storage space do you need for a vehicle?

Measure your vehicle from bumper to bumper, including any trailer tongue, mirrors, or outboard motor, then add about 2 to 3 feet so you can open doors and walk around it.

| Vehicle | Typical length | Recommended space |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motorcycle or scooter | 6 to 8 ft | [5x10 unit](/blog/5x10-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside) |
| Compact or midsize car | 14 to 16 ft | 10x20 unit |
| Full-size SUV or pickup | 17 to 20 ft | 10x25 unit or 10x20 parking space |
| Boat on a trailer (up to 20 ft) | 18 to 24 ft | [10x30 unit](/blog/10x30-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside) or 25 ft parking space |
| Travel trailer or Class C RV | 20 to 33 ft | 12x35 parking space |
| Class A motorhome or fifth wheel | 26 to 45 ft | 12x40 to 12x50 parking space |

The 10x20 is the go-to enclosed unit for cars. Our guide to the 10x20 storage unit shows exactly what fits, and the 10x30 storage unit guide covers trucks, boats, and a vehicle plus extra belongings. Also check the door height. Roll-up doors are often 7 to 8 feet tall, which rules out most RVs and boats with towers or T-tops.

## What should you look for in a vehicle storage facility?

Look for strong security, easy access for a large vehicle, and clear rules on what you can do on site. Visit in person before you sign, since a lot that looks fine online can be tight to maneuver in.

**Your facility checklist:**

- Security: gated keypad entry, good lighting, perimeter fencing, and cameras that cover the parking rows.

- Access hours: if you take your boat out at dawn or return late from a road trip, look for extended or [24-hour access](/blog/24-hour-access-storage-units-what-you-need-to-know-before-you-rent).

- Room to maneuver: wide drive aisles and pull-through spaces make life much easier with an RV or a long trailer.

- Surface: paved lots drain better and are kinder to tires than dirt or gravel.

- Power outlets: some covered and enclosed spaces include electricity for a battery tender.

- Amenities for RVs and boats: dump stations, air pumps, and wash bays are worth paying a little more for if you use your vehicle often.

- Lease rules: ask about required registration and insurance, whether you can work on the vehicle on site, and how much notice you need to give before moving out.

## Do you need insurance for a stored vehicle?

Yes, most facilities ask for proof that the vehicle is registered and insured, and their own protection plans often cover belongings but exclude vehicles. Keep your auto, RV, or boat policy active while the vehicle is in storage.

- Ask about storage or comprehensive-only coverage. Many insurers let you drop collision and keep comprehensive while the vehicle is parked, which still covers theft, fire, and weather damage at a lower premium.

- Keep liability if your state requires it. Some states require continuous liability coverage to keep the registration active, so check with your DMV before you reduce coverage.

- Financed or leased vehicle? Your lender will likely require full coverage the whole time.

- Document the condition. Take dated photos of the vehicle inside and out before you park it.

Coverage for belongings works differently. Our guide on whether [renters insurance](/blog/does-renters-insurance-cover-storage-units) covers storage units explains how home and renter policies apply to stored items.

## Short-term vs long-term vehicle storage: what changes?

For storage under about a month, a clean vehicle, a covered or outdoor space, and a quick battery check are usually enough. For storage of three months or more, follow the full prep steps above and choose more protection.

|   | Short term (under 3 months) | Long term (3 months or more) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best storage type | Outdoor or covered | Covered, enclosed, or climate controlled |
| Fuel | Normal tank | Full tank with stabilizer |
| Battery | Usually fine | Tender or disconnect |
| Tires | Normal pressure | Inflate to top of range, consider jack stands |
| Insurance | Keep current policy | Ask about storage coverage |
| Lease | Month-to-month | Ask about prepay or long-term discounts |

Common short-term reasons include a move, a renovation, or a summer away. Our guide to [short-term storage](/blog/short-term-storage-options-costs-and-how-it-works) covers flexible lease options. Long-term storage usually means seasonal RV and boat storage, a military deployment, or a classic car kept for weekend drives.

## How do vehicle storage prices differ by city, and how should you choose a location?

Vehicle storage prices follow local land costs, so the same space can cost twice as much in one metro as in another. Dense coastal cities have the least room for large parking lots, which pushes prices up.

- Higher-cost markets: cities like [Los Angeles](/storage-search/california/los-angeles), [San Francisco](/storage-search/california/san-francisco), Boston, and [New York City](/storage-search/new-york/new-york-city) often sit at the top of each range, and outdoor RV parking can be hard to find close to the city center.

- Lower-cost markets: metros like [Salt Lake City](/storage-search/utah/salt-lake-city), [Kansas City](/storage-search/missouri/kansas-city), [St. Louis](/storage-search/missouri/st-louis), Phoenix, and Indianapolis tend to have more land and more competition, so rates are often lower.

- Suburbs and edge-of-town facilities are usually cheaper than those in the urban core, especially for RV and boat parking.

**How to choose based on convenience and location:**

- Think about how often you will visit. If you use the vehicle every weekend, a facility 10 minutes from home is worth a higher rate. For a car you will not touch for a year, driving farther to save money makes sense.

- Store near where you use it. Boat owners often save time by renting near the lake or marina, and RV owners near a highway on-ramp.

- Check the route. Make sure the roads into the facility can handle a large rig without tight turns or low clearances.

- Compare three or four facilities. Prices for the same space can vary widely within a few miles, and online booking often unlocks a lower rate.

## Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to store a car? Storing a car usually costs $50 to $150 a month for outdoor parking, $75 to $200 for a covered spot, and $150 to $350 for an enclosed 10x20 unit. Climate controlled space costs more.

What size storage unit fits a car? A 10x20 unit fits most cars and small SUVs with a little room to spare. Full-size pickups and large SUVs are more comfortable in a 10x25 or 10x30.

Can you keep a car in a storage unit without insurance? Most facilities require the vehicle to be registered and insured. Many insurers offer reduced storage or comprehensive-only coverage while the car is parked.

Is covered RV storage worth it? For most owners, yes. Covered storage protects the roof, seals, and tires from sun and weather for less than the cost of an enclosed space.

Can you work on your vehicle at a storage facility? Most facilities do not allow repairs, oil changes, or fluid work on site. Light tasks like charging a battery or checking tire pressure are usually fine, but check your lease.

How long can a car sit in storage? A properly prepared car can sit for a year or more. Use fuel stabilizer, a battery tender, and correct tire pressure, and check on it every few weeks if you can.

Do I need to winterize my RV or boat if I live somewhere warm? If temperatures never drop below freezing, you can skip antifreeze, but you should still drain tanks, treat the fuel, and protect against sun and humidity.

Can I store a vehicle that is not running? Many facilities accept non-running vehicles as long as they are registered, insured, and can be moved in and out. Ask before you book, since some require vehicles to be operable.
