**Introduction**

Indoor boat storage typically costs $150 to $500 a month for an enclosed unit, and SelfStorage.help's boat storage listings show 46 facilities with drive-up access in Los Angeles alone, with rates starting from $53 a month. Indoor storage gives your boat the most protection from sun, rain, snow, and theft, while covered and outdoor spaces cost less. This guide compares every option side by side, shows which boat storage units fit your boat, and walks you through choosing a facility that is easy to reach from home and the water.

## What is indoor boat storage?

Indoor boat storage is any fully enclosed space where your boat sits out of the weather. You will usually find it in three forms:

- **Drive-up self storage units:** a large unit with a roll-up door that you back your boat and trailer into. This is the most common choice for trailered boats.

- **Indoor warehouse storage:** a shared building, often at a marina or dedicated boat facility, where boats are parked side by side.

- **Dry stack storage:** a marina building where staff lift your boat onto a rack with a forklift and launch it for you when you call ahead.

All three keep UV rays, rain, hail, and snow off your hull, deck, and upholstery. If you also store a car or RV, our [complete guide to vehicle storage](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/vehicle-storage-in-the-us-how-to-store-a-car-rv-or-boat>) covers every vehicle type in one place.

## How much does indoor boat storage cost?

Indoor boat storage usually costs $150 to $500 a month for an enclosed unit, and $200 to $600 a month if you add climate control. Dry stack storage at a marina is priced by the foot, at roughly $15 to $35 per foot each month.

Quick take:

- A 20 foot boat in dry stack storage at $17 per foot works out to about $340 a month.

- Shrink wrapping for outdoor winter storage commonly adds about $14 to $30 per foot, a cost you can often skip indoors.

- Annual contracts at many marinas cost less per foot than month-to-month rates.

## Indoor vs covered vs outdoor boat storage: which is right for you?

Indoor storage gives you the most protection, covered storage is the middle ground, and outdoor storage is the cheapest. Here is how they compare.

| **Feature** | **Outdoor** | **Covered** | **Indoor** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sun and UV protection | None | Good from above | Full |
| Rain, hail, and snow | None | Good from above | Full |
| Wind, dust, and pests | None | Limited | Full |
| Theft protection | Fenced lot | Fenced lot | Locked unit or building |
| Best for | Short stays, mild climates | Sunny or snowy regions on a budget | High-value boats, long winters, long storage |

The same trade-offs apply to RVs, so our [covered RV storage vs indoor vs outdoor guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/covered-rv-storage-vs-indoor-vs-outdoor-costs-and-how-to-choose>) is a useful companion if you store both.

## What size self storage units fit a boat?

The right size depends on your boat's full length on the trailer. SelfStorage.help's boat storage size guide matches common boats to these units:

| **Boat type** | **SelfStorage.help recommended unit size** | **Square feet** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jet ski or kayak | 5x10 to 10x10 | 50 to 100 |
| Fishing boat or skiff | 10x20 | 200 |
| Bowrider or pontoon | 10x25 to 10x30 | 250 to 300 |
| Cabin cruiser or sailboat | 30 feet and up | Dedicated boat space |

Before you book, measure:

- The full length from the trailer tongue to the outboard motor or swim platform.

- The height from the ground to the top of the windshield, T-top, or bimini frame.

- The width at the widest point, including rub rails and trailer fenders.

Add 2 to 3 feet of length and at least a foot of height clearance so you can open the door and walk around the boat. ## Is indoor boat storage worth it?

Indoor boat storage is worth it when your boat is new or high value, when you store it through a long winter, or when you live somewhere with intense sun. It protects gelcoat, vinyl seats, canvas, and electronics from the damage that costs the most to repair.

Indoor storage usually pays off if:

- You store for six months or more each year.

- You want to skip the cost of shrink wrapping every fall.

- You want faster spring launches with less cleaning and less mildew.

For boats with wood trim, upholstery, or sensitive electronics, ask about climate controlled units. Our guide to [climate controlled storage](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/climate-controlled-storage-in-the-us-everything-you-need-to-know>) explains how it works, and our [heated storage units guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/heated-storage-units-what-they-are-costs-and-when-you-need-one>) covers cold-weather options.

### Indoor boat storage at self storage facilities vs marinas

A self storage facility is usually the better value if you trailer your boat and launch it yourself. A marina with dry stack storage suits you if you want staff to launch the boat for you and keep it close to the water. Many owners also choose drive-up storage units for jet skis, kayaks, and small fishing boats, since they fit standard sizes.

## How to choose an indoor boat storage facility

The best facility is one that protects your boat and fits your routine. A low rate helps less if the drive to the ramp is long or the gate hours do not match your trips.

Check these before you sign:

- **Location:** how far it is from home and from the launch ramp you use most.

- **Access hours:** early and late gate access for fishing trips and weekend outings.

- **Drive-up access:** wide aisles and easy turning room for backing in a trailer.

- **Door height:** enough clearance for your windshield, T-top, or tower.

- **Security:** gated entry, cameras, lighting, and on-site staff.

- **Rules:** policies on fuel, batteries, and working on your boat on site.

- **Insurance:** most facilities ask for proof of coverage.

SelfStorage.help's boat storage directory lists several national brands, including [Public Storage](<https://www.publicstorage.com/>) with 240 facilities across 52 cities, [Extra Space Storage](<https://www.extraspace.com/>) with 99 facilities across 39 cities, and [StorageMart](<https://www.storage-mart.com/>) with 39 facilities across 11 cities, plus 107 local and independent storage brands. Comparing two or three nearby options is the easiest way to find the right fit.

## How to prepare your boat for indoor storage

A little preparation keeps your boat ready for the next season.

- Wash and dry the hull, deck, and interior to prevent mildew.

- Fill the fuel tank and add a fuel stabilizer.

- Change the engine oil and flush the cooling system.

- Winterize the engine and drain water lines if temperatures drop below freezing.

- Disconnect the battery, or use a trickle charger if the facility allows it.

- Remove electronics, cushions, and valuables, or store them dry.

- Check trailer tires and bearings, and chock the wheels.

Many of these steps match the ones you would use for a car, so our [long-term car storage guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/long-term-car-storage-how-to-store-your-car-safely>) is a handy checklist if you store more than one vehicle.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does indoor boat storage cost per month?** An indoor enclosed unit typically costs $150 to $500 a month, or $200 to $600 with climate control. Dry stack storage at a marina usually runs $15 to $35 per foot each month.

**Is indoor boat storage worth the extra cost?** For most owners who store through winter or in strong sun, yes. It protects your boat from UV, moisture, and pests, and you can often skip shrink wrapping.

**Can I keep a boat in self storage?** Yes. Many self storage facilities offer drive-up units that fit a boat on its trailer, and SelfStorage.help's boat storage size guide recommends a 10x20 for most fishing boats and a 10x25 to 10x30 for bowriders and pontoons.

**What size storage unit do I need for a boat?** Measure the full trailer length, then add 2 to 3 feet. Jet skis and kayaks fit a 5x10 to 10x10, while boats over 30 feet usually need a dedicated boat space.

**What is dry stack boat storage?** Dry stack storage keeps your boat on an indoor rack at a marina. Staff use a forklift to launch and retrieve it when you call ahead.

**Is covered boat storage cheaper than indoor storage?** Yes. Covered spaces usually cost $75 to $250 a month, compared with $150 to $500 for an indoor unit.

**Do I need insurance to store my boat?** Most facilities require proof of insurance. Ask your insurer about a lay-up or storage option, which can lower your premium while the boat is parked.
