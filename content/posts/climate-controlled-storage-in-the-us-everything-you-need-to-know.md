**Introduction**

[Climate controlled storage](/climate-controlled-storage) is a storage unit that stays at a steady temperature, usually between about 55 and 80 degrees, with humidity held near 55% or lower, and it typically costs 20% to 50% more than a standard unit. In practice, that means a 10x10 climate controlled unit often runs about $140 to $180 a month, compared with roughly $90 to $130 for a standard one. This guide explains what climate control actually does, which belongings need it, what it costs, how to check that a facility delivers what it promises, and when you can safely skip it.

## What is climate controlled storage?

Climate controlled storage is an indoor storage unit where a heating, cooling and dehumidifying system keeps the air inside the building within a set range all year. Most facilities aim for roughly 55 to 80 degrees Fahrenheit, though some use a wider band of about 50 to 85 degrees.

Here is what you get with a true climate controlled unit:

- Steady temperature: the building is heated in winter and cooled in summer, so the air around your things stays mild.

- Humidity control: dehumidifiers or the HVAC system keep moisture down, which is what stops mold, mildew, rust and warping.

- Indoor access: units sit inside a closed building, which also keeps out dust, pests and rain.

- Better air circulation: air moves through hallways and vents, so damp pockets are less likely to form.

You will find climate controlled self storage units in almost every size, from small 5x5 lockers to large 10x30 spaces. If you want to see facilities near you, our climate controlled storage units page lists options and prices by city.

## Climate controlled vs temperature controlled vs heated storage

These three terms sound alike but they promise different levels of protection, so ask the facility which one you are renting.

| Type | What it controls | Typical range | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Climate controlled | Temperature and humidity | About 55 to 80 degrees, humidity near 55% or lower | Wood, leather, electronics, documents, photos, art |
| Temperature controlled | Temperature only | About 50 to 85 degrees | Items sensitive to heat or cold but not to damp |
| Heated | Prevents freezing only | Usually above 40 to 50 degrees | Cold winter regions where the main risk is freezing |
| Standard (non climate controlled) | Nothing | Follows outdoor weather | Metal, plastic, tools, outdoor gear |

The key difference is humidity. Temperature control alone keeps a unit from getting very hot or very cold, but moisture is what causes most long-term damage. If you are storing anything made of wood, paper, fabric or leather, look for a unit that controls both. ## What needs climate controlled storage?

Anything that can warp, crack, rust, fade or grow mold needs climate controlled storage, especially if you are storing it for more than a few months. Here are the items that benefit most:

- Wood furniture and antiques: wood swells in humidity and shrinks in dry heat, which leads to cracks, split joints and warped drawers.

- Leather goods: couches, jackets and bags can crack in heat and grow mildew in damp air.

- Mattresses and upholstered furniture: fabric and foam soak up moisture, which invites mold and odors.

- Electronics and appliances: heat and humidity damage circuit boards, screens and batteries.

- Documents, books and photos: paper yellows and curls, and photos can stick together in humid air.

- Artwork: canvas, paint and frames all react to swings in temperature and humidity.

- Musical instruments: pianos, guitars and violins go out of tune and can crack.

- Clothing and linens: fabrics can discolor and pick up a musty smell.

- Vinyl records, DVDs and collectibles: records warp in heat, and paper sleeves and cards are easily damaged.

- Wine: it needs a steady, cool temperature to age properly.

- Business inventory and medical supplies: cosmetics, candles, paper stock and some supplies degrade in heat.

## When can you skip climate control in self storage?

You can usually skip climate control when you are storing durable items for a short time in a mild climate. A standard self storage unit works well for:

- Metal tools, toolboxes and shelving

- Plastic storage bins and plastic patio furniture

- Garden equipment, lawn mowers and bikes

- Sports gear such as kayaks, golf clubs and camping equipment

- Car tires and most car parts

- Items you will only store for a few weeks during a move

Short stays are the other big exception. If you only need a unit for a month or two between homes, a standard unit is often fine, even for furniture, as long as the season is mild. ## How much does climate controlled storage cost?

Climate controlled storage typically costs 20% to 50% more than a standard storage unit of the same size. The premium covers the energy, equipment and indoor building space the facility needs to keep conditions steady.

| Unit size | Standard unit (per month) | Climate controlled (per month) | Roughly what it holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $35 to $55 | $50 to $80 | Boxes, seasonal items, small furniture |
| 5x10 | $60 to $80 | $80 to $120 | A studio or small one-bedroom |
| 10x10 | $90 to $130 | $140 to $180 | A one or two-bedroom home |
| 10x15 | $150 to $165 | $185 to $250 | A two or three-bedroom home |
| 10x20 | $195 to $220 | $240 to $320 | A three or four-bedroom home |

These are typical national ranges. Your actual price depends on your city, the floor the unit is on, the time of year and any move-in promotions. For a full breakdown of what drives storage prices, see [how much is a storage unit](/blog/how-much-is-a-storage-unit), and if you are leaning toward the most popular size, our [10x10 storage unit](/blog/10x10-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside) guide shows what fits inside.

A few ways to keep the cost down:

- Rent the right size. Use the [storage size guide](/storage-size-guide) so you are only paying to climate control the space you need.

- Ask about upper floors. Units reached by elevator are often cheaper than ground-floor units in the same building.

- Book in fall or winter. Demand drops from roughly October to February, and rates and promotions tend to improve.

- Look for move-in deals. Many facilities offer discounted or free first months, explained in our guide to [first month free](/blog/first-month-free-storage-how-the-deals-work) storage.

## Is climate controlled storage worth it?

Climate controlled storage is worth it when the cost of replacing or repairing your belongings is higher than the extra rent. For most people [storing furniture](/blog/how-to-store-furniture-in-a-storage-unit-without-damage), electronics or family keepsakes for longer than three months, the answer is yes.

Run this quick check:

- Add up the premium. An extra $40 to $60 a month over six months comes to roughly $240 to $360.

- Estimate the risk. Compare that with the cost of replacing a warped dresser, a mildewed sofa or a box of ruined photos.

- Think about irreplaceable items. Heirlooms, artwork and documents cannot be bought again at any price, which makes protection worth more.

- Factor in your climate and season. A humid summer or a freezing winter raises the risk for almost everything you store.

If the numbers are close, a common approach is to rent a smaller climate controlled unit for your sensitive items and keep sturdy items in a cheaper standard unit or at home.

## How climate affects your storage needs across the US

Your local weather plays a big part in whether you need climate control. The main risks are humidity, extreme heat and freezing cold, and different parts of the country face different combinations.

- Humid regions: cities such as New Orleans see long, damp summers where mold and mildew can form quickly in a closed unit. Climate control is close to essential here for anything made of fabric, paper or wood.

- Hot, dry regions: in desert cities such as Phoenix and Las Vegas, summer temperatures inside a standard metal unit can climb far above outdoor highs, which can melt candles, warp vinyl and damage electronics. Inland California cities such as [Fresno](/storage-search/california/fresno) and [Sacramento](/storage-search/california/sacramento) also see long, hot summers.

- Cold-winter regions: in places like Minneapolis, [Buffalo](/storage-search/new-york/buffalo) and [Salt Lake City](/storage-search/utah/salt-lake-city), freezing temperatures can crack electronics, split wood and burst anything holding liquid.

- Four-season regions: cities such as [St. Louis](/storage-search/missouri/st-louis) and [Kansas City](/storage-search/missouri/kansas-city) combine humid summers with cold winters, so belongings face both risks in a single year.

- Mild coastal regions: in a city like [San Diego](/storage-search/california/san-diego), temperatures are gentler, but sea air can still bring humidity, so climate control still helps for long-term storage of sensitive items.

## How prices differ by city and how to choose a facility

Climate controlled storage prices follow local real estate costs, so the same unit can cost far more in a dense coastal city than in a smaller inland market. A 10x10 climate controlled unit in [New York City](/storage-search/new-york/new-york-city) or [Los Angeles](/storage-search/california/los-angeles) can cost well over $200 a month, while the same size in many Midwest cities may sit closer to $100 to $150. Prices can also vary by 30% or more between facilities just a few miles apart in the same city.

When you choose a facility, think about convenience and location as much as price:

- Distance from home: a unit 10 minutes away is easier to visit than a cheaper one 40 minutes away, and the fuel and time add up if you go often.

- Access hours: check that gate and building hours fit your schedule, especially if you work late or plan to visit on weekends.

- Route and parking: look for easy loading areas, covered entrances and carts or dollies near the door.

- Elevator access: if your unit is on an upper floor, a large freight elevator saves time and effort.

- Neighborhood: outer suburbs often have lower prices than central areas, which can be worth the extra drive for long stays.

## How to check that a facility is truly climate controlled

The best way to check a facility is to visit, ask direct questions and look at the building itself. Not every unit marketed as climate controlled offers the same level of control.

Ask the facility manager:

- What temperature range do you keep? Look for a clear answer such as 55 to 80 degrees.

- Do you control humidity as well as temperature? Ask what humidity level they aim for.

- Is the system on all year, day and night? Some buildings only heat or cool during certain months.

- Is there a backup plan if the power or HVAC fails? Ask how quickly repairs are made.

- How do you handle pests? Regular pest control matters as much as climate.

Then check for yourself:

- The hallway should feel noticeably cooler than outdoors in summer and warmer in winter.

- Look for vents or air handlers near the units.

- Check for water stains on ceilings or floors, a musty smell or condensation on walls.

- Make sure the unit door seals well and the floor is dry and clean.

For extra peace of mind, put a small battery-powered temperature and humidity monitor inside your unit and check it each time you visit.

## How to pack and store items in a climate controlled unit

Even in a climate controlled unit, how you pack makes a real difference. Good packing protects your things from dust, pressure and any small changes in conditions.

- Clean and dry everything first. Moisture or food crumbs left on items can cause mold or attract pests.

- Use plastic bins for paper and fabric. Bins with lids keep dust and moisture out better than cardboard.

- Wrap furniture in breathable covers. Use cotton sheets or furniture pads instead of plastic wrap, which can trap moisture against wood and leather.

- Keep items off the floor. Use pallets or shelving so air can circulate underneath.

- Leave space around the walls. A few inches of gap lets air move and makes it easier to reach items.

- Store electronics in original boxes where possible. Remove batteries first to prevent leaks.

- Stand mattresses and artwork upright. Keep them covered and out of direct contact with the floor.

- Label boxes on several sides. You will find things faster and avoid moving heavy items around.

## Frequently asked questions

What is climate controlled storage? It is an indoor storage unit kept at a steady temperature, usually about 55 to 80 degrees, with humidity held near 55% or lower. It protects belongings from heat, cold, moisture and mold.

How much more does climate controlled storage cost? Climate controlled storage usually costs 20% to 50% more than a standard unit. A 10x10 climate controlled unit typically runs about $140 to $180 a month, depending on your city.

What items need climate controlled storage? Wood and leather furniture, mattresses, electronics, documents, photos, artwork, musical instruments, clothing and wine all benefit from climate control, especially for stays longer than three months.

Is climate controlled storage worth it? It is worth it when the items you store would cost more to replace than the extra rent, or when they cannot be replaced at all. For sturdy items stored for a short time, a standard unit is usually enough.

What is the difference between climate controlled and temperature controlled storage? Climate controlled storage manages both temperature and humidity. Temperature controlled storage only manages heat and cold, so it gives less protection against mold and mildew.

Do I need climate controlled storage for furniture? For wood, leather or upholstered furniture stored longer than a few months, yes. Climate control prevents warping, cracking and mildew.

Can I store items in a non climate controlled unit in winter? You can store durable items such as metal tools, plastic bins and garden equipment. Avoid storing electronics, liquids, wood furniture and photos in an unheated unit in freezing climates.

How do I know if a storage unit is really climate controlled? Ask the facility what temperature and humidity range it keeps and whether the system runs all year. Then visit, feel the hallway temperature and check for vents, stains or a musty smell.
