**Introduction**

The best way to store a mattress is clean, completely dry, wrapped in a fitted mattress bag, and lying flat in a climate controlled storage unit, where a twin, full or queen fits in a 5x10 unit (about $60 to $80 a month) and a king needs a 10x10 (about $115 to $130 a month). Get those basics right and your mattress comes out of storage feeling the same as the day it went in. This guide walks you through preparing and wrapping your mattress, how to position it, what each mattress type needs, which unit size to rent, what it costs, and the mistakes that cause sagging, mold and pests.

## What is the best way to store a mattress?

Store it flat, on a clean raised surface, inside a sealed mattress bag, in a space that stays cool and dry. Here is the short version:

- Clean and dry it before you wrap it.

- Bag it in a mattress bag sized to your mattress.

- Lay it flat on pallets or a tarp, with nothing on top.

- Control the climate so temperature and humidity stay steady.

- Air it out every few months on long stays.

If you are storing a whole bedroom set, our guide on [how to store furniture in a storage unit without damage](/blog/how-to-store-furniture-in-a-storage-unit-without-damage) covers bed frames, headboards and dressers.

## How do you prepare a mattress for storage?

Preparation takes about an hour plus drying time, and it protects your mattress more than any other step.

- Strip the bedding. Wash it and store it in a sealed bin.

- Vacuum both sides and the edges to lift dust and crumbs that attract pests.

- Spot clean stains with a mild upholstery cleaner, without soaking the fabric.

- Deodorize with baking soda left for a few hours, then vacuum it up.

- Let it dry completely for 24 hours in a well-ventilated room. Trapped moisture is the main cause of mold in storage.

- Wrap it. Slide it into a mattress bag and seal the open end with packing tape on the bag only, never on the fabric.

A basic plastic mattress bag is inexpensive. For example, the [U-Haul queen mattress bag](<https://www.uhaul.com/MovingSupplies/Covers-Bags/Mattress-Bag-(Queen)/?id=2782>) is a 2 mil polyethylene bag that fits a queen mattress or box spring up to 10 inches thick, with twin, full and king sizes also available. Buy one bag per piece, so a mattress and box spring need two.

## Should you store a mattress flat or on its side?

Flat is best for anything longer than a few weeks. Lying flat spreads the weight evenly across the coils and foam layers, so the comfort layers keep their shape.

The [Sleep Foundation's mattress storage advice](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/mattress-care/how-to-store-a-mattress) recommends storing a mattress flat and keeping side storage to no more than about a month, because foam and latex models can sag when left on edge. Check your warranty too, since some manufacturers specify how a mattress should be stored.

| Position | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat on pallets or a tarp | Any stay longer than a month | Needs more floor space |
| On its long edge | Moving day and short stays under a month | Sagging and shifting comfort layers |
| Standing on its end | Avoid | Layers slide down and the edge can crush |
| Rolled or folded | Only if the maker says it is safe | Permanent creases and broken support layers |

Never stack boxes, furniture or a second mattress on top of a stored mattress. Weight on top causes dents that may not recover.

## How to store memory foam, hybrid and innerspring mattresses

Each mattress type has its own weak spot, so adjust your plan to what you own.

| Mattress type | Main risk in storage | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Memory foam | Sagging and moisture absorption | Store flat only, keep humidity steady, never roll it once unboxed |
| Latex | Heavy, can tear or sag on edge | Store flat, lift with two people, use a thick bag |
| Innerspring | Coils can bend under weight | Store flat with nothing on top |
| Hybrid | Foam layers sag, coils bend | Treat it like memory foam and keep it flat |
| Adjustable or smart bed | Electronics and motors | Remove and pack remotes and cords, keep it climate controlled |
| Box spring | Frame and fabric damage | Bag it and store it flat or on its long edge |

## What size storage unit do you need for a mattress?

A 5x10 unit fits a twin, full or queen lying flat, while a king or California king needs a 10x10 to lie flat. Use our [storage size guide](/storage-size-guide) if you are adding other furniture.

| Mattress size | Typical dimensions | To store flat | On its edge, short term |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Twin | 38 x 75 in | 5x10 | 5x10 |
| Twin XL | 38 x 80 in | 5x10 | 5x10 |
| Full | 54 x 75 in | 5x10 | 5x10 |
| Queen | 60 x 80 in | 5x10 (snug) or 10x10 | 5x10 |
| King | 76 x 80 in | 10x10 | 5x10 |
| California king | 72 x 84 in | 10x10 | 5x10 |

A queen is exactly as wide as a 5x10 unit, so it fits flat but leaves no room to walk past it. ### Tips for placing a mattress in self storage units

- Lay it on pallets or a clean tarp so it never touches bare concrete.

- Keep it near the back or along a wall so you do not need to climb over it.

- Plan your layout before moving day with our guide on [how to pack and organize a storage unit](/blog/how-to-pack-and-organize-a-storage-unit-a-step-by-step-guide).

## Do you need climate controlled self storage for a mattress?

For storage longer than a season, or anywhere with hot summers or humid weather, yes. A climate controlled unit typically stays between about 55 and 80 degrees and holds humidity steady, which protects foam, fabric and glue layers from breaking down.

Mold is the biggest threat. The [EPA's mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-course-chapter-2) recommends keeping indoor relative humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent. A standard drive-up unit can climb well past that in humid months, and moisture trapped inside a mattress bag has nowhere to go.

Climate control usually adds about 17% to 30% to the monthly rent. It is worth it when:

- You are storing a memory foam, latex or hybrid mattress.

- You will store it for more than two or three months.

- Your area has humid summers, freezing winters or big temperature swings.

You can usually skip it for a short stay of a few weeks in a mild, dry area. ## How much does it cost to store a mattress?

Storing a mattress costs about $60 to $80 a month for a standard 5x10 unit and about $115 to $130 a month for a 10x10, plus roughly 17% to 30% more for climate control. Here is a typical monthly budget:

| Unit size | Standard rate | With climate control | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x10 | $60 to $80 | $70 to $105 | One twin, full or queen mattress plus boxes |
| 10x10 | $115 to $130 | $135 to $170 | A king mattress, or a full bedroom set |

One-time costs to add:

- Mattress bags: usually under $20 each.

- Administration fee: around $50 at many facilities.

- A disc lock and a protection plan, unless your renter or home policy covers stored items.

For a full breakdown of fees, see [how much a storage unit costs](/blog/how-much-is-a-storage-unit).

Storage prices follow local real estate, so the same unit can cost much more in a dense coastal city than in a smaller inland one. When you choose a facility for a mattress, look for:

- Location: a site close to home makes it easier to check on your mattress.

- Easy access: drive-up units, wide doors, carts and elevators make a bulky mattress easier to move.

- Clean, climate controlled units: ask how the facility handles pest control.

Large national brands such as [Public Storage](https://www.publicstorage.com/) and [Extra Space Storage](https://www.extraspace.com/) offer climate controlled units in many cities, so compare a few nearby options and their current online rates.

## Common mistakes that damage a stored mattress

- Wrapping it while damp. Even slight moisture can turn into mildew inside a sealed bag.

- Putting it straight on concrete. Raise it on pallets instead.

- Stacking items on top. Boxes and furniture leave dents in the comfort layers.

- Storing it on its side for months. Foam and latex layers can sag and shift.

- Moving it alone. A queen or king mattress is awkward and heavy. Our tips on [how to move heavy furniture without damage](/blog/how-to-move-heavy-furniture-without-damage) help you load it safely.

## Frequently asked questions

How do you store a mattress long term? Clean and dry it fully, seal it in a mattress bag, and lay it flat on pallets in a climate controlled storage unit. Check on it every few months and air it out.

Can you store a mattress on its side? Yes, for a short time. Keep side storage to about a month at most, since foam and latex layers can sag when left on edge.

What size storage unit do I need for a queen mattress? A 5x10 unit fits a queen lying flat, though it is a snug fit. If you are storing a bed frame and other furniture too, a 10x10 gives you room to move.

Should I put plastic on a mattress in storage? Yes. A fitted plastic mattress bag keeps out dust, dirt and pests. Make sure the mattress is completely dry before you seal it, and choose a climate controlled unit for long stays.

Can you store a mattress in a non-climate controlled storage unit? For a few weeks in a mild, dry area, usually yes. For longer stays or humid and hot regions, climate control protects against mold and foam breakdown.

How much does it cost to store a mattress? Most people pay about $60 to $80 a month for a 5x10 unit or about $115 to $130 a month for a 10x10, with climate control adding roughly 17% to 30%.

How long can a mattress stay in storage? A well-prepared mattress can stay in a climate controlled unit for many months or even years, as long as it is kept flat, sealed, dry and checked from time to time.
