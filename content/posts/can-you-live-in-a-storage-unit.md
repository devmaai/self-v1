No, you cannot live in a storage unit, and doing so is illegal in every US state. Storage facilities are zoned for storing property, not for people to sleep or live in, so every lease bans it and staff will act if they find someone staying overnight. Below is why the law treats it this way, the risks involved, and where to turn for housing help if you are in a tight spot.

## Is it legal to live in a storage unit?

- No. Storage units are commercial spaces zoned for storing goods, not homes for people to live in.

- State storage laws say so directly. California's Self-Service Storage Facility Act, for example, states that no occupant may use a facility for residential purposes.

- Every storage lease limits use to storing personal property and bans living or sleeping in the unit.

## Why living in a storage unit is not allowed

- Zoning and building codes: units have no certificate of occupancy and are not built to house people.

- Lease terms: operators can end your rental the moment they find someone living in a unit.

- Insurance: storage protection plans cover stored property only and never the people inside.

## The safety risks

Beyond the law, a storage unit is a dangerous place to sleep:

- No plumbing, running water, or sanitation

- Poor ventilation, which lets carbon monoxide build up from any heater or generator

- Fire risk from unauthorized heating or cooking devices

- Extreme heat and cold, with units running much hotter than the outside air in summer

- No windows, natural light, or emergency exits

## What happens if you get caught

- Immediate eviction and loss of access to your belongings

- Possible trespassing charges or zoning penalties, depending on the state

- Reports to authorities if unsafe conditions involve children

## Where to find housing help

If you are considering a storage unit because you have nowhere else to go, these resources can help:

- Call 2-1-1 or visit 211.org to connect with local shelters and rental assistance.

- Contact HUD for housing programs and Section 8 vouchers.

- Reach out to the Salvation Army or a local shelter for emergency housing.

- Veterans can contact VA housing programs for support.

- Visit HHS.gov for a directory of federal homelessness resources.

## Legal ways to use a storage unit during a move

If you simply need somewhere to keep your things during a transition, a storage unit is built for exactly that:

- Store your belongings while you stay with family or in short-term housing.

- Choose a smaller unit to keep costs low during a move or renovation.

- Pick a month-to-month unit so you are not locked into a long term.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can you sleep in a storage unit overnight?**

No. Facilities prohibit overnight stays and track access through gates, cameras, and staff.

**Do storage facilities have cameras?**

Most do, along with gate access logs and on-site staff, which is how they notice someone living in a unit.

**Can you live in a climate controlled storage unit?**

No. Climate control only changes the temperature inside the unit. The ban on living in any storage unit still applies.

**What states allow living in a storage unit?**

None. It is illegal across the US.

**Can you run a business from a storage unit?**

You can store business inventory and equipment, but you cannot use a unit as a staffed office or a place to live. Check your facility's rules for what is allowed.
