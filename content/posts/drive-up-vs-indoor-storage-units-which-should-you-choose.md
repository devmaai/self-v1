**Introduction**

A drive-up storage unit lets you park your car or truck right outside the door and load directly through a roll-up gate, while an indoor unit sits inside a building and is reached through a hallway, stairs, or an elevator. Drive-up units usually cost about the same as indoor units of the same size, sometimes a little more for the convenience, while indoor units carry a bigger premium only when climate control is bundled in. The right choice comes down to what you are storing, how often you need to get to it, and how much weather and temperature swings matter for your belongings. This guide compares both types on cost, security, climate protection, and everyday convenience so you can pick the one that fits your move.

## What is a drive-up storage unit?

A drive-up unit is a ground-floor storage space with its own exterior door, usually a roll-up garage-style door, that you can back a vehicle right up to. You load and unload directly from your car, truck, or trailer without carrying items through a building.

Drive-up units are common at facilities built in rows, similar to a strip of garages. They work well when you value speed and simplicity over weather protection.

Typical features:

- Individual exterior entrance for each unit

- Roll-up or swing door wide enough for furniture, appliances, and equipment

- Direct vehicle access, often with a parking spot right in front

- Usually not climate controlled, though some facilities offer drive-up units with climate control at a higher price

## What is an indoor storage unit?

An indoor unit sits inside a larger building and is reached through a shared hallway, and often a stairwell or elevator for upper floors. You park in a central lot or loading area and carry or cart your items inside.

Indoor units are the more common setup at multi-story urban facilities, where land is expensive and stacking units vertically makes better use of the building.

Typical features:

- Shared building entrance, often with keypad or key fob access

- Hallways and elevators connecting units on multiple floors

- Climate control included at most facilities, though not universally

- Extra layer of shelter from rain, snow, and direct sun

## Drive-up vs. indoor storage: cost comparison

Access type is one of the factors that moves your monthly rate, alongside size, location, and climate control. Here is how the two typically compare for the same unit size.

| Factor | Drive-up unit | Indoor unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base monthly rate | Similar to indoor, sometimes $10 to $30 more for ground-floor convenience | Similar to drive-up on the ground floor, often less on upper floors reached by elevator |
| Climate control | Available at some facilities, usually an add-on | Often included or bundled, typically adding 17% to 30% to the base rate |
| Deposit and fees | Standard admin fee and optional deposit apply the same as any unit | Same fee structure as drive-up |
| Best value for | Vehicles, furniture, and frequent loading trips | Long-term storage of sensitive items where climate control matters more than access speed |

## Drive-up vs. indoor storage: security comparison

Both types can be secure, but they protect your belongings in different ways.

- Drive-up units rely on a facility-wide perimeter, such as a fence, gate code, and cameras covering the driving lanes. Your unit's own door lock is your main defense, so a solid disc lock matters more here than at an indoor facility.

- Indoor units add a layer of security between the parking lot and your unit, since a visitor needs to pass through a locked building entrance before reaching the hallway. Many indoor facilities also use individual door alarms tied to a keypad code.

Neither type is inherently unsafe. Ask any facility about camera coverage, gate hours, and whether staff walk the property, since security practices vary more by facility than by access type.

## Drive-up vs. indoor storage: weather and climate protection

This is where the two types differ the most. A drive-up unit's exterior door is exposed to rain, wind, and direct sun every time you open it, and the unit itself can swing with the outside temperature unless it specifically includes climate control. An indoor unit sits inside a building envelope, which buffers it from weather even without dedicated climate control, and most indoor units add true temperature and humidity control on top of that.

If you are storing anything sensitive to heat, cold, or moisture, climate control matters more than whether the unit is drive-up or indoor. See What Is Climate Controlled Storage? for what it protects against and when it is worth the extra cost.

## Which should you choose based on what you are storing?

Match the unit type to your items rather than defaulting to whichever is cheaper that month.

**Choose drive-up for:**

- Vehicles, boats, and trailers you access often

- Furniture and appliances during a move, when you want to load a truck quickly

- Tools, patio furniture, and other items that tolerate temperature swings

- Business or contractor inventory you load and unload frequently

**Choose indoor for:**

- Wood furniture, electronics, and musical instruments sensitive to humidity

- Documents, photos, and artwork

- Long-term storage where you rarely visit the unit

- Items you are storing in a region with harsh summers or winters

## Pros and cons at a glance

|   | Drive-up | Indoor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pros | Fast loading, no elevators or hallways, easy for large or heavy items | Better weather protection, often bundled climate control, added security layer |
| Cons | More weather exposure, climate control usually costs extra if offered at all | Slower loading, hallway and elevator width can limit large items |

## How to decide: questions to ask before you book

- How often will I visit the unit? Frequent access favors drive-up, since you skip the hallway and elevator every trip.

- What am I storing? Wood, electronics, and paper goods favor climate-controlled indoor space. Vehicles and hardy items do fine drive-up.

- What is the local climate? Extreme heat, cold, or humidity make climate control worth the added cost regardless of access type. See How Much Is a Storage Unit? for regional price differences.

- Can I fit large items through the building? Confirm hallway width and elevator size before booking an indoor unit for furniture, appliances, or equipment.

- Do I need help staying organized? Whichever type you choose, a clear layout makes a real difference over time. See How to Organize a Storage Unit for a simple system.

- Is my coverage in place? Confirm insurance or a protection plan before move-in, whether you use a facility plan or your own policy. See Self Storage Insurance Coverage

## Frequently asked questions

What is a drive-up storage unit? It is a ground-floor unit with its own exterior door that you can back a vehicle up to, so you load and unload directly without going through a building.

Are drive-up storage units more expensive than indoor units? Not usually. Base rates are similar for the same size, though indoor units often cost more once climate control is bundled in, typically 17% to 30% above the base rate.

Are drive-up storage units climate controlled? Some are, but it is not standard. Ask the facility directly, since climate-controlled drive-up units usually cost more than a standard drive-up space.

What does drive-up access mean for a storage unit? It means the unit has its own exterior entrance at ground level, so you can pull a vehicle directly up to the door instead of carrying items through a shared building.

Is indoor storage safer than drive-up storage? Indoor facilities typically add a locked building entrance on top of the property's perimeter security, which is an extra layer drive-up units do not have. Both can be secure when the facility maintains good gates, cameras, and lighting.

Which is better for storing a car? Drive-up, in most cases, since you can pull the vehicle in and out without navigating hallways or elevators. Confirm the unit is wide and tall enough for your vehicle before booking.

Can I switch from drive-up to indoor storage later? Many facilities let you transfer to a different unit type if space is available, though you may need to sign a new agreement and move your belongings yourself.

Do indoor units always have climate control? No. Climate control is common at indoor facilities but not guaranteed, so confirm it directly rather than assuming it comes with every indoor unit.
