**Introduction**

A storage unit in Springfield, Missouri costs about $35 to $260 a month depending on size, and the popular 10x10 unit typically runs $75 to $135, with recent citywide averages landing near $90. That makes Springfield one of the more affordable storage markets in the country, a little cheaper than Kansas City and St. Louis and far below coastal cities. This guide breaks down current Springfield storage prices by size, shows which parts of town cost the most and least, explains when climate control and boat or RV storage are worth it, lists the fees that land on your bill, and shares practical ways to pay even less.

## Springfield storage unit prices by size

Here is what Springfield facilities typically charge each month, based on current street rates across the city and nearby towns like Nixa, Ozark, and Republic.

| Unit size | Typical monthly cost | With climate control | Roughly what it holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $35 to $70 | +$10 to $25 | A closet's worth of boxes and seasonal gear |
| 5x10 | $50 to $90 | +$15 to $30 | A studio or small one-bedroom apartment |
| 10x10 | $75 to $135 | +$20 to $40 | One to two bedrooms of furniture |
| 10x15 | $95 to $165 | +$25 to $50 | Two to three bedrooms plus appliances |
| 10x20 | $120 to $200 | +$30 to $60 | A three to four bedroom home, or a car |
| 10x30 | $150 to $260 | +$40 to $80 | A large home, a boat, or business stock |

Quick take:

- The 10x10 is the most rented size in Springfield, and recent averages for one sit close to $90 a month.

- Across all sizes, the typical Springfield unit averages in the $80s to low $100s a month, well under the national average.

- Online specials and discounted first months can bring a 10x10 under $60 at some facilities, so the posted rate is rarely the only price on offer.

## Why storage in Springfield costs less than most cities

Springfield storage prices sit at the lower end of the national range for a few local reasons.

- Affordable land. Springfield has room to grow, so many facilities are single-story drive-up sites that cost less to build and run than multi-story city buildings.

- Plenty of competition. Dozens of facilities operate across Springfield and Greene County, which keeps rates and move-in deals competitive.

- Larger homes with garages. Many households have garages and basements, so demand is steadier and less urgent than in dense coastal cities.

- A big student population. Missouri State University, Drury University, Evangel University, and Ozarks Technical Community College create spikes in demand around move-out, but plenty of supply the rest of the year.

- Outdoor gear and lake toys. Boats, campers, and trailers drive demand for parking spaces and large units, which facilities price to stay attractive.

## Cheapest and priciest parts of Springfield for storage

Where you book matters. The same 10x10 can cost $25 to $50 a month less depending on which part of Springfield you choose.

| Area | Price tendency | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Downtown, Commercial Street, and near Missouri State | Highest | Fewer facilities, more indoor and climate controlled units, and steady student demand |
| South Springfield near Battlefield Road and South Glenstone Avenue | Above average | Busy retail corridor close to newer subdivisions |
| Southeast Springfield and the Highway 65 corridor | Moderate | Newer facilities with a mix of drive-up and climate controlled units |
| North Springfield near Kearney Street and North Glenstone Avenue | Lowest | Older drive-up facilities, more land, and frequent online deals |
| West Springfield near the airport and Chestnut Expressway | Low to moderate | Larger lots that suit vehicle and boat parking |
| Nixa, Ozark, and Republic | Low to moderate | Suburban drive-up sites with room for RVs and trailers |

A few practical takeaways:

- If you only visit your unit a few times a year, a facility on the north side can save you $300 to $600 a year compared with a unit near downtown.

- If you need weekly access, a slightly pricier unit close to home can still be the better deal once you count gas and time.

- If you live south of the city, compare Nixa and Ozark facilities too, since they often undercut rates along Battlefield Road.

## Is climate control worth it in Springfield?

Climate control is worth it in Springfield for valuable or sensitive items and for any stay that runs through summer or winter. The upgrade usually adds $10 to $40 a month for common sizes.

It is worth paying for if:

- You are storing through July and August, when humid days in the upper 80s and 90s can push the inside of a closed metal unit well past 100 degrees.

- You are storing through winter, when cold snaps and ice storms can crack finishes and damage electronics.

- You are storing wood or leather furniture, electronics, artwork, documents, photos, vinyl records, or musical instruments.

- Your items will stay in storage for more than a few months.

You can usually skip it for tools, bikes, lawn equipment, patio furniture, and short stays in spring or fall. For a full breakdown, read what climate controlled storage covers.

## Extra fees to budget for in Springfield

The advertised monthly rate is rarely your full bill. Plan for some combination of these.

| Fee | Typical cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Administration fee | $15 to $30, one time | Charged at move-in at most facilities |
| Security deposit | $0 to one month's rent | Often waived, refundable where it applies |
| Insurance or protection plan | $9 to $25 a month | Your renters or homeowners policy may already cover stored items |
| Disc lock | $10 to $20, one time | Some facilities require their own lock |
| Late fee | Set by your rental agreement | Usually charged after a short grace period |
| Rent increases | Often 5% to 10% after 6 to 12 months | Ask how often rates change before you sign |

Missouri's self-service storage law sets the rules for liens, default notices, and lien sales, so read the late payment section of your rental agreement before you sign. If you plan to use your own insurance, check the off-premises limit, since many policies cover stored items at only a share of your total personal property coverage. ## When is storage cheapest in Springfield?

Storage in Springfield is usually cheapest from November through February, after the summer moving season has passed.

- May through August is the priciest stretch, when lease turnovers, home sales, and job relocations overlap.

- Mid-May is busy near campus, so students at Missouri State, Drury, and Evangel should reserve a unit a few weeks before finals.

- Late spring is busy for boat and RV spaces, as owners pull vehicles out of winter storage and others look for summer parking.

- Fall and winter bring the best promotions, including discounted first months and waived admin fees.

If your dates are flexible, booking a few weeks before the summer rush gets you more choice of sizes and a better starting rate. Many facilities run move-in specials, and our guide to first month free storage deals explains how to spot an offer that actually saves you money.

## How to get a cheaper storage unit in Springfield

- Right-size the unit. A well-packed 10x10 beats a half-empty 10x15. Check the 10x10 size guide and the 5x10 size guide before you book.

- Look north or out to the suburbs. Facilities in North Springfield, Republic, Nixa, and Ozark usually cost less than units near downtown or Battlefield Road.

- Pick drive-up when you can. Standard drive-up units are often the cheapest option in Springfield, and they make loading easier.

- Reserve online. Web-only rates and move-in specials usually beat the walk-in price.

- Ask about the rate after the promo. Confirm when and by how much your rent can rise.

- Skip the protection plan if you are covered. Use your renters or homeowners policy if it already covers stored belongings.

- Consider a locker for small loads. If you only have a few boxes, a storage locker can cost less than a full unit.
