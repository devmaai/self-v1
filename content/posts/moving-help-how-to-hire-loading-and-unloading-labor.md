**Introduction**

Hiring moving help to load or unload a truck usually costs $40 to $80 per mover per hour, so a two-person crew runs about $100 to $180 an hour and most small moves land between $200 and $500. Labor only movers do the heavy lifting while you handle the truck, the container or the storage unit yourself, which can cut your moving bill by half or more compared with a full service company. This guide covers what moving labor costs, when labor only help makes sense, how to hire a reliable crew, what to ask before you book, how to prepare for moving day and how to use loaders for a storage unit.

## What is labor only moving help?

Labor only moving help is a crew you hire just for the physical work: carrying, loading, stacking and unloading. You provide the vehicle or container, and the crew provides the muscle and know-how.

Labor only movers typically handle:

- Loading a rental truck, a [moving container](/blog/how-do-moving-containers-work-costs-companies-and-tips-2026) or a trailer

- Unloading at your new home and placing furniture in the right rooms

- Moving items into or out of a storage unit

- Rearranging heavy furniture inside one home

- Basic furniture disassembly and reassembly, such as bed frames and table legs

They usually do not drive your belongings, supply a truck or carry cargo insurance on the trip itself. That is the main difference from a full service mover, and it is why the hourly cost is so much lower.

## How much does moving labor cost?

Moving labor costs about $40 to $80 per mover per hour in most US cities, with a minimum booking of 2 to 4 hours. Here is what you can expect to pay in total for local loading or unloading.

| Home size | Typical crew | Typical hours | Typical labor cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Studio or small apartment | 2 movers | 2 to 3 | $200 to $500 |
| 1 to 2 bedrooms | 2 to 3 movers | 3 to 6 | $400 to $900 |
| 3 or more bedrooms | 3 to 4 movers | 5 to 8 | $800 to $1,500 or more |
| Storage unit only (5x10 to 10x10) | 2 movers | 2 to 3 | $200 to $450 |

If you need help at both ends, budget for two separate bookings: one crew to load and one to unload. Many households pay the minimum twice for a small move.

What pushes the price up:

- Stairs and long carries: walk-up apartments and long hallways add time.

- Heavy or awkward items: pianos, safes, gun cabinets and large appliances may carry a surcharge.

- Peak timing: weekends, month-end and summer dates often cost 10% to 30% more.

- Unpacked boxes: crews bill by the hour, so unfinished packing becomes paid time.

- Big-city rates: dense, high-cost metros charge toward the top of the range.

Remember to add a tip, a rental truck or container, and moving supplies to your budget. Our moving home guide walks through a full moving budget step by step.

## Labor only movers vs full service movers: which do you need?

Labor only movers suit you best when you are comfortable driving the truck yourself and want to save money. Full service movers suit you best when you want one company to handle everything from loading to driving to unloading.

|   | Labor only movers | Full service movers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical local cost | $200 to $900 for most moves | $800 to $2,500 for most moves |
| Truck or container | You rent and drive it | Included |
| Packing | Optional add-on, often limited | Usually available as a package |
| Insurance in transit | Usually your responsibility | Basic valuation included |
| Best for | DIY moves, containers, storage units | Larger homes, busy schedules, long distance |

Labor only help is a strong fit when:

- You are moving locally with a rental truck or a moving container.

- You only need help at one end, such as loading a container that a company will haul.

- You are moving belongings into or out of storage.

- You have mostly boxes and a few large pieces of furniture.

## How to hire loading and unloading help

The easiest way to hire moving help is to book a local labor crew through an online moving labor marketplace or directly with a local moving company that offers labor only service. Follow these steps to find a crew you can trust.

- Measure the job. List your large furniture, count your boxes and note stairs, elevators and parking distance at each address.

- Pick your date and window. Midweek and mid-month dates are usually cheaper and easier to book.

- Get two or three quotes. Compare the hourly rate, the minimum hours, the crew size and any travel or equipment fees.

- Check reviews and history. Look for recent reviews that mention careful handling, punctuality and clear communication.

- Confirm insurance and licensing. Ask whether the crew carries liability coverage and what happens if something is damaged.

- Book in writing. Get the rate, the start time, the crew size and the cancellation policy in a confirmation message or email.

- Reconfirm the day before. A quick text or call keeps everyone on schedule.

Try to book two to four weeks ahead for summer or month-end moves. In quieter months, a few days' notice is often enough.

## What to ask before you book moving helpers

Asking the right questions up front prevents surprise charges and moving day stress.

- What is your hourly rate per mover, and how many hours is the minimum?

- Does the clock start when you arrive or when you leave your last job?

- Do you charge a travel fee, fuel fee or equipment fee?

- Will you bring dollies, furniture pads, straps and tools?

- Can you disassemble and reassemble beds, tables and desks?

- Do you carry liability insurance, and how do you handle damage claims?

- Are there extra charges for stairs, long carries or very heavy items?

- What is your cancellation and rescheduling policy?

## How to prepare for your movers on moving day

The more ready you are when the crew arrives, the fewer hours you pay for. A well-prepared two-hour job can easily turn into four hours when packing is unfinished.

Before the crew arrives:

- Finish packing and tape every box closed.

- Label boxes by room and mark fragile items clearly.

- Empty dresser drawers if they are heavy or fragile.

- Clear hallways, doorways and a path to the truck.

- Reserve parking for the truck as close to the door as you can.

- Keep valuables, medication, documents and chargers with you.

When the crew is working:

- Walk the crew through the home and point out fragile or high-priority items.

- Tell them the order you want things loaded, with heavy furniture first.

- Stay nearby to answer questions, and keep water on hand.

- Check the empty rooms, closets and cabinets before the crew leaves.

How much should you tip moving labor? A common guideline is about $4 to $5 per mover per hour, or roughly $20 to $50 per mover depending on the length and difficulty of the job. Tipping is optional, so adjust for the care and effort you see.

## Using moving labor to load storage units and self storage

Moving help works very well for storage units because the crew can load, stack and organize the space so you use every square foot. That often means you can rent a smaller self storage unit and pay less each month.

### How loaders save you money on self storage units

Professional loaders stack boxes floor to ceiling, stand mattresses and sofas on end and fill gaps with smaller items. A careful crew can fit a full one-bedroom apartment into a 10x10, while a loosely packed load might need a larger unit. Our [10x10 storage unit](/blog/10x10-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside) guide and the [storage size guide](/storage-size-guide) help you pick the right size before moving day.

### Tips for loading a storage unit

- Put heavy, sturdy items at the back and along the walls.

- Leave a narrow aisle down the middle so you can reach things later.

- Keep items you will need soon near the door.

- Use pallets or plastic sheeting under items if the floor may get damp.

- Ask the facility about loading carts, dollies and drive-up access before you book the crew.

## Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to hire movers to load a truck? Most people pay $200 to $500 for two movers to load a truck for a studio or small apartment. Larger homes typically run $400 to $1,500 or more, depending on crew size and hours.

What are labor only movers? Labor only movers are crews you hire to load, unload or rearrange your belongings. You provide the truck, container or storage unit, and they provide the lifting.

How many hours do movers need to load a truck? Two movers usually need 2 to 3 hours for a studio or one-bedroom and 3 to 6 hours for a two-bedroom. Stairs, long carries and unfinished packing add time.

Is labor only moving cheaper than full service movers? Yes, in most cases. Labor only help for a local move often costs $200 to $900, while a full service local move commonly runs $800 to $2,500.

Do moving helpers bring equipment? Most crews bring dollies, furniture pads and basic tools, but you should confirm when you book. Some charge a small fee for extra equipment or supplies.

Can I hire movers just to unload? Yes. You can book a crew for loading only, unloading only or both. Each booking usually has its own 2 to 4 hour minimum.

How much should I tip moving labor? A common guideline is $4 to $5 per mover per hour, or about $20 to $50 per mover for a typical job. Tip more for stairs, heavy items or extra care.

Can I hire moving help to load a storage unit? Yes. Many labor crews will meet you at a storage facility, and a careful crew can stack your unit tightly so you may be able to rent a smaller, cheaper size.
