**Introduction**

Long-term car storage usually costs $50 to $200 a month for outdoor or covered parking and $150 to $380 a month for an enclosed 10x20 storage unit, and a little preparation keeps your car ready to drive when you return. If your car will sit for more than 30 days, you should fill the tank with stabilized fuel, change the oil, protect the battery, set the tire pressure, and keep your insurance active. This guide covers your storage options and costs, a step-by-step prep checklist, what to do with insurance and registration, and how to bring your car back on the road.

## What counts as long-term car storage?

Long-term car storage means parking a vehicle you will not drive for about 30 days or more. That is the point where fuel starts to break down, batteries drain, and tires can develop flat spots, so a few extra steps start to matter.

You might need long-term storage when you:

- **Store a car for winter** to keep a convertible, classic, or sports car out of snow and road salt.

- **Deploy, travel, or study abroad** for several months.

- **Move to a new city** and need a place for a second car while you settle in.

- **Own more cars than parking spaces** at home or in your building.

For a wider look at storing cars, RVs, and boats, see our [complete vehicle storage guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/vehicle-storage-in-the-us-how-to-store-a-car-rv-or-boat>).

## What are your long-term car storage options?

You can choose from outdoor parking, covered parking, an enclosed self storage unit, or a climate controlled indoor space. The longer you store and the harsher your local weather, the more protection is worth paying for.

| **Storage type** | **What you get** | **Typical monthly cost** | **Best for** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Outdoor parking | A marked space in a fenced, gated lot | $50 to $100 | Daily drivers stored for a few months in a mild climate |
| Covered parking | A roof overhead, open sides | $75 to $200 | Protection from sun, hail, and light snow |
| Enclosed storage unit (10x15 or 10x20) | A private drive-up unit with a roll-up door | $150 to $380 | Winter car storage, classic cars, and longer stays |
| Climate controlled indoor storage | An enclosed space with steady temperature and humidity | Adds $25 to $150 to the base rate | Classic, collector, and high-value cars |

Many facilities list these spaces as [car storage](<https://www.selfstorage.help/car-storage>), and large self storage brands such as [Public Storage](<https://www.publicstorage.com/>), [Extra Space Storage](<https://www.extraspace.com/>), and [CubeSmart](<https://www.cubesmart.com/>) offer vehicle parking or drive-up units at many locations. Some truck rental companies, including [U-Haul](<https://www.uhaul.com/>), also offer vehicle storage at their facilities.

## What size self storage unit do you need for a car?

Most cars fit in a 10x20 self storage unit, while a compact car can fit in a 10x15. A 10x20 also leaves room to open the doors and walk around the car, which makes checks and battery charging easier.

- **10x15:** compact cars and small coupes with little room to spare.

- **10x20:** most sedans, small SUVs, and pickup trucks with a standard bed.

- **10x25 or 10x30:** full-size trucks, large SUVs, or a car plus boxes and furniture.

## How do you prepare a car for long-term storage?

Prepare your car by cleaning it, servicing the fluids, protecting the battery and tires, and keeping pests out. Work through this checklist the week before you park.

- **Wash and wax the car.** Dirt, bird droppings, and road salt can damage the paint while the car sits. Vacuum the inside and remove all food and trash.

- **Change the oil** if you will store the car for more than 30 days, since used oil holds contaminants that can harm the engine.

- **Fill the tank and add fuel stabilizer.** A full tank limits moisture, and a stabilizer can keep gas fresh for up to about 12 months. Drive a few miles so the treated fuel reaches the engine.

- **Top off other fluids,** including coolant, brake fluid, and washer fluid.

- **Protect the battery.** Connect a battery tender if the space has an outlet you are allowed to use, or disconnect the negative cable.

- **Set the tire pressure** to the recommended level, or slightly above, to help prevent flat spots. For very long stays, some owners place the car on jack stands.

- **Skip the parking brake.** Brake pads can stick to the rotors over time, so use wheel chocks instead.

- **Block pests.** Place a cover over the exhaust tip and air intake, and set traps or deterrents around the car. Leave yourself a reminder to remove the covers before you start the engine.

- **Use a breathable car cover.** A breathable cover keeps dust off without trapping moisture against the paint.

Many of the same habits help with anything you keep in storage. Our guide on [how to pack and organize a storage unit](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/how-to-pack-and-organize-a-storage-unit-a-step-by-step-guide>) shows how to plan the space around your car if you are storing boxes too.

## How do you store a car for winter?

To store a car for winter, follow the full prep checklist, then choose an enclosed or covered space that keeps snow, ice, and road salt off the car. Winter car storage is most common in cold, snowy places such as [Buffalo](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/new-york/buffalo>), [Rochester](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/new-york/rochester>), and [Salt Lake City](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/utah/salt-lake-city>).

- **Wash off road salt first,** including the underbody, since salt speeds up rust.

- **Check the coolant mix** so it protects the engine at your lowest local temperatures.

- **Choose an enclosed unit** if you can, because it blocks snow, wind, and freezing rain.

- **Consider** [**climate controlled storage**](<https://www.selfstorage.help/climate-controlled-storage>) for classic cars, leather interiors, and stays of several months. Our [climate controlled storage guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/climate-controlled-storage-in-the-us-everything-you-need-to-know>) explains when it is worth the extra cost.

- **Book in the fall,** since spaces for winter car storage can fill up quickly in cold-weather cities.

## What should you do about insurance and registration?

Keep your car insured while it is in storage, and check your state's registration rules before you cancel anything. Dropping coverage can leave a gap in your insurance history and may break state rules.

- **Ask your insurer about storage coverage.** Many insurers let you reduce coverage to comprehensive only while the car is parked, which protects against theft, fire, and weather damage at a lower cost.

- **Keep the loan or lease in mind.** If you finance or lease the car, your lender may require full coverage.

- **Follow your state's rules.** In New York, you must [surrender your plates](<https://dmv.ny.gov/registration/surrender-return-or-turn-in-your-vehicle-plates-and-registration>) before you cancel liability insurance. In California, you can file for [Planned Nonoperation](<https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-registration/vehicle-registration-renewal/file-for-planned-non-operation/>) with the DMV when a car will not be driven or parked on public roads.

- **Check what the facility requires.** Most self storage facilities ask that the car be registered, insured, and in running condition.

Wondering how your home policy fits in? Our guide on [whether renters insurance covers storage units](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/does-renters-insurance-cover-storage-units>) explains what is usually covered for belongings, which works differently from auto coverage.

## How much do car storage units cost in different cities?

Car storage costs follow local rents, so an enclosed unit in a dense coastal city often costs much more than the same space in a smaller inland market. Urban 10x20 units commonly run $180 to $380 a month, while suburban and rural units can start near $80 to $200.

**How to choose a facility based on location and convenience:**

- **Store close to home** if you plan to visit, start the car, or charge the battery.

- **Check drive-up access** and the width of the driveways and door openings.

- **Ask about access hours** so you can pick up the car when you need it.

- **Look for security features** such as gated entry, cameras, and good lighting.

- **Compare three or four facilities,** since prices for the same size can vary by 30% or more within a few miles.

If you only need a space for a few weeks, our guide to [short-term storage](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/short-term-storage-options-costs-and-how-it-works>) covers flexible options.

## How do you take a car out of long-term storage?

Before you drive, inspect the car, reverse every storage step, and start the engine gently. A careful restart helps you catch small problems before they become expensive.

- **Remove the exhaust and intake covers** and any pest traps.

- **Look for signs of rodents,** such as nests or chewed wires, under the hood and in the cabin.

- **Reconnect or check the battery,** and charge it if it is low.

- **Set the tire pressure** back to the recommended level.

- **Check the fluids, wipers, and brakes,** and test the brakes at low speed before you head onto the road.

- **Let the engine idle for a few minutes** before your first drive.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does long-term car storage cost?** Outdoor and covered parking typically costs $50 to $200 a month, while an enclosed 10x20 storage unit usually runs $150 to $380 a month depending on your city.

**Can you store a car in a self storage unit?** Yes. Many facilities rent drive-up 10x15 and 10x20 units for cars. Most ask that the car be registered, insured, and in running condition.

**How long can a car sit without being driven?** A car can usually sit for two to four weeks without much trouble. Beyond about 30 days, you should use fuel stabilizer, protect the battery, and follow the rest of the prep checklist.

**Should I disconnect my battery for long-term storage?** A battery tender is the best choice if you can use an outlet. If not, disconnecting the negative cable helps stop the battery from draining.

**Do I need insurance on a stored car?** Keeping some coverage is usually wise, and many insurers let you switch to comprehensive only while the car is parked. Check your state's rules before you cancel liability coverage.

**What size storage unit do I need for a car?** A 10x20 unit fits most cars and small trucks. A 10x15 can work for a compact car.

**Is climate controlled storage worth it for a car?** It is worth considering for classic, collector, and high-value cars, or for long stays in very hot, cold, or humid areas.

**Should I fill the gas tank before storing a car?** Yes. A full tank with fuel stabilizer limits moisture and helps keep the fuel system in good shape.
