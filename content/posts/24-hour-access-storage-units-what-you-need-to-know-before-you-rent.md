**Introduction**

Most self storage facilities lock their gates on a set schedule, often 6 a.m. to 9 or 10 p.m., and only a minority of facilities offer true round-the-clock entry. When they do, 24-hour access storage units typically cost $10 to $40 more per month than a standard unit of the same size. This guide walks through what 24-hour access actually means, who it's worth paying for, how it differs from extended gate hours, and what to expect when you show up at 2 a.m. to grab your holiday decorations.

## Do storage units really offer 24-hour access?

Some do, but it is not the industry default. Most facilities run on a posted access window, commonly somewhere between 6 a.m. and 9 or 10 p.m., with the office closing several hours before the gate itself locks. A smaller share of facilities, usually larger operations in bigger metro areas, keep the gate open all day and night for tenants who need it.

When a listing says "24-hour access," it almost always means the gate and your unit, not the front office. Staff still keep normal business hours even if you can drive onto the property anytime.

- Standard access: a fixed daily window, often 6 a.m. to 9 or 10 p.m.

- Extended access: a longer window than standard, sometimes with an added fee or manager approval.

- True 24-hour access: the gate never locks you out, day or night, every day of the week.

Always confirm access hours in writing before you sign. A facility website or third-party listing can be outdated, and "24-hour access" is sometimes used loosely to mean "extended hours."

## Is 24-hour access storage more expensive?

Yes, in most markets. You are paying for the extra security infrastructure and staff oversight that around-the-clock entry requires, on top of the unit itself. The table below shows a typical pattern for the premium by size, layered on top of a standard monthly rate.

| Unit size | Typical standard rate | 24-hour access premium |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $35 to $55 | +$5 to $15 |
| 5x10 | $60 to $80 | +$10 to $20 |
| 10x10 | $115 to $130 | +$10 to $25 |
| 10x15 | $150 to $165 | +$15 to $35 |
| 10x20 or larger | $195 and up | +$20 to $40 |

A 10x10 unit with round-the-clock access, for example, often lands somewhere in the $125 to $155 range depending on the city. The premium tends to be smaller in dollar terms on tiny lockers and larger on bigger units, since bigger units already carry a higher base rate.

## Who actually needs round-the-clock access?

Not everyone does, and plenty of renters pay for it without ever using it outside normal hours. It tends to be worth the extra cost if you fit one of these situations:

- Shift workers whose schedules fall outside the typical 6 a.m. to 9 p.m. window.

- Small business owners who load and unload inventory on their own timeline, including early morning deliveries.

- Movers on a tight deadline, packing a truck late at night before a long drive.

- Seasonal storage users who want flexibility during a short term storage stint without being boxed into set hours.

- Anyone in a different time zone managing storage remotely for a move or a relative's belongings.

- Movers using a portable container, loading at odd hours before a moving container pickup.

If your visits are occasional and predictable, like a monthly trip to swap out seasonal gear, a standard access window is usually fine and saves you money every month.

## 24-hour access vs. extended gate hours: what's the difference?

Extended access sits between standard and full 24-hour entry. A facility might stretch its gate hours to, say, 5 a.m. to midnight instead of offering true all-night access, or allow after-hours entry only with manager sign-off or an added monthly fee.

The practical differences:

- Standard access has one fixed window that applies to every tenant.

- Extended access usually still has a cutoff, just a later one, and sometimes requires requesting the upgrade separately from the unit itself.

- True 24-hour access has no cutoff at all, and the gate code or app works at any hour, any day.

Ask specifically whether the listed hours are the facility's standard policy or an extended tier, since the two get advertised with similar language.

## What do you need to get in after hours?

Facilities that offer 24-hour access still control entry carefully, since an unstaffed gate at 3 a.m. is a security risk they have to manage. Expect one or more of these:

- A personal gate code tied to your account, often required even during the day.

- A keyfob or access card at facilities that have upgraded past keypad-only entry.

- A mobile app for newer or larger operators, sometimes combined with a code.

- Photo ID on file, checked against your lease when you sign up for after-hours privileges.

- A signed addendum or waiver acknowledging the facility's after-hours policies, which some operators require before activating 24-hour access.

Keep your code and any fob with you, not written on your lease paperwork in the unit. Losing access to your own code at 11 p.m. defeats the purpose of paying for 24-hour entry.

## Is it safe to visit a storage unit late at night?

It can be, but the risk depends heavily on the facility, not just the hour. Good 24-hour facilities are built for after-dark visits: well-lit rows, security cameras covering the gate and hallways, and individually alarmed units. Weaker ones are not, and a dim, empty lot at midnight is not where you want to discover that the cameras are decorative.

Before you rely on late-night access, check for:

- Bright, even lighting across the entire drive aisle, not just near the office.

- Visible cameras at the gate, in hallways, and near your specific unit.

- A gate that actually enforces entry, rather than one that's propped open or easily tailgated.

- A general sense of upkeep, since neglected landscaping and broken lights often track with lax security overall.

If you are ever uneasy about a visit, bring someone with you or push it to daylight hours instead. No storage premium is worth a genuine safety risk.

## Can you get 24-hour access with climate control or drive-up units?

Often, yes, though it depends on the facility's layout. Climate controlled buildings with 24-hour access typically use a keypad or fob at the main building entrance in addition to the perimeter gate, since the indoor hallways need their own access control. Drive-up units with round-the-clock access usually only need the perimeter gate code, since you pull straight up to a roll-up door without passing through an interior building.

If you specifically want both features, confirm the combination directly with the facility rather than assuming. Some locations offer 24-hour access on their drive-up rows but restrict their climate controlled building to standard hours for staffing reasons, or the reverse.

## How to find a facility with genuine 24-hour access

Search filters on listing sites are not always accurate, so verify before you commit:

- Call or message the facility directly and ask for their posted gate hours in writing.

- Ask what "24-hour access" includes, since some locations use the phrase for extended hours rather than a true all-night window.

- Check whether it's available at sign-up or added later, since a few operators treat it as an upgrade you request after moving in.

- Compare it against the standard rate for the same size, using a resource like our guide to storage unit pricing, so you know exactly what premium you're paying.

- Search by state on our state storage search pages to compare facilities and access policies side by side in your area.

If a listing promises 24-hour access but can't produce a written policy, treat that as a red flag rather than a bonus feature.

## Frequently asked questions

Are 24-hour access storage units available everywhere? No. They are more common at larger facilities and in bigger metro markets. In smaller towns, standard daytime access windows are still the norm.

What's the difference between 24-hour access and extended gate hours? Extended hours stretch the daily window but still have a cutoff. True 24-hour access has no cutoff at all, any day of the week.

Is 24-hour access storage more expensive? Usually, by roughly $10 to $40 a month depending on unit size and market, on top of the standard rate for that size.

Do I need a special lease for 24-hour access? Sometimes. Some facilities activate it automatically with the unit, while others require a signed addendum or a separate request after move-in.

Is it safe to visit a storage unit late at night? It depends on the facility's lighting, cameras, and gate enforcement more than the hour itself. Check those details before relying on after-hours visits.

What ID or code do I need for after-hours entry? Most facilities use a personal gate code, sometimes paired with a keyfob, access card, or mobile app, plus photo ID on file from move-in.

Can I get 24-hour access with a climate controlled unit? Often yes, though indoor climate controlled buildings may use a separate keypad or fob at the building entrance in addition to the perimeter gate.

Does 24-hour access mean the office is open too? No. The office keeps normal business hours even when the gate and your unit are accessible around the clock.
