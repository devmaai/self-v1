A storage unit in Salt Lake City costs about $35 to $420 a month depending on size and location, and most renters land in the $90 to $210 range for the most popular 10x10 unit. Prices swing more here than in most cities because of the gap between the Avenues and downtown on one end and West Valley City, West Jordan, and Magna on the other. This guide breaks down current Salt Lake City storage prices by size, what pushes them up or down, how the valley's dry winters and ski season change the math, and where you are likely to find the best rate.

## Salt Lake City storage unit prices by size

Here is what Salt Lake City facilities typically charge each month, based on current street rates across the valley.

|   |   |   |   |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unit size | Typical monthly cost | With climate control | Roughly what it holds |
| 5x5 | $35 to $80 | +$20 to $35 | A closet's worth of boxes and small items |
| 5x10 | $55 to $120 | +$25 to $45 | A studio or one-bedroom apartment |
| 10x10 | $90 to $210 | +$35 to $70 | One to two bedrooms of furniture |
| 10x15 | $130 to $260 | +$45 to $90 | Two to three bedrooms plus appliances |
| 10x20 | $175 to $320 | +$55 to $110 | A three to four bedroom home, or a car |
| 10x30 | $230 to $420 | +$70 to $140 | A large home, an RV, or business inventory |

The wide range on the 10x10 is not a typo. A unit in West Jordan or Magna can book for well under $100, while the same size downtown or in Sugar House often runs north of $200. Always compare a few zip codes before you commit.

## What drives storage prices in Salt Lake City

Several local factors explain why the same size unit can cost so differently depending on where you look.

- East side versus west side. Downtown, the Avenues, Sugar House, and Holladay carry higher real estate costs, and that shows up directly in storage rents. West Valley City, West Jordan, Magna, and Midvale tend to run noticeably cheaper.

- Elevation and dry climate. Salt Lake's low humidity is easier on stored belongings than a coastal city, but big temperature swings between summer heat and winter cold still make climate control worth considering for sensitive items.

- Ski season demand. Every fall, skiers and boarders need somewhere to store summer gear, bikes, and patio furniture, and every spring they need somewhere to put skis, boards, and snowblowers. That seasonal swap keeps facilities busy twice a year.

- University of Utah turnover. Students moving in and out around the academic calendar create short-term demand spikes near campus each August and May.

- Floor and access type. Ground-floor, drive-up units cost more than upper-floor units reached by elevator, the same as in most cities.

## Is climate control worth it in Salt Lake City?

Yes, for a lot of renters, even though the valley's air is drier than a coastal city. Climate control here typically adds $20 to $70 a month over a standard unit, and the swing from a 100-degree July to a sub-freezing January is what makes it worth considering.

It is worth paying for if you are storing:

- Wood and leather furniture, which can crack in extreme dry heat or cold

- Electronics, appliances, and musical instruments

- Documents, photos, and artwork

- Anything stored longer than a season through both summer heat and winter cold

You can usually skip the upgrade for tools, patio furniture, metal shelving, and camping or ski gear that is built to handle temperature swings. If you are still deciding, the full breakdown of what climate controlled storage covers walks through it by item type.

Storage prices in Salt Lake City follow real estate values closely, so where you book matters as much as what you book.

|   |   |   |

Driving fifteen to twenty minutes west or south from downtown can cut a meaningful amount off the same size unit, so it is worth widening your search before you book.

## How ski season and winter affect Salt Lake City storage prices

Salt Lake City's storage market runs on a different seasonal clock than most of the country.

- Winter is the cheapest time to rent, typically November through February, once the back-to-school rush and summer moving season have passed.

- Fall brings a gear swap spike, as skiers and boarders move summer equipment out of garages and into storage to make room for winter gear.

- Spring brings the reverse swap, when ski and snowboard equipment comes out of storage and summer gear, bikes, and patio furniture go back in.

- RVs and boats need a winter home. Many Salt Lake City renters use storage specifically to get vehicles off the driveway before the first heavy snow, which raises demand for larger drive-up units each fall.

If your storage needs are tied to the seasons, booking a few weeks ahead of the fall gear swap gets you more choice and a better rate than waiting until the first snowstorm.

## How to get a cheaper storage unit in Salt Lake City

- Right-size the unit. A 10x10 that fits snugly beats a 10x15 with empty space, every time.

- Look west or south. West Valley City, West Jordan, Magna, and Midvale consistently undercut downtown and the east-side suburbs.

- Book in winter. November through February avoids both the summer moving rush and the fall ski-gear swap.

- Reserve online. Web-only first-month promotions are common and usually beat the walk-in rate.

- Ask about the rate after the promo period. Many facilities raise rent after the first few months, so get the full picture before you sign.

- Use your existing insurance. If your renters or homeowners policy already covers stored property, skip the facility's protection plan.
