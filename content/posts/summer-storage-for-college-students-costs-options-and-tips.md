**Introduction**

Summer storage for college students usually costs $150 to $400 for a three month break, based on $40 to $80 a month for a 5x5 unit, $60 to $120 for a 5x10, or about $25 to $60 per person when roommates split a 10x10. This guide shows you how summer pricing works, how a self storage unit compares with a student pickup service, what size to rent, and when to book. You will also find a move-out timeline, a packing checklist, and simple ways to keep your summer bill low. For the bigger picture on storage during college, start with our [complete guide to student storage](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/student-storage-in-the-us-the-complete-guide-for-college-students>).

## How much does summer storage cost for college students?

Most students pay $150 to $400 in total for summer storage, depending on the unit size, the city, and whether they share. Because most facilities bill by the full month, a typical mid-May to late-August break usually means paying for four months.

| **Option** | **Typical monthly cost** | **Typical summer total (3 to 4 months)** | **Best for** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 self storage unit | $40 to $80 | $120 to $320 | One dorm room of boxes and a mini fridge |
| 5x10 self storage unit | $60 to $120 | $180 to $480 | One student with a desk, dresser, or twin mattress |
| 10x10 shared by 3 or 4 students | $25 to $60 per person | $75 to $240 per person | Roommates with furniture |
| Student pickup service | $14 to $40 per item | $150 to $500 for 8 to 15 items | A few boxes and no car |

Quick take:

- **Budget for move-in costs.** An admin fee of around $25 to $50, a disc lock, and a protection plan of about $8 to $40 a month are common. Our guide to [how much a storage unit costs](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/how-much-is-a-storage-unit>) explains each fee.

- **National rates have been fairly steady.** [Yardi Matrix's national self storage reports](<https://www.multihousingnews.com/self-storage-national-report-august-2026/>) showed street rates slightly lower year over year through much of 2026, though prices near campus still climb in May and June.

- **Location matters most.** A unit near a campus in [Los Angeles](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/california/los-angeles>) or [Manhattan](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/new-york/manhattan>) can cost twice as much as the same size in a college town such as [Provo](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/utah/provo>) or [Syracuse](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/new-york/syracuse>).

## Self storage units vs pickup services: which is better for summer?

A self storage unit is usually cheaper once you have furniture or friends to share with, while a pickup service is often easier if you have a handful of boxes and no car. Here is how the two compare for a summer break.

| **Factor** | **Self storage unit** | **Student pickup service** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How you pay | Flat monthly rent for the whole unit | Per item, per month |
| Moving effort | You pack, load, and drive | They pick up and deliver |
| Access over summer | Visit any time during access hours | Usually no access until delivery |
| Furniture | Fits beds, desks, and couches easily | Large items cost much more |
| Sharing with roommates | Easy and cheap | Each person pays per item |

**Choose a self storage unit if:**

- You are storing a mattress, desk, dresser, or couch.

- Two to four roommates can share one unit and one lease.

**Choose a pickup service if:**

- You have fewer than about eight boxes and a suitcase.

- You do not have a car and are flying home right after finals.

National brands such as [Public Storage](<https://www.publicstorage.com/>), [Extra Space Storage](<https://www.extraspace.com/>), and [CubeSmart](<https://www.cubesmart.com/>) operate facilities near many campuses and let you reserve online. [U-Haul](<https://www.uhaul.com/>) offers self storage alongside truck and trailer rentals, which helps if you need to move a full dorm room in one trip. If you prefer a container brought to you, [PODS](<https://www.pods.com/>) is one well-known portable storage option, and our guide to [how moving containers work](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/how-do-moving-containers-work-costs-companies-and-tips-2026>) explains the process.

## What size storage unit do you need for summer break?

Most students need a 5x5 for a dorm room of boxes or a 5x10 if they have furniture, while a group of roommates usually fits a shared apartment into a 10x10. Base your choice on your bulkiest items, since furniture fills space much faster than boxes.

- **5x5 (25 sq ft):** about 10 to 15 boxes, bedding, a mini fridge, and a lamp. See our [5x5 storage unit guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/5x5-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside>).

- **5x10 (50 sq ft):** a twin mattress, a desk and chair, a small dresser, and 15 to 20 boxes. See our [5x10 storage unit guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/5x10-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside>).

- **10x10 (100 sq ft):** the furniture and boxes from a two or three bedroom student apartment. See our [10x10 storage unit guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/10x10-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside>).

## When should you book summer storage?

Book three to four weeks before you move out, which means mid-April for most schools that finish in May. Small units near campus are the first to sell out once finals begin.

| **When** | **What to do** |
| --- | --- |
| 6 weeks before move-out | Agree on sharing, list your items, and pick a size |
| 4 weeks before | Compare three or four facilities and reserve online |
| 2 weeks before | Buy boxes and bins, and pack items you will not need |
| 1 week before | Book a truck or a friend with a car |
| Move-out week | Move in before finals end, when trucks are less busy |
| Late August | Give notice and empty the unit before the next billing date |

Timing tips:

- **Watch the billing date.** Moving out a few days before a new month starts can save you a full month of rent.

- **Ask about promotions.** Many facilities run move-in offers in spring. Our breakdown of [first month free storage deals](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/first-month-free-storage-how-the-deals-work>) shows what to check before you sign.

- **Short gap only?** If you need storage for just a few weeks between leases, our guide to [short-term storage](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/short-term-storage-options-costs-and-how-it-works>) covers flexible options.

## What should you put in storage and what should you take home?

Store bulky items you will only use on campus, and take home anything valuable, personal, or needed over the summer. This keeps your unit small and protects what matters most.

**Good to store:**

- Mattress toppers, bedding, and pillows in sealed bags

- Mini fridge, microwave, and fans

- Desk chair, shelves, lamps, and rugs

- Textbooks, school supplies, and dorm decor

- Winter coats, boots, and out-of-season clothes

- Bikes, scooters, and sports gear

**Take home with you:** laptops and tablets, passports and IDs, prescriptions, jewelry, anything sentimental, and the clothes you will wear over the summer.

**Never store:** food, drinks, aerosol cans, lighter fluid, cleaning chemicals, or anything flammable. Facilities list prohibited items in the lease, and food in particular attracts pests.

## How do you pack a dorm room for summer storage?

Pack in sturdy boxes or bins of the same size, keep heavy items in small boxes, and label everything on two sides. Good packing protects your things and helps you fit into a smaller, cheaper unit.

- **Use matching medium boxes or plastic bins** so they stack evenly.

- **Pack books in small boxes** so each one stays easy to lift.

- **Defrost and dry the mini fridge** a day before, then leave the door slightly open in storage.

- **Wash clothes and bedding first,** and use vacuum bags to save space. Our guide on [how to store clothes in a storage unit](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/how-to-store-clothes-in-a-storage-unit-a-long-term-guide>) has more tips.

- **Protect mattresses** with a fitted plastic cover and store them flat or upright on their side, as explained in [how to store a mattress](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/how-to-store-a-mattress-in-a-storage-unit-without-damage>).

- **Take apart furniture** and tape screws in a bag to each piece.

- **Load heavy items first** and keep the boxes you will need in August near the door.

For a full loading plan, follow our [step-by-step guide to packing and organizing a storage unit](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/how-to-pack-and-organize-a-storage-unit-a-step-by-step-guide>).

## Do you need climate controlled self storage for the summer?

You need climate control if you are storing electronics, books, wood furniture, or instruments through a hot or humid summer. Standard units can get very hot from June to August, which can warp, crack, or mildew sensitive items.

**Climate control is worth it for:**

- Electronics, speakers, and small appliances

- Textbooks, notebooks, photos, and artwork

- Wood or particleboard furniture

- Musical instruments

- Anything stored in hot or humid regions

You can usually skip it for clothes in sealed bins, bikes, and sports gear, or for short stays in mild coastal climates. Climate control often adds about 17% to 30% to the monthly rate. Learn how it works in our guide to [climate controlled storage](<https://www.selfstorage.help/climate-controlled-storage>).

## How can you save money on college summer storage?

The quickest way to save is to share a unit and rent only the space you need. A few other habits can cut your bill further.

- **Split a 10x10** with three roommates to bring your cost down to about $25 to $45 a month each.

- **Book online early,** since web rates and spring promotions are often lower than walk-in prices.

- **Look a few miles from campus,** where rates for the same size are often lower.

- **Ask about student discounts** and prepay discounts for several months.

- **Check your family's homeowners policy** before buying the facility protection plan, since many policies cover a student's belongings stored away from home.

- **Sell or donate** what you will not need next year. Many campuses run move-out donation drives.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does summer storage cost for college students?** Most students spend $150 to $400 for a full summer. Sharing a 10x10 with roommates can bring your share down to about $75 to $240 for the season.

**What size storage unit do I need for a dorm room?** A 5x5 usually holds one dorm room of boxes, bedding, and a mini fridge. Choose a 5x10 if you also have a mattress, desk, or dresser.

**When should I reserve summer storage?** Reserve three to four weeks before move-out, ideally by mid-April for a May move-out, since small units near campus fill fast.

**Is a pickup service cheaper than a storage unit?** A pickup service can cost about the same for fewer than eight items. Once you add furniture or share with friends, a self storage unit is usually cheaper.

**Can I store my mini fridge over the summer?** Yes. Clean, defrost, and dry it fully, then leave the door slightly open so mold and odors do not build up.

**Do I need climate control for summer storage?** Choose climate control for electronics, books, wood furniture, and instruments, especially in hot or humid areas. Clothes in sealed bins and sports gear usually do fine in a standard unit.

**Can my roommates and I share one storage unit?** Yes. One person usually signs the lease, so agree on payments, access, and move-out dates in writing before you move in.

**Do storage facilities offer student discounts for summer?** Some locations do, so ask each facility directly. Online rates and spring move-in offers often save you as much as a student discount.
