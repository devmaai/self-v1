**Introduction**

A 10x10 storage unit gives you 100 square feet of floor space, enough to hold the furniture and boxes from a one to two bedroom home, and it is the single most rented size in the country. It typically costs $115 to $130 a month for a standard unit, or $145 to $205 with climate control added. It sits right between the smaller 5x10, built for a studio, and the bigger 10x15, built for a bigger home or extra overflow. Below you will find the exact dimensions, what actually fits inside, current pricing, and how it compares to the sizes on either side so you book the right one the first time.

## How big is a 10x10 storage unit?

A 10x10 storage unit measures 10 feet wide by 10 feet deep, for 100 square feet of floor space. With a typical 8-foot ceiling, that works out to roughly 800 cubic feet of storage volume, and facilities with 9 to 10-foot ceilings offer closer to 900 to 1,000 cubic feet.

To put that in perspective, 100 square feet is about the size of a small bedroom, or roughly half a one-car garage. It is double the floor space of a 5x10 unit and two-thirds the size of a 10x15, which is the next size up at most facilities.

|   |   |
| --- | --- |
| Dimension | Measurement |
| Width | 10 feet |
| Depth | 10 feet |
| Floor space | 100 square feet |
| Volume (8-ft ceiling) | ~800 cubic feet |
| Volume (9 to 10-ft ceiling) | ~900 to 1,000 cubic feet |
| Comparable space | A small bedroom, or half a one-car garage |

## What fits in a 10x10 storage unit?

A well-packed 10x10 unit holds the entire contents of a one to two bedroom home if you stack to the ceiling and keep the layout tight. You can typically fit:

- A full bedroom set: queen or king bed, dresser, nightstands, and a mirror

- A living room set: a sofa, loveseat, coffee table, and a TV

- A small dining set that seats 2 to 4 people

- One major appliance, such as a washer, dryer, or refrigerator

- 30 to 40 medium moving boxes

- Bikes, seasonal gear, and bins along the walls

- A motorcycle instead of furniture, if you are storing a vehicle rather than a household

Most people use a 10x10 for one of three reasons: moving a one or two bedroom apartment, storing the overflow from a downsize, or keeping business inventory and equipment in one place. If you are trying to fit a full house or a vehicle alongside your furniture, you have likely outgrown this size. ## How much does a 10x10 storage unit cost?

A 10x10 storage unit typically costs $115 to $130 a month for a standard, non-climate-controlled unit, and $145 to $205 a month with climate control added. In expensive metro markets like Los Angeles or New York, the same unit can run $160 to $250 or more.

|   |   |   |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit size | Typical monthly cost | With climate control |
| 5x10 | $60 to $80 | $80 to $125 |
| 10x10 | $115 to $130 | $145 to $205 |
| 10x15 | $150 to $165 | $185 to $260 |
| 10x20 | $195 to $220 | $240 to $340 |

A few things move the price up or down:

- Climate control. Keeping the unit between about 55 and 80 degrees adds roughly 20% to 40% to the base rent on a mid-size unit, which is worth it for wood furniture, electronics, and anything sensitive to humidity.

- Location and season. Rates in large coastal metros run well above these figures, while smaller inland markets run below them. Prices also climb in late spring and summer, the busiest moving months.

- Access type. A 10x10 is available as either drive-up or indoor at most facilities, and drive-up access usually costs a little more since you can pull a vehicle right up to the door.

## Is a 10x10 storage unit big enough for a car?

A 10x10 unit can fit a compact car or a small sedan, but it is a tight squeeze and leaves little to no room for anything else. Most facilities and renters treat a 10x10 as a household size first and a vehicle size second.

- Compact car or small sedan: fits, with only a few inches of clearance on each side and no room for boxes.

- Midsize or full-size car: often too tight, especially if the door needs to swing open to load or unload items.

- Truck, SUV, or boat on a trailer: generally needs a 10x20 or a dedicated outdoor parking spot instead.

- Motorcycle or ATV: fits easily, with plenty of room left over for gear and boxes.

If vehicle storage is your main goal, a dedicated vehicle storage spot is usually cheaper than a household unit this size. ## Who actually needs a 10x10 storage unit?

A 10x10 fits a specific set of situations better than any other size, which is why it is the one most facilities rent out first:

- Renters moving a one or two bedroom apartment between leases or during a renovation.

- Downsizers who are keeping a meaningful amount of furniture but moving into a smaller home.

- College students and young professionals consolidating a full apartment's worth of belongings over a summer.

- Small business owners who need room for inventory, tools, or seasonal stock without paying for commercial space.

- Homeowners mid-renovation who need to clear a room or two of furniture for a few months.

If your situation is smaller, like a dorm room or a studio, a 5x10 will usually cost you less for the same peace of mind. If you are moving a full house or storing a vehicle alongside your furniture, size up instead of trying to make a 10x10 work.

## Tips for packing a 10x10 storage unit efficiently

Getting full value out of 100 square feet comes down to how you load it, not just what you bring.

- Use the full height. Stack boxes to the ceiling and store furniture like mattresses and tabletops vertically along the walls.

- Disassemble what you can. Bed frames, tables, and shelving units take up far less room broken down than left whole.

- Load heavy items first and low. Appliances and boxed items go on the bottom, lighter and more fragile items go on top.

- Keep a path to the back. Even in a small unit, leaving a narrow gap down one side saves you from unpacking everything to reach one box.

- Protect against moisture. Use pallets or a plastic sheet under anything touching the floor if the unit is not climate controlled.

- Make an inventory as you load. A quick list of what went in and where makes it easy to find things later. Our guide on how to create a self storage inventory walks through a simple system, and how to organize a storage unit covers layout tips specific to smaller spaces.

## Frequently asked questions

How big is a 10x10 storage unit? It measures 10 feet by 10 feet, for 100 square feet of floor space and roughly 800 to 1,000 cubic feet of volume depending on ceiling height.

What can fit in a 10x10 storage unit? A 10x10 typically holds the full contents of a one to two bedroom home, including a bedroom set, a living room set, one major appliance, and 30 to 40 boxes.

How much does a 10x10 storage unit cost? Expect $115 to $130 a month for a standard unit and $145 to $205 a month with climate control, depending on your city and the facility's access type.

Is a 10x10 storage unit the most common size? Yes. The 10x10 is the most rented storage unit size in the country because it fits a full apartment's worth of belongings without the extra cost of a larger unit.

Can I fit a car in a 10x10 storage unit? A small or compact car can fit, but it is tight and leaves no room for anything else. A midsize car or larger usually needs a 10x15 or 10x20 instead.

Is a 10x10 or a 10x15 storage unit better for a one-bedroom apartment? A 10x10 is usually enough for a one-bedroom unless you have a lot of large furniture or a second room's worth of overflow, in which case a 10x15 gives you breathing room.

Does a 10x10 storage unit have drive-up access? Many do, though some facilities only offer this size as an indoor unit reached by hallway or elevator. Confirm access type before booking if you plan to load directly from a vehicle.

Is climate control worth it for a 10x10 storage unit? It is worth the extra cost if you are storing wood furniture, electronics, documents, or anything else sensitive to heat and humidity, especially for more than a few months.
