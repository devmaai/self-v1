**Introduction**

A first month free storage deal waives your first full month of rent, which saves most renters about $60 to $200 depending on unit size and city. You usually still pay an admin fee, insurance or a protection plan, and sometimes a lock or deposit at move-in, and the free month often only applies if you rent online for a minimum of two or three months. This guide explains how these storage unit deals actually work, the fine print to check, the other promotions you will see, and how to tell whether a free month is truly the cheapest option for you.

## How does first month free storage work?

A first month free promotion means the facility charges $0 rent for your first month, then bills the regular monthly rate from month two onward.

Most offers follow the same basic pattern:

- You reserve online or by phone and the promo is attached to that specific unit.

- You pay move-in costs on day one, such as an admin fee, insurance, and taxes, even though rent is $0.

- Your second month bills at the standard rate, sometimes prorated depending on your move-in date.

- The deal applies to new customers only, and usually to select unit sizes the facility wants to fill.

Some facilities describe the same offer as "1st month free," "$0 first month," or "first month on us." The wording changes, but the structure is the same: one month of rent waived, everything else charged as normal.

## What fees do you still pay with a free first month?

Even with free rent, expect to pay something at move-in. These are the usual line items.

| Cost | Typical amount | Paid when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Admin or setup fee | $20 to $50, one time | At move-in |
| Insurance or protection plan | $11 to $47 a month | At move-in, then monthly |
| Disc lock | $10 to $20, one time | At move-in, if you need one |
| Security deposit | $0 to one month's rent | At move-in, where required |
| Sales tax on rent | Varies by state and city | Monthly once rent starts |
| Second month's rent | Full standard rate | Usually 30 days after move-in |

A quick example: on a 10x10 unit at $120 a month, a free first month saves you $120. If you still pay a $30 admin fee, a $15 lock, and $14 for a protection plan, your move-in total is about $59 instead of roughly $179. That is a real saving, as long as the rent you pay afterward is fair. You can often skip the facility protection plan if your own policy already covers stored items, so check whether renters insurance covers storage units before you sign.

## Common rules and fine print on storage promotions

The terms of a first month free storage deal matter more than the headline. Read these points before you book.

- Minimum stay: many offers require you to keep the unit for at least two or three months. Leave early and the facility may charge back the free month.

- Online only: the best promotions are often reserved for online bookings and may not be honored for walk-ins.

- Select units: the promo usually applies only to certain sizes or floors, so the exact unit you want may not qualify.

- New customers: existing tenants who move to a new unit rarely get the deal.

- Autopay: some facilities require autopay to keep the promotional terms.

- Rate changes: your monthly rent can change after move-in, which is the single biggest factor in what the deal is really worth.

Ask the manager to confirm each of these in writing or in your rental agreement, so there are no surprises on your second bill.

## Do storage units raise rent after a first month free deal?

Yes, rent often goes up after the promotional period. Many facilities apply a rate increase within the first 6 to 12 months, and increases of roughly 5% to 15% or more per year are common across the industry.

This is why a free month can end up costing more than it saves. Picture two offers on the same size unit:

| Offer | Month 1 | Months 2 to 6 | Months 7 to 12 | Year one total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First month free, $130 rate, 15% increase at month 7 | $0 | $130 | about $150 | about $1,550 |
| No promo, $115 rate, no increase in year one | $115 | $115 | $115 | $1,380 |

In this example, the lower standard rate wins by about $170 over a year. Before you book, ask:

- How soon after move-in can my rent increase?

- How much has rent typically gone up for existing tenants?

- How much notice will I get before an increase?

If you only need storage for two or three months, a free first month is usually a great deal. For storage of a year or more, the long-term rate matters more than the promo.

## Other storage unit deals you will see

First month free is the best known offer, but facilities rotate through several other storage specials.

- $1 first month: nearly the same as a free month, with a token charge to activate the rental.

- 50% off the first two or three months: often worth more than one free month if you are storing for a full season.

- Second month free: you pay the first month, then get month two free, which helps if you need a short buffer between moves.

- Discount for prepaying: paying for 6 or 12 months up front can lock in a lower rate and sometimes protects you from early increases.

- Free truck or moving supplies: some facilities include free use of a moving truck on move-in day, which can save $40 to $100 on a local move.

- Student and military discounts: many facilities offer 5% to 10% off with valid ID.

Run the numbers on each offer over the full time you plan to store, not just the first month. Our guide to the cheapest storage unit prices shows more ways to lower the monthly rate.

## Is a first month free deal worth it?

A first month free deal is worth it when the regular rate is competitive and you will store for a short or medium period. It is less valuable when the standard rate is high or you plan to stay for years.

**A free month is a good fit if:**

- You are storing between moves or during a renovation for two to four months.

- The facility's standard rate is close to or below other options nearby.

- You can meet the minimum stay without paying a penalty.

**Look closer if:**

- The standard rate is noticeably higher than similar units nearby.

- The facility cannot tell you when or how much rent will increase.

- You plan to store for more than a year.

## How to find first month free storage deals

The easiest way to find storage first month free offers is to compare a few facilities at once and book online. A few habits help you land the best price:

- Search by city: compare rates and current promotions on your local page, such as storage units in Los Angeles, New York City, or Salt Lake City.

- Rent in the off-season: promotions are most common from fall through winter, when fewer people are moving.

- Check new facilities: newly opened locations often run strong move-in specials to fill units.

- Right-size your unit: a deal on a unit that is too big still costs you every month. Our guide to storage unit sizes helps you pick the right fit.

- Call and ask: managers can sometimes match a nearby offer or tell you about unadvertised specials.

## Frequently asked questions

Is first month free storage really free? The rent for your first month is free, but you usually still pay an admin fee, insurance or a protection plan, and sometimes a lock or deposit. Expect to pay something at move-in.

Do I have to stay a minimum time to keep the free month? Often, yes. Many deals require a two or three month minimum, and leaving early can mean paying back the free month.

Can I get first month free storage near me? Most cities have at least a few facilities running move-in specials. Compare local rates online and filter for current promotions to find them quickly.

Will my storage rent go up after the promo? It might. Many facilities raise rent within the first 6 to 12 months, so ask how soon and how much before you sign.

Is $1 first month the same as first month free? Practically, yes. You pay $1 instead of $0 for the first month, and the same fees and fine print usually apply.

Which is better, first month free or 50% off for three months? If the standard rate is the same, 50% off for three months saves you one and a half months of rent, so it beats one free month as long as you stay at least three months.

When are storage deals most common? Fall and winter, roughly October through February, when demand drops and facilities compete harder for new renters.

Can existing tenants get first month free? Rarely. These promotions are designed for new customers, though you can ask about loyalty pricing or a prepay discount.
