**Introduction**

Short-term storage usually means renting a self storage unit month to month for anywhere from a few weeks to about six months, and most people pay $60 to $220 a month plus a one-time admin fee of around $25 to $50. Because almost every facility rents on a monthly basis with no long contract, you can move in this week and move out next month without a penalty. This guide covers how short-term rentals work, what they cost by size, which type of temporary storage fits your situation, and how to keep the total bill as low as possible.

## How does short-term storage work?

Short-term storage works on a month-to-month rental agreement: you pay one month at a time, you can leave whenever you want, and you usually only need to give notice before your next billing date.

Here is the typical process from start to finish:

- Reserve a unit online or by phone. Most facilities hold it for a few days at no charge.

- Sign the rental agreement and pay move-in costs, which usually include the first month's rent (sometimes prorated), an admin fee, and proof of insurance or a protection plan.

- Bring your own lock or buy a disc lock at the office.

- Use the unit as long as you need, paying each month on your billing date.

- Give move-out notice (often 7 to 10 days, sometimes none), empty and sweep the unit, and remove your lock.

Most facilities do not offer weekly rentals, so even a two-week stay is billed as one month. The good news is that a monthly agreement gives you the flexibility of temporary storage without the commitment of a lease.

## How much does short-term storage cost?

Short-term storage costs the same monthly rate as any other self storage rental, so a 10x10 unit runs about $115 to $130 a month nationally. What changes on a short stay is how much the one-time fees and any first-month promotion affect your total.

| Unit size | Typical monthly rent | 1-month total with fees | 3-month total with fees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $35 to $55 | $60 to $105 | $130 to $215 |
| 5x10 | $60 to $80 | $85 to $130 | $205 to $290 |
| 10x10 | $115 to $130 | $140 to $180 | $370 to $440 |
| 10x15 | $150 to $165 | $175 to $215 | $475 to $545 |
| 10x20 | $195 to $220 | $220 to $270 | $610 to $710 |

Totals assume a one-time admin fee of about $25 to $50 and do not include insurance, a lock, or local taxes. For a deeper breakdown of rates and fees, see our guide to how much a storage unit costs.

A few cost points matter more on a short rental:

- Admin fees are spread over fewer months, so they make up a bigger share of your total.

- Promotions like a free or $1 first month can cut a two-month stay nearly in half.

- Climate control adds roughly 17% to 30% to the monthly rate, which you can often skip for a short summer or winter stay in a mild area.

## Is there a minimum rental period for a storage unit?

Most self storage facilities have a one-month minimum, because rent is billed monthly. There is rarely a minimum beyond that unless you accept a promotion.

Watch for these exceptions:

- Promotional minimums: a first month free deal may require you to stay two or three months. Read how these offers work in our guide to first month free storage.

- Prepay discounts: paying for six or twelve months up front saves money, but refunds for unused months vary by facility.

- Vehicle and [RV](/rv-storage) spaces: some parking spots carry longer minimums, especially in high-demand areas.

Ask the manager to confirm the minimum stay and the move-out notice period before you sign.

## Short-term storage options compared

Self storage units are the most common choice, but they are not your only option for temporary storage. The right pick depends on how long you need it, how much you are storing, and whether you want someone else to do the moving.

| Option | Best for | Typical cost | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Self storage unit | Most moves, renovations, and seasonal items | $35 to $220+ a month | Admin fee and one-month minimum |
| Portable storage container | Storing at your home or moving the container to a new city | About $150 to $300 a month plus delivery fees | Delivery and pickup charges, driveway space and permits |
| Full-service or valet storage | People who want pickup, storage, and return handled for them | Varies by volume, often priced per bin or item | Retrieval fees and limited access |
| Mover's storage-in-transit | Gaps between a move-out and move-in date | Quoted by the mover, usually per day or per month | Your belongings stay on the mover's schedule |
| Friend or family garage | Very small loads for a few weeks | Free or low cost | No security, insurance, or climate control |

If you are weighing a container against a unit, our guide to how moving containers work covers container pricing in more detail.

## When does short-term storage make sense?

Short-term storage makes sense any time you need your belongings out of the way for a few weeks or months but plan to bring them back or move them on soon.

Common reasons people rent temporary storage:

- Between homes: your lease ends before your new place is ready, or your closing dates do not line up.

- Home renovations and staging: clearing rooms for flooring, painting, or listing photos.

- College breaks: students storing a dorm room over the summer instead of hauling it home.

- Military deployment or a work assignment: keeping your things safe while you are away for a set period.

- Seasonal items: holiday decorations, patio furniture, or sports gear between seasons.

- Downsizing or decluttering: giving yourself time to decide what to keep, sell, or donate.

If your plans stretch past six months to a year, look at long-term rates and prepay discounts, which often beat paying month to month.

## How to choose the right short-term storage unit

The right unit is the smallest one that fits your stacked belongings, at a facility with the access hours and features your timeline needs.

- Size it tightly. Paying for empty space hurts more on a short stay. A 5x10 unit fits most studio or one-bedroom moves, while a 10x10 unit holds one to two bedrooms.

- Check access hours. If you will be in and out often during a move, 24-hour access storage or extended gate hours can save you time.

- Choose drive-up if you are moving heavy items. Loading straight from a truck is faster when you only plan to stay a month or two.

- Decide on climate control by season. Skip it for a short stay in mild weather, but add it for electronics, wood furniture, or documents in extreme heat or humidity.

- Compare a few nearby facilities. Rates on the same size can vary widely across a single city. You can compare local options on our pages for cities like Los Angeles, Salt Lake City, New York City, and St. Louis.

## How to save money on short-term storage

The easiest ways to cut the cost of temporary storage are to time your move-in, use a promotion, and move out before your next billing date.

- Book online to catch web-only rates and first-month deals.

- Ask about prorating so you only pay for the days left in your first billing cycle.

- Rent in fall or winter, when demand is lower and facilities compete harder for new tenants.

- Use your own coverage. Many home or renter policies cover stored items, which can replace a $11 to $47 monthly protection plan.

- Move out on time. Mark your billing date on your calendar and give notice early so you do not pay for an extra month.

- Pack well to fit a smaller unit. Uniform boxes stack higher and help you rent one size down.

## Frequently asked questions

Can you rent a storage unit for just one month? Yes. Nearly all self storage facilities rent month to month, so you can rent for one month and move out with proper notice. You will usually pay a one-time admin fee on top of the first month's rent.

Can you rent a storage unit for a week? Most facilities do not offer weekly rates, so a one-week or two-week stay is billed as a full month. Some facilities prorate your first month, which can lower the cost if you move in late in the billing cycle.

What is the cheapest short-term storage option? A small self storage unit, such as a 5x5 at about $35 to $55 a month, is usually the cheapest option with real security. Free options like a friend's garage cost less but offer no protection or insurance.

Do I need insurance for short-term storage? Most facilities require some coverage no matter how long you stay. You can often use your existing home or renter policy instead of buying the facility's protection plan.

Is month-to-month storage more expensive than long-term storage? The monthly rate is usually the same, but long-term renters can save with prepay discounts. Short-term renters pay the admin fee over fewer months, so the average cost per month is a little higher.

How much notice do I need to give before moving out? Many facilities ask for 7 to 10 days of notice before your next billing date, and some need none. Check your rental agreement so you do not get charged for another month.

Is a portable container better than a storage unit for short-term storage? A container is handy if you want to load at home or ship your things to a new city. For local, short stays, a self storage unit is usually cheaper because you avoid delivery and pickup fees.

Can I get first month free on a short-term rental? Often, yes, but many deals require a minimum stay of two or three months. Confirm the minimum before you book so the promotion actually saves you money.
