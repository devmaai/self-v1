**Introduction**

Covered RV storage typically costs $125 to $200 a month for an average 25 to 35 foot RV, which sits between outdoor parking at about $75 to $150 and fully indoor storage at about $150 to $400. For most owners, a covered space is the best balance of price and protection, while indoor storage makes sense for high-value rigs or harsh climates. This guide compares all three options side by side, shows what you can expect to pay by RV class, and walks you through choosing the right space at the right facility.

## What is covered RV storage?

Covered RV storage is a parking space with a roof overhead, usually a steel canopy or carport-style structure, and open or partly open sides. Your RV stays outside, but the roof blocks direct sun, rain, hail, and snow from landing on it.

You will usually find two styles:

- **Canopy or carport:** a roof on posts with fully open sides. This is the most common and most affordable covered option.

- **Three-sided covered:** a roof plus a back wall and side walls, open at the front. This adds protection from wind-driven rain and sideways sun.

UV exposure is a leading cause of faded paint and cracked roof seals, which is why covered spaces are so popular. ## Covered vs indoor vs outdoor RV storage: how do they compare?

The short answer: outdoor storage is the cheapest, indoor storage gives the most protection, and covered RV storage sits in between on both price and protection.

| **Feature** | **Outdoor (uncovered)** | **Covered** | **Indoor (enclosed)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typical monthly cost* | $75 to $150 | $125 to $200 | $150 to $400 |
| Sun and UV protection | None | Good | Full |
| Rain, hail, and snow | None | Good from above | Full |
| Wind and dust | None | Limited | Full |
| Temperature control | None | None | Available at some facilities |
| Security | Fenced lot, gated entry | Fenced lot, gated entry | Locked unit inside a secured site |
| Availability for large rigs | High | Good | Limited for 40 foot and longer |
| Best for | Short stays, mild climates, tight budgets | Year-round storage in sunny or snowy regions | Luxury rigs, long storage, extreme climates |

*For an average 25 to 35 foot RV. Ranges based on [HomeGuide's RV storage cost data](<https://homeguide.com/costs/rv-storage-cost>). Your local rate depends on your city and the facility.

Quick take:

- If your RV sits outside for more than a season in strong sun, covered storage usually pays for itself in avoided roof and paint repairs.

- If your rig is new, high value, or stored through hard freezes, indoor storage is worth pricing out.

- If you use your RV often and live in a mild climate, outdoor parking keeps costs low.

## How much does covered RV storage cost by RV class?

Length is the biggest driver of RV storage cost, because many facilities price by the foot or by the size of the space. Outdoor storage often runs about $3 to $6 per linear foot each month, and indoor storage about $6 to $12 per linear foot. Covered storage usually lands between the two.

| **RV type** | **Typical length** | **Outdoor per month** | **Indoor per month** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pop-up camper | 10 to 16 ft | $30 to $100 | $60 to $200 |
| Class B camper van | 16 to 25 ft | $50 to $150 | $100 to $300 |
| Class C motorhome | 20 to 35 ft | $60 to $210 | $120 to $420 |
| Travel trailer | 20 to 35 ft | $60 to $210 | $120 to $420 |
| Fifth wheel or toy hauler | 25 to 40 ft | $75 to $240 | $150 to $480 |
| Class A motorhome | 35 to 45 ft | $105 to $270 | $210 to $540 |

Expect covered rates for each class to fall roughly in the middle of the outdoor and indoor ranges above.

## When is indoor RV storage worth the extra cost?

Indoor RV storage is worth it when the value of your RV, or the damage your climate can do, outweighs the higher monthly rate. An enclosed space protects against sun, moisture, wind, dust, pests, and temperature swings all at once.

Indoor storage makes the most sense if:

- Your RV is new or high value and you want to protect its resale price.

- You store through long, freezing winters with heavy snow loads.

- You live somewhere with intense year-round sun, such as the deserts of Southern Utah around [St. George](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/utah/st-george>).

For extra protection from heat and humidity, ask whether the facility offers climate controlled spaces. ## Is outdoor RV storage good enough?

Outdoor RV storage is good enough when you use your RV often, store it for a short time, or live in a mild climate. It is also the easiest option to find for very long rigs.

To make outdoor storage work well:

- Use a breathable, well-fitted RV cover to block UV rays.

- Put tire covers on to prevent sun cracking.

- Inspect roof seals and caulking every few months.

- Choose a paved or gravel space with good drainage rather than grass or dirt.

## Can you keep an RV in self storage units?

Yes, some self storage facilities offer enclosed drive-up units large enough for smaller RVs, campers, and trailers. A 10x30 unit fits many travel trailers, pop-ups, and Class B vans, while larger Class A and Class C motorhomes usually need a dedicated RV space or a unit at least 10x40.

Measure your RV first, including the hitch, ladder, bike rack, mirrors, rooftop air conditioner, and antennas. Then add clearance:

- At least 10 to 15 feet of extra length so you can open doors and walk around it.

- 2 to 3 feet of extra clearance above your highest point for covered or indoor spaces.

## How do RV storage prices differ by city?

RV storage prices follow local land costs, so rates in big coastal metros are usually higher than in smaller inland cities and rural areas. Space for large vehicles is especially tight in dense cities, which pushes covered and indoor rates up.

- In large metros such as [Los Angeles](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/california/los-angeles>) or [San Diego](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/california/san-diego>), covered and indoor spaces fill quickly and often carry waitlists.

- In smaller inland cities such as [Salt Lake City](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/utah/salt-lake-city>) or [Kansas City](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/missouri/kansas-city>), you can often find more covered spaces at lower rates.

## How to choose an RV storage facility

The best RV storage facility is one that protects your RV and is easy for you to use. A lower rate does not help much if the location, gate hours, or lot layout make it hard to get your RV in and out.

Before you sign, check:

- **Location and route:** how far it is from home and whether the drive avoids low bridges, tight turns, and steep grades.

- **Access hours:** whether you can get in early for road trips or late when you return.

- **Lot layout:** wide aisles, an easy turning radius, and pull-through spaces for long rigs.

- **Security:** gated keypad entry, cameras, good lighting, and on-site staff.

- **Amenities:** electric hookups for battery charging, a dump station, air for tires, and a wash bay. These often add to the monthly rate, so ask what is included.

- **Rules:** policies on propane tanks, batteries, working on your RV on site, and insurance requirements.

- **Contract terms:** prepaying for 6 to 12 months can sometimes save 5% to 15%, so ask about long-term rates and any seasonal price changes.

Many national brands offer RV and vehicle parking, including [Public Storage](<https://www.publicstorage.com/>), [Extra Space Storage](<https://www.extraspace.com/>), and [SmartStop Self Storage](<https://smartstopselfstorage.com/>), alongside many local and independent RV storage specialists. Comparing two or three nearby options is the easiest way to find the right fit.

### How to prepare your RV for covered storage

A little preparation keeps your RV in good shape while it is parked.

- Clean the interior and remove all food to prevent pests.

- Drain the fresh, gray, and black water tanks.

- Winterize the plumbing if temperatures will drop below freezing.

- Disconnect or remove the battery, or use a trickle charger if the facility has power.

- Check tire pressure and use wheel chocks.

- Confirm your RV insurance covers it while in storage.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does covered RV storage cost per month?** Covered RV storage usually costs about $125 to $200 a month for a 25 to 35 foot RV. Longer rigs and spaces in large metros cost more.

**Is covered RV storage worth it?** For most owners who store outdoors for more than a season, yes. The roof blocks the UV, hail, and snow that cause the most common roof and paint damage.

**What is the difference between covered and indoor RV storage?** Covered storage has a roof but open or partly open sides, while indoor storage is a fully enclosed space. Indoor storage protects against wind, dust, pests, and temperature swings, and costs more.

**What is the cheapest way to store an RV?** Outdoor, uncovered parking is the cheapest option, often $75 to $150 a month. Facilities in smaller cities and rural areas usually offer the lowest rates.

**What size space do I need for my RV?** Measure your RV's full length, including the hitch and any rear accessories, then add 10 to 15 feet. Many travel trailers fit a 10x30 space, while large motorhomes need 40 feet or more.

**Can I store an RV in a regular storage unit?** Smaller RVs, campers, and trailers can fit in large drive-up units. Most motorhomes need a dedicated RV space because of their length and height.

**Do I need insurance to store my RV?** Most facilities require proof of insurance. Ask your insurer about a storage option, which can lower your premium while the RV is parked.
