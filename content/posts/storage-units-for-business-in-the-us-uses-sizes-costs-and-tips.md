**Introduction**

A [business storage](/business-storage) unit in the US usually costs $40 to $80 a month for a 5x5 or 5x10, $100 to $180 for a 10x10, and $190 to $350 for a 10x20 or 10x30, which makes it one of the cheapest ways to add space without signing a commercial lease. Small businesses, contractors, e-commerce sellers, and offices use units to hold inventory, tools, files, and seasonal stock on a flexible month-to-month basis. This guide covers what businesses store, which sizes fit common needs, what you can expect to pay, the features that matter for daily use, and how insurance and taxes work so you can rent with confidence.

## What is business storage?

Business storage is a self storage unit rented by a company or sole proprietor to hold work items such as inventory, equipment, tools, records, or marketing materials. You get the same kind of unit a household would rent, but you choose features that support how a business runs: longer access hours, drive-up loading, delivery acceptance, and sizes that match your stock.

Most facilities rent business units on a month-to-month agreement, so you can scale up before a busy season and scale back afterward. That flexibility is the main reason owners pick self storage over a warehouse or a bigger office.

**Business storage is a good fit when you:**

- Run a business from home and your garage or spare room is full

- Need a secure place for tools and materials close to your job sites

- Sell online and want stock in one organized place

- Have files and records you must keep but rarely open

- Want overflow space for a retail shop without moving to a bigger store

## What do businesses keep in storage units?

Businesses store almost anything they need on hand but do not want taking up expensive office or shop space. The most common uses fall into a few groups.

| Offices and professional services | Archived files, spare furniture, old computers | 5x5 to 10x10 |

| Restaurants and caterers | Extra chairs, tables, event equipment, dry goods packaging | 10x10 to 10x15 |
| Event and photo businesses | Lighting, staging, props, cases | 10x10 to 10x20 |

Inventory and e-commerce stock. Online sellers often outgrow a home office fast. A unit with shelving lets you sort stock by product, pack orders on site, and keep your home free of boxes. Our upcoming guide to inventory storage for e-commerce sellers goes deeper on setup and picking routines.

Tools and equipment. Contractors, landscapers, and cleaning companies use drive-up units as a small depot. You can load a truck or van at the door before the first job of the day. See our planned guide to storage units for contractors for layout ideas.

Documents and records. Many businesses must keep tax records, contracts, and client files for years. A small [climate controlled unit](/climate-controlled-storage) protects paper from heat and damp. Our upcoming guide to document storage for business covers boxing, labeling, and retention basics.

Seasonal and event items. Retailers rotate holiday stock, and event companies store staging between bookings, so they only pay for space when they need it.

**Sizing tips for business use:**

- Plan aisles. If you need to reach items often, leave a walkway down the middle. A unit you visit daily needs about 20% to 30% more space than one you fill and lock.

- Go vertical. Freestanding shelving roughly doubles the usable space in most units, especially for boxed stock.

- Measure pallets. A standard pallet is 48 by 40 inches. A 10x10 fits about four on the floor with a walkway, while a 10x20 fits about eight.

- Check ceiling height. Many units have 8 to 10 foot ceilings, which matters if you stack shelving or tall equipment.

## How much does a business storage unit cost?

Business storage costs the same as personal storage for the same unit, which is about $40 to $350 a month across common sizes, with the national average near $137 a month. Climate control, drive-up access, and a busy city location push the price higher.

| --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $35 to $55 | $50 to $85 |
| 5x10 | $60 to $80 | $80 to $125 |
| 10x10 | $115 to $130 | $145 to $205 |
| 10x15 | $150 to $165 | $185 to $260 |
| 10x20 | $195 to $220 | $240 to $340 |
| 10x30 | $260 to $320 | $320 to $470 |

These are typical national ranges. Our guide to [how much a storage unit costs](/blog/how-much-is-a-storage-unit) breaks down the full pricing picture.

**Extra costs to plan for:**

- Admin fee: around $20 to $50 at move-in.

- Protection plan or insurance: about $11 to $47 a month, or more for higher coverage limits.

- Lock: $10 to $20 if you do not bring your own.

- Shelving and carts: $100 to $400 for sturdy metal shelving and a hand truck.

- Rate increases: introductory rates often rise after a few months, so ask how often rent changes.

## Self storage vs warehouse vs office space: which is better for a business?

Self storage is usually the best choice for small businesses that need under 500 square feet, want month-to-month terms, and do not need a loading dock or staff on site. A warehouse makes more sense once you move pallets in volume, need a forklift, or run daily shipping with employees.

| Option | Typical commitment | Best for | Main trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Self storage unit | Month to month | Small stock, tools, files, seasonal items | Limited space, no forklift or dock at most sites |
| Flex or shared warehouse | Monthly or per pallet | Growing sellers who need fulfillment help | Higher cost per item, less direct access |
| Warehouse lease | Often 1 to 5 years | High volume, daily shipping, staff on site | Long lease, utilities, and setup costs |
| Bigger office or shop | Often 3 to 5 years | Teams that need storage next to the work | Paying office rates for storage space |

A quick rule of thumb: if your stock fits in a 10x30 or smaller and you visit a few times a week, self storage is simpler and cheaper. If you are filling more than two large units, it is worth pricing a small warehouse or a storage container rental for your own lot. ## What features matter most in a business storage facility?

The features that matter most for business use are access hours, drive-up loading, delivery acceptance, security, and climate control for sensitive stock. These decide how much time you save every week.

Access hours. Standard gate hours often run about 6 a.m. to 9 or 10 p.m. If you start early or finish late, look at [24-hour access](/blog/24-hour-access-storage-units-what-you-need-to-know-before-you-rent) storage units so the unit fits your schedule.

Drive-up access. Ground-level units with roll-up doors let you back a van or truck right up to the unit. This is the top feature for contractors and anyone moving heavy items often.

Loading areas and carts. Indoor facilities usually have covered loading bays, carts, dollies, and freight elevators. Ask how many carts are available at busy times.

Delivery acceptance. Some facilities accept packages and freight for business tenants during office hours. Policies vary a lot from site to site, so ask:

- Will staff sign for packages and pallets for you?

- Which carriers do they accept, and is there a size or weight limit?

- Where are deliveries held, and for how long?

- Is there a fee for the service?

Security. Look for gated entry with personal codes, video cameras, good lighting, individual door alarms where offered, and staff on site during the day.

Climate control. Electronics, paper, cosmetics, candles, wood, fabrics, and many retail products are sensitive to heat and humidity. A climate controlled storage unit protects them, especially in hot or humid regions.

Business services. Some facilities offer extras such as Wi-Fi, mailboxes, conference rooms, printing, or shipping supplies for sale. These are handy if you work from the facility often.

## Do you need insurance for a business storage unit?

Yes, you should insure business property in storage, and most facilities require some form of coverage before you move in. Personal policies often limit or exclude business property, so check your coverage carefully.

- Renters or homeowners insurance may cover some personal items in storage, but it usually limits or excludes inventory and business equipment. Our guide on whether [renters insurance](/blog/does-renters-insurance-cover-storage-units) covers storage units explains the common limits.

- A business owner's policy or commercial property policy may extend to property kept off site. Ask your agent whether your storage address is covered and up to what amount.

- Facility protection plans usually cost about $11 to $47 a month and cover a set amount, often $2,000 to $10,000. Check whether they cover business property and what events they include.

- Keep an inventory list with photos and values, and update it when your stock changes. This makes any claim much easier.

## Is a storage unit tax deductible for a business?

A storage unit used only for business is generally treated as a deductible business expense, since rent for space used to run your business is a normal operating cost. Personal storage is not deductible, and a unit used for both needs a clear split.

To keep things simple at tax time:

- Rent the unit in your business name or pay with a business card or account.

- Keep every invoice and your rental agreement with your business records.

- Use the unit only for business where you can, so the expense is clear.

- Track related costs such as locks, shelving, insurance, and [moving labor](/blog/moving-help-how-to-hire-loading-and-unloading-labor).

Tax rules depend on your business structure and situation, so confirm the details with your accountant or tax professional before you file.

## How do storage prices differ by city, and how should you choose a facility?

Storage prices follow local real estate, so the same 10x10 business unit can cost twice as much in a dense coastal city as in a smaller inland market. Business owners in [New York City](/storage-search/new-york/new-york-city), [San Francisco](/storage-search/california/san-francisco), [Los Angeles](/storage-search/california/los-angeles), Boston, and Seattle usually pay well above the national average, and drive-up units in those cities can be harder to find. Owners in [Kansas City](/storage-search/missouri/kansas-city), [St. Louis](/storage-search/missouri/st-louis), [Salt Lake City](/storage-search/utah/salt-lake-city), [Sacramento](/storage-search/california/sacramento), Columbus, and Indianapolis usually find more drive-up units at lower rates.

**How to choose a facility based on convenience and location:**

- Map your daily route. A unit between your home, shop, and job sites saves more than a cheaper unit across town. Ten extra minutes each way adds up fast if you visit every day.

- Check road access. Contractors and anyone with a trailer should look for wide driveways, easy turning room, and units near the gate.

- Look just outside the center. Facilities a few miles from downtown often cost 20% to 30% less for the same size.

- Compare three or four facilities, since rates on the same unit can vary widely within a few miles.

- Visit at your busiest time of day to see how easy the gate, parking, and elevators are when it counts.

## How do you set up and run a business storage unit?

Set up your unit like a small stockroom, with shelving, clear labels, and a simple tracking system, so you can find anything in minutes. A little setup on day one saves hours each month.

- Install shelving along the walls and keep a center aisle clear.

- Put fast-moving items near the door and slow-moving items at the back.

- Label shelves and boxes with item names or SKUs that match your inventory list.

- Keep a packing station with tape, mailers, and a scale if you ship from the unit.

- Use bins with lids for small parts and anything that could attract dust.

- Keep heavy items low and never stack boxes higher than you can safely reach.

- Track what goes in and out with a spreadsheet or inventory app, and check it monthly.

- Review your size every few months. If the unit is half empty after peak season, moving to a smaller one can cut costs. If you need space only for a short busy period, see our guide to [short-term storage](/blog/short-term-storage-options-costs-and-how-it-works).

What you cannot store: almost every facility bans hazardous materials such as fuel, propane, chemicals, and fireworks, along with perishable food, live animals, and anything illegal. Most also do not allow you to run a business that serves customers out of the unit or to use it as a workshop with power tools. Read the rental agreement for the exact rules.

## Frequently asked questions

How much does a business storage unit cost per month? Most business storage units cost $40 to $350 a month depending on size, location, and features. A 10x10, the most popular size, usually runs $115 to $130 a month, or more with climate control.

Can I run my business out of a storage unit? Most facilities let you store and organize business items, and many let you pack orders on site. Very few allow customer visits, employees working full days, or workshop use, so check the rental agreement.

Can a storage facility accept deliveries for my business? Some facilities accept packages and freight for business tenants during office hours, while others do not. Ask about carriers, size limits, fees, and how long they hold deliveries.

What size storage unit do I need for inventory? A 5x10 suits a small online shop, a 10x10 fits about four pallets with a walkway, and a 10x20 fits about eight. Add space if you need aisles to pick orders.

Do I need climate control for business storage? You need climate control for electronics, paper records, cosmetics, wood, fabric, and other products that can be damaged by heat or humidity. Tools, metal shelving, and outdoor equipment usually do fine in a standard unit.

Is a business storage unit tax deductible? A unit used only for business is generally a deductible business expense. Keep your invoices and rental agreement, and confirm the details with your tax professional.

Is self storage cheaper than a warehouse? For small amounts of stock, self storage is usually cheaper and much more flexible because you rent month to month with no setup costs. A warehouse becomes better value when you need dock access, forklifts, or several thousand square feet.

Can I rent a storage unit in my business name? Yes, most facilities let you rent in a business name. You may need to show your business details and an ID for the person signing the agreement.
