**Introduction**

Large storage units usually mean 10x20, 10x25 and 10x30 spaces, which cost about $195 to $320 a month in most US cities before climate control. A 10x20 suits most three to four bedroom homes, a 10x25 adds room for a vehicle or overflow, and a 10x30 handles a large home plus a car or business stock. This guide compares the three sizes side by side, shows the price gap between each step, and gives you a quick way to choose without paying for space you will not use.

## What counts as a large storage unit?

Most facilities call any unit of 200 square feet or more a large storage unit. The three common large self storage units share a 10-foot width and differ only in depth, which makes them easy to compare.

| Size | Floor space | Depth compared to a 10x20 | Common access type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10x20 | 200 sq ft | Baseline | Drive-up or indoor |
| 10x25 | 250 sq ft | 5 feet deeper | Mostly drive-up |
| 10x30 | 300 sq ft | 10 feet deeper | Mostly drive-up |

A few things to keep in mind:

- Ceiling heights usually run 8 to 10 feet, so stacking adds a lot of usable volume.

- The 10x25 is the least common of the three, so not every facility lists one.

- Some facilities offer 10x40 or 12x30 units, but these are rare and often sold as vehicle or commercial space.

If you want a full picture of a single size, see our guides to the [10x20 storage unit](/blog/10x20-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside) and the [10x30 storage unit](/blog/10x30-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside). This post focuses on choosing between them.

## How much do large storage units cost?

A 10x20 averages about $195 to $220 a month, a 10x25 about $225 to $270, and a 10x30 about $260 to $320 in a typical US market. Climate control adds roughly 20% to 50% on top.

| Size | Typical monthly cost | With climate control | Cost per sq ft per month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10x20 | $195 to $220 | $240 to $340 | About $0.98 to $1.10 |
| 10x25 | $225 to $270 | $280 to $400 | About $0.90 to $1.08 |
| 10x30 | $260 to $320 | $320 to $470 | About $0.87 to $1.07 |

What the numbers tell you:

- Each 5-foot step up costs about $30 to $50 more per month.

- The price per square foot drops slightly as units get bigger, so a larger unit can be better value when you will truly fill it.

- Prices in dense coastal cities such as [Los Angeles](/storage-search/california/los-angeles), [New York City](/storage-search/new-york/new-york-city) and [San Francisco](/storage-search/california/san-francisco) can run 30% to 60% above these ranges, while smaller inland markets often come in lower.

For a wider view of rates and fees across every size, read [how much is a storage unit](/blog/how-much-is-a-storage-unit).

## 10x20 vs 10x25 vs 10x30: which size fits your load?

Choose a 10x20 for a three to four bedroom home, a 10x25 for that same home plus a vehicle or heavy overflow, and a 10x30 for a four to five bedroom home, two vehicles, or business inventory.

| If you are storing | Best fit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A three bedroom home with no vehicle | 10x20 | Room for furniture, appliances and boxes with a walkway |
| A four bedroom home packed tightly | 10x20 or 10x25 | A 10x25 gives you an aisle to reach items |
| A home's contents plus one car | 10x25 | The extra 5 feet keeps the car clear of furniture |
| A boat or small RV on a trailer | 10x25 or 10x30 | Measure the full length, including the hitch |
| A large home plus a car or truck | 10x30 | Enough depth for both without unloading the car |
| Business stock, shelving or equipment | 10x25 or 10x30 | Space for rows of shelving and a cart aisle |

A simple rule: if your list fits in a 10x20 only when every inch is packed, step up to a 10x25. You will save time and reduce damage when you need to reach something at the back.

## When is a 10x25 storage unit the right choice?

A 10x25 storage unit is the right choice when a 10x20 feels tight and a 10x30 feels like too much. It is the middle option that many renters overlook.

It works well for:

- A full home plus a vehicle: park a sedan at the front and keep furniture behind it.

- Long-term storage you will visit often: the extra depth leaves room for a center aisle.

- Mixed household and garage items: lawn equipment, tools and bikes sit along one wall without crowding furniture.

- Small business owners: contractors and online sellers get space for shelving without paying for a full 10x30.

Because fewer facilities build 10x25 units, they can be harder to find during busy summer months. Reserve early if you have a move date, and ask the facility to confirm the exact dimensions, since some list a 10x24 or 10x26 under the same label.

## Do you need a large unit for a car, boat, or RV?

You need at least a 10x20 for most cars, a 10x25 or 10x30 for trucks, larger SUVs and most boats on trailers, and outdoor or covered parking for most RVs.

| Vehicle | Typical length | Smallest enclosed unit that usually works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compact or midsize car | 14 to 16 ft | 10x20 |
| Full-size SUV or pickup | 17 to 20 ft | 10x25 |
| Boat on a trailer | 18 to 26 ft with trailer | 10x25 or 10x30 |
| Class B camper van | 19 to 22 ft | 10x25 or 10x30, if door height allows |
| Class A or C RV | 25 ft and up | Covered or outdoor parking space |

Before you rent, check the door height and width, since most roll-up doors are 7 to 8 feet tall. For full preparation steps, read our guide to [vehicle storage](/car-storage) in the US.

## How to choose the right large self storage unit

Choose the smallest large unit that holds everything with a walkway, then confirm access, door size and climate needs before you sign.

Follow these steps:

- List your big items first. Sofas, beds, dressers, appliances and vehicles decide the size more than boxes do.

- Measure anything over 6 feet. Include trailer tongues, table leaves and mattress lengths.

- Plan a walkway. A 2 to 3 foot aisle down the middle makes a large unit far easier to use.

- Pick the access type. Drive-up units let you back a truck to the door, which saves hours when you move heavy furniture.

- Decide on climate control. Wood, leather, electronics and paper do better in a [climate controlled unit](/climate-controlled-storage), especially in hot or humid areas. Our climate controlled storage guide explains when it is worth the extra cost.

- Check access hours. If you will visit early or late, look for [24-hour access](/blog/24-hour-access-storage-units-what-you-need-to-know-before-you-rent) storage units.

If you are planning a full household move, our moving home guide covers timing, budgets and when to use storage between homes.

## How to save money on large storage units

You can usually cut the cost of large storage units by 10% to 30% by sizing down where you can, booking online and renting in the off-season.

- Rent in fall or winter: demand drops between October and February, and so do rates.

- Book online: web rates and move-in deals are often lower than walk-in prices. Learn how [first month free](/blog/first-month-free-storage-how-the-deals-work) storage offers work before you sign.

- Skip climate control for sturdy items: tools, metal shelving and vehicles rarely need it.

- Compare a few facilities nearby: rates for the same size can vary widely across one city.

- Ask about the rate after the promo: introductory prices often rise after a few months.

- Consider a portable container for short moves: for a quick move, [moving containers](/blog/how-do-moving-containers-work-costs-companies-and-tips-2026) can cost less than a large unit plus a truck.

## Frequently asked questions

What is the largest storage unit size? The largest standard storage unit is usually a 10x30, at 300 square feet. Some facilities offer 10x40 or 12x30 units, and outdoor parking spaces can go up to 40 or 50 feet for RVs.

How much is a 10x25 storage unit? A 10x25 storage unit typically costs about $225 to $270 a month, or $280 to $400 with climate control, depending on your city.

Is a 10x25 big enough for a car and furniture? Yes, in most cases. A midsize car takes up about 15 feet of depth, which leaves around 10 feet for furniture and boxes.

What is the difference between a 10x20 and a 10x30? A 10x30 is 10 feet deeper and adds 100 square feet of floor space. It usually costs about $65 to $100 more per month.

Are large storage units cheaper per square foot? Often, yes. Large units tend to cost slightly less per square foot than small units, so they are better value when you fill them.

Can I run a business out of a large storage unit? Most facilities let you store inventory and equipment, but they do not allow you to work, sell or host customers inside the unit. Check the lease rules before you rent.

Do large storage units have electricity? Most do not. A few facilities offer units with power outlets for a higher rate, so ask if you need to charge a vehicle battery or run lights.

How far ahead should I reserve a large storage unit? Reserve one to three weeks ahead in spring and summer. Large units, especially 10x25s, are limited at many facilities and fill fast during moving season.
