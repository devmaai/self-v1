**Introduction**

To store furniture in a storage unit without damage, clean and fully dry every piece, take apart what you can, wrap it in breathable covers instead of sealed plastic, and keep it off the floor on pallets with a gap from the walls. For wood, leather, upholstery and anything stored longer than a season, a [climate controlled unit](/climate-controlled-storage), which typically adds $20 to $75 a month, is the safest choice. This guide walks you through prep, wrapping, packing the unit, picking the right size, and the most common mistakes that cause warping, mold and scratches.

## What do you need to store furniture in a storage unit?

You need a small kit of cleaning supplies, protective wrap and basic tools before move day. Gathering it all first keeps you from cutting corners at the unit.

- Cleaning: mild soap, microfiber cloths, wood cleaner, leather conditioner and a vacuum with an upholstery brush.

- Covers: cotton sheets, moving blankets or breathable furniture covers. Skip sealed plastic for long stays.

- Padding: foam corners, bubble wrap for glass and mirrors, and cardboard for tabletops.

- Hardware: screwdrivers, an Allen key set and zip-top bags for screws and bolts.

- Floor protection: wooden pallets or plastic floor sheeting to lift items off the concrete.

- Labels: a marker and tape so every bag of hardware and every wrapped piece has a name.

Most of this costs $40 to $120 in total, far less than replacing one water-stained sofa.

## How do you prepare furniture for storage?

Prepare furniture by cleaning it, letting it dry completely and breaking it down into flat, stackable parts. Moisture and dirt trapped under a cover are the main causes of mold and stains in storage.

- Clean every surface. Dust wood, vacuum upholstery and wipe metal. Food crumbs and oils attract pests and leave marks.

- Dry for at least 24 hours. Anything packed damp can grow mildew within weeks, especially in humid months.

- Treat the material. Polish wood, condition leather and use a fabric protector on upholstery.

- Disassemble what you can. Remove table legs, bed frames, shelves and headboards. Tape or bag the hardware and attach it to the matching piece.

- Empty drawers, then use them. Drawers can hold light items such as linens, but keep them under about 20 pounds so the runners do not bend.

- Photograph it. Quick photos of each piece record its condition, which helps with any insurance claim later.

## How to wrap and protect each type of furniture

Wrap furniture in breathable material and match the protection to the material. Different surfaces fail in different ways, so one approach does not suit everything.

| Furniture type | How to prepare it | Best wrap | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wood tables, dressers, chairs | Clean, polish, remove legs and knobs | Moving blankets or cotton sheets | Warping and cracking in heat and humidity |
| Leather sofas and chairs | Clean and condition | Cotton sheet or breathable cover | Drying, cracking and color fading |
| Fabric sofas and upholstered chairs | Vacuum and treat with fabric protector | Breathable furniture cover | Mildew, odors and pests |
| Mattresses | Vacuum and air out | Breathable mattress bag | Mold if stored flat on the floor or sealed airtight |
| Glass tops and mirrors | Remove from frames where possible | Bubble wrap plus cardboard corners | Cracks from pressure or tipping |
| Metal frames and outdoor furniture | Clean and dry fully | Blanket or sheet | Rust from trapped moisture |
| Electronics and appliances | Unplug, drain and dry | Original box or padded wrap | Condensation and temperature swings |

A few rules apply to every piece:

- Avoid sealed plastic for long stays. Plastic wrap is fine for a few days in transit, but it traps moisture over weeks and months.

- Never tape directly onto wood or leather. Adhesive can lift the finish. Tape the blanket to itself instead.

- Protect corners and legs. These take the most knocks while you load and unload.

## How to store a couch in a storage unit

Store a couch upright on its back or flat on its feet, depending on the space you have, wrapped in a breathable cover and lifted off the floor. Vacuum and clean it first, remove the cushions and legs if possible, and store the cushions on top or beside it in fabric bags rather than plastic.

- Standing a sofa on end saves floor space, but only do it for sturdy frames and shorter stays, since it can stress the joints and springs.

- Laying it on its feet is the safest option for long-term storage and for leather or antique pieces.

- Never stack heavy items on a couch. Weight on the cushions or arms can crush the padding and bend the frame.

Sectionals should be separated into their individual pieces, with each section wrapped on its own.

## Do you need climate controlled storage for furniture?

You need climate control for wood, leather, upholstered and antique furniture, and for any furniture you plan to store for more than a few months in a hot, cold or humid area. A climate controlled unit usually holds temperatures between about 55 and 80 degrees and keeps humidity steadier, which prevents wood from swelling, glue joints from loosening and fabric from growing mildew.

It typically costs about 17% to 30% more than a standard unit. You can often skip it for:

- Metal and plastic furniture

- Outdoor and patio sets

- Short stays of a few weeks in a mild climate

## How to pack furniture into self storage units

Pack self storage units with the heaviest, sturdiest furniture at the back and on the bottom, and keep a clear walkway to the front. A planned layout protects your furniture and lets you reach things without unpacking the whole unit.

- Lay down pallets or floor sheeting first. Concrete floors can wick moisture, even indoors.

- Leave 2 to 3 inches between furniture and the walls so air can move around each piece.

- Load large, heavy items first: dressers, bed frames, tables and appliances go at the back.

- Stand mattresses and tabletops on their edges against a wall, padded and supported so they cannot fall.

- Stack only on strong, flat surfaces. Put light boxes on top of dressers, never heavy boxes on chairs or sofas.

- Keep a center aisle. A walkway means you will not climb over furniture to reach a box.

- **Put items you will need first near the door.**

## What size storage unit do you need for furniture?

Most people need a 5x10 for a studio, a 10x10 for one to two bedrooms of furniture, and a 10x15 or 10x20 for a larger home. Rent the size that fits your furniture once it is stacked and arranged with an aisle, rather than the size that feels roomy when empty.

| Unit size | Typical furniture it holds | Typical monthly cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | A chair, side tables, small boxes | $35 to $55 |
| 5x10 | Studio furniture: bed, dresser, small sofa | $60 to $80 |
| 10x10 | One to two bedrooms of furniture | $115 to $130 |
| 10x15 | Two to three bedrooms, including appliances | $150 to $165 |

## How to store furniture long term in self storage

To store furniture long term, choose climate control, use breathable covers and check on the unit every one to three months. Small problems such as a damp corner or a slipping cover are easy to fix early and costly to discover after a year.

- Visit regularly. Look for moisture, pests, odors and any covers that have slipped.

- Air out upholstery on visits by loosening covers for a few minutes.

- Reapply leather conditioner every six months or so for long stays.

- Add moisture absorbers in humid regions, and replace them as they fill.

- Review your coverage. Check whether your policy covers stored items in our guide to whether [renters insurance](/blog/does-renters-insurance-cover-storage-units) covers storage units.

## Frequently asked questions

How do you store furniture in a storage unit so it does not get moldy? Clean and dry every piece fully, use breathable covers instead of plastic, raise items off the floor and leave a gap from the walls. Climate control adds the strongest protection against mold.

Is it okay to wrap furniture in plastic for storage? Plastic is fine for protection during the move itself. For storage longer than a few days, swap it for blankets or breathable covers so moisture can escape.

Can you store furniture in a storage unit without climate control? Yes, for metal, plastic and outdoor furniture, or for short stays in a mild climate. Wood, leather, upholstered and antique pieces are safer in a climate controlled unit.

Should you stand a couch on its end in storage? Only for sturdy frames and shorter stays. For long-term storage or leather sofas, keep the couch on its feet to protect the frame and springs.

How long can furniture stay in a storage unit? Well-prepared furniture can stay in storage for years, especially in a climate controlled unit. Check on it every one to three months to catch problems early.

What size storage unit do I need for a one-bedroom apartment? A 10x10 unit usually fits one to two bedrooms of furniture, while a 5x10 suits a studio. Leave room for a walkway.

Do you need insurance for furniture in storage? Most facilities require some coverage. Your home or renters policy may already cover stored items, so check before buying the facility plan.
