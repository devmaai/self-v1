**Introduction**

Commercial movers typically charge $750 to $5,000 for a small office of around 10 people and $5,000 to $15,000 or more for a mid-sized office of 50 to 100 employees, with local crews billing about $100 to $200 an hour for three or four movers. The final price depends on your square footage, how much IT equipment you have, and how far you are going. In this guide you will see what a commercial moving company does, what office movers cost by size, how to vet a mover, and how to use self storage to keep your business running while you relocate.

## What do commercial movers do?

Commercial movers relocate businesses, including offices, retail stores, medical practices, warehouses, and studios, from one location to another. They plan the move around your working hours so your team loses as little time as possible.

A full-service commercial moving company usually handles:

- **Planning:** a site walkthrough, an inventory, and a move schedule.

- **Packing and labeling:** crates for desk contents, files, and supplies, with color-coded labels by department or room.

- **Furniture work:** taking apart and rebuilding desks, cubicles, conference tables, and shelving.

- **IT and equipment:** disconnecting, wrapping, and moving computers, servers, printers, and phones. Some movers bring in IT partners for cabling.

- **Specialty items:** safes, medical or lab equipment, artwork, and retail fixtures.

- **Storage:** short-term holding for furniture or stock if your new space is not ready.

## How much do commercial movers cost?

Most local office moves cost $750 to $15,000, and large relocations can run $30,000 or more. Movers price jobs by the hour for small local moves and by a fixed quote for larger or long-distance moves.

| **Office size** | **Approximate square feet** | **Typical cost range** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small (about 10 employees) | 1,000 to 1,500 | $750 to $5,000 |
| Medium (about 25 to 50 employees) | 3,000 to 5,000 | $1,500 to $15,000 |
| Large (50 to 100 employees) | 5,000 to 10,000 | $5,000 to $30,000 |
| Enterprise (200+ employees) | 10,000+ | $15,000 to $30,000 or more |

Other common ways movers price the work:

- **Hourly crew rates:** about $75 to $150 an hour for three movers and a truck, and about $100 to $200 an hour for four movers.

- **Per employee:** roughly $50 to $250 or more per workstation, depending on how much each desk holds.

- **IT and cabling:** often a separate line item, commonly a few thousand dollars for a 30-person office.

What pushes the price up:

- **Distance:** interstate moves cost far more than moves across town.

- **After-hours work:** nights and weekends keep your business open but can carry higher labor rates.

- **Access:** stairs, small elevators, loading dock rules, and long carries from the truck add time.

- **Season:** May through August is peak moving season, so quotes are often higher in summer.

Costs also vary from city to city. Labor, parking, and building rules in dense metros such as New York City or Los Angeles usually make the same move cost more than it would in a smaller market. ## How to choose a commercial moving company

Choose a commercial moving company by checking its licensing, its experience with businesses like yours, and the coverage it offers before you compare prices. A low quote means little if the mover cannot protect your equipment or finish on schedule.

Use this short vetting list:

- **Check the USDOT number.** Interstate movers must be registered with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration. You can look up any mover with the [FMCSA mover search tool](<https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move/search-mover>). For local moves, check your state's licensing rules.

- **Ask for commercial references.** Look for experience with your type of space, such as medical offices, retail, or server rooms.

- **Get at least three on-site quotes.** A walkthrough leads to a more accurate price than a phone estimate.

- **Confirm coverage.** Basic liability is often limited to a set amount per pound per item, so ask about full value protection and request the mover's certificate of insurance for your building manager.

- **Ask who does the work.** Find out whether the company uses its own crews or subcontractors.

- **Get it in writing.** Your contract should list the dates, inventory, services, rates, and any extra fees.

## Office relocation timeline: when should you start planning?

Start planning an office relocation about three to six months before your move date for a small or mid-sized office, and six to twelve months ahead for a large one. That gives you time to review your lease, book movers, and plan your IT transfer.

| **Time before the move** | **What to do** |
| --- | --- |
| 6 months | Review your lease notice terms, set a budget, and name an internal move lead |
| 3 to 4 months | Get mover quotes, measure the new space, and plan the floor layout |
| 2 months | Book your mover, order internet and phone service, and update vendors |
| 1 month | Start packing archives and rarely used items, and reserve storage if needed |
| 1 to 2 weeks | Label desks and equipment, back up data, and brief your team |
| Move week | Pack personal desks, disconnect IT, and walk through both spaces |

## Office moving checklist: what to do before move day

An office moving checklist keeps every task assigned and on time. Give each item an owner and a deadline so nothing falls through the gaps.

- **Inventory everything:** furniture, IT equipment, supplies, and stock, so you know what moves, what goes into storage, and what you can sell or donate.

- **Back up all data** and confirm your IT plan for servers, phones, and Wi-Fi at the new site.

- **Update your address** with clients, suppliers, your bank, your website, and online business listings.

- **Book elevators and loading docks** at both buildings and share the details with your mover.

- **Pack non-essentials early**, such as archive files, seasonal displays, and spare furniture.

- **Give each employee a packing kit** with crates, labels, and a floor plan that shows where their desk goes.

- **Plan the first day:** set up workstations, the kitchen, and restrooms before your team arrives.

## How self storage units help during a business move

Self storage units give your business a secure place for furniture, files, inventory, and equipment during a move, especially when your new space is smaller or not ready on time. Renting a unit for a few months can also stop you from paying movers to haul items you do not need right away.

Common ways businesses use storage during a move:

- **Bridging a gap** between your old lease ending and your new space opening.

- **Storing spare furniture** while you decide what fits the new layout.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much do commercial movers charge per hour?** Most local commercial movers charge about $75 to $150 an hour for a three-person crew and a truck, and about $100 to $200 an hour for four movers. After-hours and weekend work can cost more.

**How much does it cost to move a small office?** A small office of around 10 employees usually costs $750 to $5,000 to move locally. IT setup, specialty items, and long distances add to the total.

**What is the difference between commercial movers and residential movers?** Commercial movers specialize in business moves, including IT equipment, cubicles, and after-hours scheduling. Residential movers focus on homes and personal belongings.

**How far in advance should I book office movers?** Book office movers about two to three months before a small or mid-sized move, and earlier for large moves or summer dates. Start planning the full office relocation three to six months ahead.

**Do commercial movers provide storage?** Many commercial moving companies offer short-term storage, and you can also rent your own self storage unit near your new office. Your own unit gives you direct access to files and stock whenever you need them.

**Do commercial movers need a USDOT number?** Movers that cross state lines must be registered with the FMCSA and have a USDOT number. Local movers follow state rules, so check licensing with your state.

**Is it cheaper to move an office on a weekend?** Weekend moves can carry higher labor rates, but they often save money overall because your team keeps working during the week. Ask movers to quote both weekday and weekend options.
