**Introduction**

A storage unit in [Stockton](/storage-search/california/stockton), California costs about $40 to $360 a month depending on size, and the popular [10x10 unit](/blog/10x10-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside) typically runs $90 to $145, with the citywide average close to $110 to $115. That puts Stockton slightly below the national average and far below Bay Area cities like [San Jose](/storage-search/california/san-jose) and [Oakland](/storage-search/california/oakland), where the same unit can cost 50% more. This guide breaks down current Stockton storage prices by size, shows which parts of town cost the most and least, covers car and [RV storage](/rv-storage), lists the fees that land on your bill, and explains when and how to book for a better rate.

## Stockton storage unit prices by size

Here is what Stockton facilities typically charge each month, based on current street rates across the city and nearby San Joaquin County suburbs.

| Unit size | Typical monthly cost | With climate control | Roughly what it holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $40 to $70 | +$10 to $25 | A closet's worth of boxes and seasonal gear |
| 5x10 | $60 to $100 | +$15 to $35 | A studio or small one-bedroom apartment |
| 10x10 | $90 to $145 | +$25 to $55 | One to two bedrooms of furniture |
| 10x15 | $130 to $200 | +$30 to $70 | Two to three bedrooms plus appliances |
| 10x20 | $160 to $260 | +$40 to $90 | A three to four bedroom home, or a car |
| 10x30 | $230 to $360 | +$55 to $110 | A large home, a boat, or business stock |

Quick take:

- The 10x10 is the most rented size in Stockton, averaging roughly $105 to $115 a month.

- A 5x5 locker is often under $50, which makes it one of the cheapest small-unit markets in California.

- Online specials can cut the first month or two by up to half, so the posted rate is rarely the only price on offer.

## Why storage in Stockton costs less than the Bay Area

Stockton storage prices sit well below coastal California for a few local reasons.

- Lower land costs. Land in the Central Valley costs a fraction of what it does in Silicon Valley or [San Francisco](/storage-search/california/san-francisco), and facility owners pass those savings through to monthly rents.

- More room to build. Stockton has more industrial and commercial land, so large drive-up facilities are common and supply keeps pace with demand.

- Bigger homes and more garages. Many Stockton homes have garages and yards, so fewer households need storage just to function day to day.

- Steady demand from movers. Bay Area residents relocating for lower housing costs, plus families moving between Stockton, Lodi, Manteca, and Tracy, keep mid-size units busy.

- Vehicle and RV demand. With lots of trucks, boats, and RVs, many facilities dedicate space to outdoor parking, which helps keep indoor unit prices competitive.

## Is climate control worth it in Stockton?

Climate control is worth it in Stockton for valuable or sensitive items and for longer stays. The upgrade usually adds $10 to $55 a month, and it pays off mainly because of the Central Valley summer.

It is worth paying for if:

- You are storing through summer, when Stockton often sits in the high 90s and can top 100 degrees, and a closed metal unit gets much hotter inside.

- You are storing wood or leather furniture, electronics, artwork, documents, photos, or musical instruments.

- Your items will stay in storage for more than a few months.

- You are storing near the Delta waterways, where winter tule fog and damp air can lead to mildew over time.

You can usually skip it for tools, bikes, patio furniture, and short stays in spring or fall. For a full breakdown, read what [climate controlled storage](/climate-controlled-storage) covers.

## Extra fees to budget for in Stockton

The advertised monthly rate is rarely your full bill. Plan for some combination of these.

| Fee | Typical cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Administration fee | $20 to $35, one time | Charged at move-in at most facilities |
| Security deposit | $0 to one month's rent | Often waived, refundable where it applies |
| Insurance or protection plan | $10 to $30 a month | Your renters or homeowners policy may already cover stored items |
| Disc lock | $10 to $20, one time | Some facilities require their own lock |
| Late fee | Set by your rental agreement | California law limits how much can be charged |
| Rent increases | Often 5% to 10% after 6 to 12 months | Ask how often rates change before you sign |

California's self storage law sets rules for late fees, lien notices, and lien sales, so read the late payment section of your rental agreement before you sign. If you plan to use your own insurance, check the off-premises limit, since many policies cover stored items at only a share of your total personal property coverage. Our guide to whether [renters insurance](/blog/does-renters-insurance-cover-storage-units) covers storage units explains what to look for.

## When is storage cheapest in Stockton?

Storage in Stockton is usually cheapest from November through February, after the summer moving season has passed.

- May through August is the priciest stretch, when lease turnovers, home sales, and graduations overlap.

- Early May is busy near University of the Pacific, so students should reserve a unit a few weeks before move-out.

- Fall and winter bring the best promotions, including discounted first months and waived admin fees.

If your dates are flexible, booking a few weeks before the summer rush gets you more choice of sizes and a better starting rate. Many facilities run [move-in specials](/blog/first-month-free-storage-how-the-deals-work), and our guide to first month free storage deals explains how to spot an offer that actually saves you money.

## How to get a cheaper storage unit in Stockton

- Right-size the unit. A well-packed 10x10 beats a half-empty 10x15. Check the 10x10 size guide and the 5x10 size guide before you book.

- Look south and along Highway 99. South Stockton and Weston Ranch facilities usually cost less than Brookside or Spanos Park for the same size.

- Choose an upper floor or a standard unit. Elevator-access and non-climate units often cost less than ground-floor climate controlled space.

- Reserve online. Web-only rates and move-in specials usually beat the walk-in price.

- Ask about the rate after the promo. Confirm when and by how much your rent can rise.

- Skip the protection plan if you are covered. Use your renters or homeowners policy if it already covers stored belongings.

- Consider a locker for small loads. If you only have a few boxes, a [storage locker](/blog/what-is-a-storage-locker-sizes-costs-and-when-to-rent-one) can cost far less than a full unit.
