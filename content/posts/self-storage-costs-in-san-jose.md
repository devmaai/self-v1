**Introduction**

A storage unit in [San Jose](/storage-search/california/san-jose) costs about $55 to $500 a month depending on size and location, and the most popular [10x10 unit](/blog/10x10-storage-unit-size-price-and-what-fits-inside) typically runs $130 to $220, with the citywide average sitting around $165 to $175. That makes San Jose one of the pricier storage markets in the country, roughly 40% to 50% above the national average for a 10x10. This guide breaks down current San Jose storage prices by size, shows which parts of the South Bay cost the most and least, covers the fees that land on your bill, and explains when and how to book for a better rate.

## San Jose storage unit prices by size

Here is what San Jose facilities typically charge each month, based on current street rates across the city and nearby South Bay suburbs.

| Unit size | Typical monthly cost | With climate control | Roughly what it holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $55 to $90 | +$15 to $35 | A closet's worth of boxes and seasonal gear |
| 5x10 | $85 to $135 | +$20 to $45 | A studio or small one-bedroom apartment |
| 10x10 | $130 to $220 | +$30 to $70 | One to two bedrooms of furniture |
| 10x15 | $190 to $290 | +$40 to $90 | Two to three bedrooms plus appliances |
| 10x20 | $250 to $390 | +$55 to $120 | A three to four bedroom home, or a car |
| 10x30 | $340 to $500 | +$70 to $145 | A large home, a boat, or business stock |

Quick take:

- The 10x10 is the most rented size in San Jose, averaging roughly $165 to $175 a month.

- A 5x10 is the best value for apartment renters, often around $105 a month.

- Online specials can cut the first month or two by up to half, so the posted rate is rarely the only price on offer.

## Why storage costs more in San Jose

San Jose storage prices sit well above the national average for a few local reasons.

- Some of the most expensive land in the country. Silicon Valley real estate is costly, and facility owners pass those land and building costs through to monthly rents.

- Smaller homes and pricey square footage. When every extra bedroom costs a fortune, many households use a storage unit as an affordable spare room for bikes, sports gear, and seasonal items.

- A large renter population. Many San Jose residents rent apartments or condos with limited closet space and no garage, which keeps small and mid-size units in steady demand.

- Constant job moves. Tech hiring cycles, relocations, and remote-work shifts mean people move in and out of the area often, and many need storage between leases.

- Limited room for new facilities. Zoning rules and competition for industrial land make it hard to build new storage, so supply grows slowly.

## Cheapest and priciest San Jose areas for storage

Where you book can matter almost as much as the size you pick. The same 10x10 can cost $50 or more per month less a short drive south or east.

| Area | Price tendency | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Downtown, Willow Glen, Rose Garden | Highest | Dense, central neighborhoods with few facility sites |
| Santa Clara, Sunnyvale, Cupertino, West San Jose | Above average | Strong demand from tech workers and high home values |
| North San Jose, Milpitas, Campbell | Moderate to high | Good facility choice near freeways and business parks |
| East San Jose, South San Jose, Blossom Valley | Moderate | More supply and larger drive-up facilities |
| Morgan Hill, Gilroy | Lowest | Lower land costs on the southern edge of the county |

A few practical takeaways:

- If you only visit your unit a few times a year, a facility in South San Jose or further south can save you hundreds of dollars a year.

- East San Jose facilities along major roads often run frequent online discounts, so compare the web rate with the walk-in rate.

## Is climate control worth it in San Jose?

Climate control is worth it in San Jose for valuable or sensitive items and for longer stays. The upgrade usually adds $15 to $70 a month, and it pays off mostly because of summer heat.

It is worth paying for if:

- You are storing through the summer, when San Jose regularly hits the 90s and a closed metal unit can get much hotter inside.

- You are storing wood or leather furniture, electronics, computers, artwork, documents, photos, or musical instruments.

- Your items will stay in storage for more than a few months.

- You are storing near the bay, where winter fog and damp can lead to musty smells over time.

You can usually skip it for tools, bikes, patio furniture, and short stays in spring or fall. For a full breakdown, read what [climate controlled storage](/climate-controlled-storage) covers.

## Extra fees to budget for in San Jose

The advertised monthly rate is rarely your full bill. Plan for some combination of these.

| Fee | Typical cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Administration fee | $25 to $40, one time | Charged at move-in at most facilities |
| Security deposit | $0 to one month's rent | Often waived, refundable where it applies |
| Insurance or protection plan | $10 to $40 a month | Your renters or homeowners policy may already cover stored items |
| Disc lock | $10 to $25, one time | Some facilities require their own lock |
| Late fee | Set by your rental agreement | California law limits how much can be charged |
| Rent increases | Often 5% to 10% after 6 to 12 months | Ask how often rates change before you sign |

California's self storage law sets rules for late fees, lien notices, and lien sales, so read the late payment section of your rental agreement before you sign. If you plan to use your own insurance, check the off-premises limit, since many policies cover stored items at only a share of your total personal property coverage. ## When is storage cheapest in San Jose?

Storage in San Jose is usually cheapest from November through February, after the summer moving season has passed.

- May through August is the priciest stretch, when lease turnovers, home sales, and graduations overlap.

- Late August is busy near San Jose State University, so students should reserve a unit a few weeks before move-out.

- Fall and winter bring the best promotions, including discounted first months and waived admin fees.

If your dates are flexible, booking a few weeks before the summer rush gets you more choice of sizes and a better starting rate. Many facilities run [move-in specials](/blog/first-month-free-storage-how-the-deals-work), and our guide to first month free storage deals explains how to spot an offer that actually saves you money.

## How to get a cheaper storage unit in San Jose

- Right-size the unit. A well-packed 10x10 beats a half-empty 10x15. Check the 10x10 size guide and the 5x10 size guide before you book.

- Look south and east. South San Jose, East San Jose, and Morgan Hill facilities usually cost less than Downtown or West Valley sites for the same size.

- Choose an upper floor. Elevator-access units often cost less than ground-floor or drive-up units.

- Reserve online. Web-only rates and move-in specials usually beat the walk-in price.

- Ask about the rate after the promo. Confirm when and by how much your rent can rise.

- Skip the protection plan if you are covered. Use your renters or homeowners policy if it already covers stored belongings.

- Consider a locker for small loads. If you only have a few boxes, a [storage locker](/blog/what-is-a-storage-locker-sizes-costs-and-when-to-rent-one) can cost far less than a full unit.
