**Introduction**

Cheap storage units in Brooklyn usually cost $10 to $45 a month for a small 5x5 and $100 to $190 for a budget 10x10 with an online move-in special, while typical 10x10 rates across the borough run about $270 to $415 a month. Brooklyn has more than 60 self storage facilities, from Williamsburg and Greenpoint down to Sheepshead Bay and Canarsie, so you have real choice if you know where to look. This guide shows what cheap storage costs by size, which Brooklyn neighborhoods tend to be the most affordable, the top-rated brands to check first, and easy ways to lower your monthly bill.

## How much do cheap storage units cost in Brooklyn?

A cheap storage unit in Brooklyn runs about $10 to $450 a month for most renters, depending on size, neighborhood, and whether you book during an online promotion. Here are typical monthly ranges based on current listed rates across the borough.

| **Unit size** | **Budget price range** | **Typical rate** | **Roughly what it holds** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $10 to $45 | $85 to $100 | A closet of boxes and seasonal gear |
| 5x10 | $30 to $110 | $145 to $200 | A studio apartment or dorm room |
| 10x10 | $100 to $190 | $270 to $415 | One to two bedrooms of furniture |
| 10x15 | $160 to $260 | $340 to $600 | Two to three bedrooms |
| 10x20 | $340 to $450 | $445 to $800 | A three-bedroom home or a car |

A few quick notes:

- The lowest prices are usually first-month or limited-time online specials, so expect the regular rate once the promotion ends.

- Most Brooklyn facilities are multi-story indoor buildings, so climate control is widely available and often adds only $15 to $40 a month.

- Self storage in Brooklyn costs more than the national average because New York City land is expensive. Our guide to [how much a storage unit costs](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/how-much-is-a-storage-unit>) shows the national picture.

The cheapest self storage in Brooklyn is usually in east and southeast Brooklyn around East New York, Cypress Hills, Brownsville, Canarsie, and Flatlands, and in the industrial pockets of Sunset Park and Borough Park. Neighborhoods close to Manhattan, such as Brooklyn Heights, DUMBO, Downtown Brooklyn, Park Slope, and Williamsburg, tend to cost the most.

## What makes storage units more expensive in Brooklyn?

Storage units cost more in parts of Brooklyn mainly because of high land prices, small apartments, and constant moving across New York City. Several local factors push prices up:

- **High land values.** Brooklyn is often more affordable than Manhattan, but New York City land is still expensive, so facilities charge more than the national average.

- **Small living spaces.** Many Brooklyn apartments and brownstone units have limited closet space, so lots of residents rent a unit year-round for seasonal items, bikes, and extra furniture.

- **Frequent moves.** Lease turnovers keep demand high, especially in summer. Our [moving home guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/moving-home-in-the-us-costs-timing-and-how-to-plan-your-move>) helps you plan the timing.

- **Students.** Brooklyn College, Pratt Institute, NYU Tandon, LIU Brooklyn, and City Tech create a rush for small units each May and August. Our [student storage guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/student-storage-in-the-us-the-complete-guide-for-college-students>) explains how to book early and save.

- **Limited parking.** Street parking is tight in much of the borough, so drive-up and vehicle spaces fill quickly. Our [vehicle storage guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/vehicle-storage-in-the-us-how-to-store-a-car-rv-or-boat>) and [car storage page](<https://www.selfstorage.help/car-storage>) explain your options.

## How to get cheap storage in Brooklyn

The fastest way to get cheap storage in Brooklyn is to book online during a promotion at a facility in east or southeast Brooklyn. These tips can cut your costs even more:

- **Grab a move-in special.** Many Brooklyn facilities offer a discounted or free first month. Our [first month free storage guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/first-month-free-storage-how-the-deals-work>) explains how to spot a deal that truly saves money.

- **Book in fall or winter.** November through February is usually the most affordable time, after the summer moving rush.

- **Reserve online.** Web-only rates are often lower than walk-in prices.

- **Choose an upper floor.** Upper-floor units reached by elevator usually cost less than ground-floor or drive-up units.

- **Pay only for what you need.** Climate control is often standard in Brooklyn indoor buildings. Our [climate controlled storage guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/climate-controlled-storage-in-the-us-everything-you-need-to-know>) shows which items need it.

- **Use your own insurance.** Your renters or homeowners policy may already cover stored items. See [does renters insurance cover storage units](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/does-renters-insurance-cover-storage-units>).

- **Ask about future rate increases.** Many facilities raise rent after 6 to 12 months, so confirm the plan before you sign.

## Is cheap storage in Brooklyn safe?

Yes, cheap storage in Brooklyn can be safe, as long as the facility has the basic security features you need. A low price is only a good deal if your belongings stay protected.

Before you rent, check for:

- Gated or keypad access with a personal entry code

- Video cameras covering hallways, elevators, and entrances

- Good lighting and on-site staff during office hours

- Individual unit alarms or disc locks

- Climate control if you are storing electronics, wood furniture, or documents through humid summers and cold winters

- Clear rental terms, including admin fees, late charges, and how rent increases work

New York sets its own rules for self storage liens in [New York Lien Law Section 182](<https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LIE/182>), so read your rental agreement carefully and keep your payments up to date.
