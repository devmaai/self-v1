**Introduction**

A storage unit in Los Angeles costs about $55 to $520 a month depending on size and neighborhood, and the most popular 10x10 unit typically runs $130 to $220 before climate control. That puts Los Angeles among the most expensive storage markets in the country, well above the national average for the same size. This guide breaks down current Los Angeles storage prices by size, shows which parts of the metro cost the most and least, covers the fees that end up on your bill, and explains when and how to book for a better rate.

## Los Angeles storage unit prices by size

Here is what Los Angeles facilities typically charge each month, based on current street rates across the city and nearby suburbs.

| Unit size | Typical monthly cost | With climate control | Roughly what it holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $55 to $95 | +$20 to $40 | A closet's worth of boxes and seasonal gear |
| 5x10 | $85 to $140 | +$25 to $50 | A studio or small one-bedroom apartment |
| 10x10 | $130 to $220 | +$35 to $75 | One to two bedrooms of furniture |
| 10x15 | $190 to $300 | +$45 to $95 | Two to three bedrooms plus appliances |
| 10x20 | $250 to $400 | +$60 to $125 | A three to four bedroom home, or a car |
| 10x30 | $340 to $520 | +$70 to $150 | A large home, a boat, or business stock |

Quick take:

- The 10x10 is the most rented size in Los Angeles, at roughly $130 to $220 a month.

- A 5x10 is the best value for apartment renters, often under $140 a month.

- The same unit can cost 30% to 60% more on the Westside than in the San Fernando Valley or the Inland Empire.

## Why storage is so expensive in Los Angeles

Los Angeles storage prices sit near the top of the national range for a few local reasons.

- Land is scarce and costly. Facility owners compete with housing, retail, and studios for every parcel, and those land costs show up in your monthly rent.

- A renter-heavy city. More than half of Los Angeles households rent, and many apartments come with little closet space and no garage, which keeps small and mid-size units full all year.

- Strict zoning for new facilities. Many neighborhoods limit where self storage can be built, so supply grows slowly while demand keeps rising.

- Constant moving. Job changes, entertainment industry projects, and students at UCLA, USC, and Cal State LA create steady move-in and move-out traffic.

- Multi-story buildings. Most central facilities are tall indoor buildings with elevators, which cost more to build and run than single-story drive-up sites.

## Cheapest and priciest Los Angeles areas for storage

Where you book matters almost as much as the size you pick. The same 10x10 can cost $80 or more per month less just 30 minutes inland.

| Area | Price tendency | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Santa Monica, Venice, West LA, Beverly Hills | Highest | Dense, land-constrained Westside neighborhoods |
| Hollywood, Silver Lake, Downtown, Koreatown | Above average | Central, high-density neighborhoods with few new facilities |
| Pasadena, Culver City, Glendale, Burbank | Moderate to high | Strong demand but more facility choice |
| San Fernando Valley, South LA, Long Beach, Torrance | Moderate | More supply and easier freeway access |
| Inland Empire and northern Orange County suburbs | Lowest | Lower land costs and larger facilities |

A few practical takeaways:

- If you only visit your unit a few times a year, a facility in the Valley or in Riverside can save you hundreds of dollars a year.

- South Bay and Long Beach facilities are a good middle ground if you live near the coast but want lower rates.

- Orange County options in Anaheim and Santa Ana often undercut Westside prices for the same size.

## Is climate control worth it in Los Angeles?

Climate control is worth it for many Los Angeles renters, especially inland. The upgrade usually adds $20 to $75 a month, and it pays off when heat is the main risk to your belongings.

It is worth paying for if:

- You store in the San Fernando Valley, the San Gabriel Valley, or the Inland Empire, where summer and early fall heat waves can push a closed metal unit well past 100 degrees.

- You are near the beach, where marine layer humidity and salt air can rust metal and warp wood over time.

- You are storing wood or leather furniture, electronics, artwork, film or camera gear, documents, photos, or musical instruments.

- Your items will stay in storage for more than a few months.

You can usually skip it for tools, patio furniture, bikes, and short stays. For a full breakdown, read what climate controlled storage covers.

## Extra fees to budget for in Los Angeles

The advertised monthly rate is rarely your full bill. Plan for some combination of these.

| Fee | Typical cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Administration fee | $25 to $50, one time | Charged at move-in at most facilities |
| Security deposit | $0 to one month's rent | Often waived, refundable where it applies |
| Insurance or protection plan | $12 to $45 a month | Your renters or homeowners policy may already cover stored items |
| Disc lock | $10 to $25, one time | Some facilities require their own lock |
| Late fee | Set by your rental agreement | California law caps how much can be charged |
| Rent increases | Often 5% to 10% after 6 to 12 months | Ask how often rates change before you sign |

California's self storage law sets rules for late fees, lien notices, and lien sales, so read the late payment section of your rental agreement before you sign. If you plan to use your own insurance, check the off-premises limit, since many policies cover stored items at only a percentage of your total personal property coverage.

## When is storage cheapest in Los Angeles?

Storage in Los Angeles is usually cheapest from November through February, after the summer moving season and the late-summer student rush have passed.

- May through August is the priciest stretch, when home sales, lease turnovers, and graduations all overlap.

- Late August and September are tight near campuses in Westwood, University Park, and Pasadena, so book early if you are a student.

- Fall and winter bring the best promotions, including discounted first months and waived admin fees.

If your dates are flexible, booking a few weeks before the summer rush gets you more choice of sizes and a better starting rate. Many facilities run move-in specials, and our guide to first month free storage deals explains how to spot an offer that actually saves you money.

## How to get a cheaper storage unit in Los Angeles

- Right-size the unit. A well-packed 10x10 beats a half-empty 10x15. Check the 10x10 size guide and the 5x10 size guide before you book.

- Look outside the Westside. Valley, South Bay, and Inland Empire facilities consistently cost less for the same size.

- Choose an upper floor. Elevator-access units often cost less than ground-floor or drive-up units.

- Reserve online. Web-only rates and move-in specials usually beat the walk-in price.

- Ask about the rate after the promo. Confirm when and by how much your rent can rise.

- Skip the protection plan if you are covered. Use your renters or homeowners policy if it already covers stored belongings.

- Consider a locker for small loads. If you only have a few boxes, a storage locker can cost far less than a full unit.

## Frequently asked questions

How much is a storage unit in Los Angeles? Most Los Angeles storage units cost $55 to $520 a month depending on size. A 10x10 typically runs $130 to $220 without climate control.

How much is a 10x10 storage unit in Los Angeles? A 10x10 usually costs $130 to $220 a month, or roughly $165 to $295 with climate control, depending on the neighborhood.

What is the cheapest storage unit in Los Angeles? A 5x5 is the cheapest size, usually $55 to $95 a month. For the lowest rates, look at facilities in the San Fernando Valley, South LA, or the Inland Empire.

Why is storage so expensive in Los Angeles? Land is scarce and expensive, zoning limits new facilities, and a large renter population keeps demand high all year.

When is the best time to rent storage in Los Angeles? November through February is usually cheapest, after the summer moving season and the back-to-school rush have passed.

Do I need climate control in Los Angeles? It is a smart choice in hot inland areas and near the coast, and for furniture, electronics, and documents stored longer than a few months.

Are drive-up storage units cheaper in Los Angeles? Not always. Drive-up units are convenient, but upper-floor indoor units in the same facility are often cheaper per square foot.
