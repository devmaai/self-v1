**Introduction**

Cheap storage units in Los Angeles usually cost about $40 to $80 a month for a 5x5 and $100 to $200 a month for a 10x10, compared with a citywide average closer to $250 for a 10x10. Prices swing a lot by neighborhood, unit floor, and promotion, so the same size can cost twice as much a few miles away. This guide shows affordable price ranges by size, the parts of LA where storage is cheapest, the brands with the most budget-friendly options, and the simple moves that lower your monthly bill.

## How much do cheap storage units cost in Los Angeles?

The cheapest storage units in Los Angeles start around $40 a month for a small 5x5, and an affordable 10x10 typically runs $100 to $200 a month. Online promotions can push first-month prices even lower.

Los Angeles is one of the priciest storage markets in the country, so "cheap" here means the low end of a high range. The table below compares the affordable end of the market with typical citywide averages.

| Unit size | Cheap range in LA (per month) | Typical citywide average | Roughly what it holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $40 to $80 | About $120 | Boxes, seasonal gear, a small closet |
| 5x10 | $60 to $130 | About $150 | A studio apartment or dorm room |
| 10x10 | $100 to $200 | About $250 | One to two bedrooms of furniture |
| 10x15 | $140 to $260 | About $295 | Two to three bedrooms |
| 10x20 | $180 to $380 | About $460 | A three to four bedroom home or a car |

A few things to keep in mind:

- Most facilities add a one-time admin fee of around $25 to $30, plus a lock and insurance or a protection plan.

- The lowest advertised rates are often introductory and can rise after six to twelve months.

- Climate control usually adds $20 to $75 a month, so skip it when your items can handle heat.

## Where can you find the cheapest storage in Los Angeles?

The cheapest storage in Los Angeles is usually in East LA, South LA, and the San Fernando Valley, plus nearby suburbs inland and to the south. Westside neighborhoods such as Santa Monica, Venice, and West LA tend to be the most expensive.

| Area | Price tendency | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Santa Monica, Venice, West LA, Beverly Hills | Highest | Dense, land-constrained Westside neighborhoods |
| Hollywood, Koreatown, Silver Lake | Above average | Central, high-density areas with few new facilities |
| Downtown, Boyle Heights, East LA, South LA | Moderate to low | More industrial land and many facilities competing |
| San Fernando Valley (Van Nuys, North Hollywood, Sylmar) | Moderate to low | Larger sites and easier freeway access |
| Long Beach, Orange County, Inland Empire | Often lowest | Lower land costs and bigger facilities |

If you only visit your unit a few times a year, driving a little farther can save you a lot:

- Long Beach is a good middle ground if you live in the South Bay or near the coast.

- Anaheim and Santa Ana often undercut Westside prices for the same size.

- Riverside is one of the most affordable options for long-term storage you rarely need to access.

## Why is storage so expensive in Los Angeles?

Storage in Los Angeles costs more because land is scarce, zoning limits new facilities, and a large renter population keeps demand high all year.

- Land is costly. Facilities compete with housing, retail, and studios for every parcel.

- Lots of renters. More than half of LA households rent, and many apartments have small closets and no garage.

- Slow new supply. Many neighborhoods restrict where new self storage can be built.

- Constant moves. Job changes, entertainment projects, and students at UCLA, USC, and Cal State LA keep units busy.

- Multi-story buildings. Central facilities often have elevators and indoor halls that cost more to build and run.

Knowing these drivers helps you shop smarter. Areas with more industrial land and more competing facilities tend to have more affordable rates.

## How can you get a cheaper storage unit in Los Angeles?

The fastest ways to get cheap storage in Los Angeles are to book online, pick an upper-floor unit, rent the smallest size that fits, and look a few miles outside the Westside.

- Reserve online. Web rates are often well below walk-in prices for the same unit.

- Use move-in specials. Look for $1 first month, half off for two months, or waived admin fees. Our first month free storage guide explains how to compare offers.

- Choose an upper floor. Elevator-access units usually cost less than ground-floor or drive-up units.

- Skip climate control when it is safe. Tools, bikes, patio furniture, and plastic bins usually do fine without it for short stays.

- Book in late fall or winter. November through February tends to bring the best rates, while May through August is the busiest stretch.

- Use your own insurance. Your renters or homeowners policy may already cover stored items.

- Ask about future increases. Confirm when and by how much your rent can rise after the promo ends.

## Is cheap storage in Los Angeles safe?

Yes, cheap storage in Los Angeles can be safe if the facility has the right basics. A lower price usually reflects location or unit type, so check security features before you sign.

What to look for:

- Gated entry with personal access codes

- Video surveillance and good lighting

- On-site staff during office hours

- Individually alarmed units or floor-limited access at some facilities

- Clear rental terms covering fees, rate increases, and late payments

California law sets rules for late fees and lien sales, so read the late payment section of your rental agreement. Visit the facility in person or check recent photos before you move in.

What is the cheapest storage unit size in Los Angeles? A 5x5 is the cheapest size, usually $40 to $80 a month at affordable facilities. Lockers can cost even less.
