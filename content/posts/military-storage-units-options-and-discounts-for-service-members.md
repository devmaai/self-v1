**Introduction**

Military storage units are regular self storage units rented by service members, and many facilities near bases take 5% to 10% off monthly rent when you show a valid military ID. A 10x10 unit, the most common size for a PCS move or deployment, typically runs about $115 to $130 a month before any discount. This guide covers which brands offer military discounts, when the government pays for storage instead of you, how federal law protects your stored belongings, and how to pick the right size and location.

## What are military storage units?

A military storage unit is a standard storage unit that a service member rents during a permanent change of station (PCS), a deployment, training, or the gap between leaving one home and moving into the next. You rent it the same way as anyone else, and you may also qualify for military pricing and extra legal protections.

You will usually need storage in one of these situations:

- **Deployment:** you keep your household goods, car, or personal items safe while you are away for several months.

- **PCS move:** your new housing is not ready yet, or you are moving into a smaller place on or off base.

- **Barracks or shipboard living:** you cannot keep furniture, seasonal gear, or a vehicle where you live.

- **Separation or retirement:** you need time to decide where to settle before moving everything.

If your timeline is a few weeks or months, our guide to [short-term storage options and costs](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/short-term-storage-options-costs-and-how-it-works>) explains how month-to-month rentals work.

Yes. Several national self storage brands offer an active duty discount, usually 5% to 10% off the monthly rent. Terms vary by brand and by facility, so always confirm with the location you plan to use.

| **Brand** | **Military discount** | **Key terms** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extra Space Storage | 5% off monthly rent | Active duty with valid military ID, at select facilities near military bases; the account must be in the service member's name |
| CubeSmart | 5% off monthly rent | Active military; show your military ID at move-in; can be combined with other CubeSmart promotions |
| StorQuest | 10% off | Offered to active military members at StorQuest locations across the country |

A few details are worth knowing before you sign:

- [Extra Space Storage](<https://www.extraspace.com/self-storage/faq/does-extra-space-storage-offer-a-military-discount/>) applies its discount to base rent before taxes, fees, and protection plans, and it cannot be combined with other Extra Space offers.

- [CubeSmart](<https://www.cubesmart.com/military>) lets you stack the military discount on top of its current promotions, which can make the first months especially affordable.

- [StorQuest](<https://www.storquest.com/blog/5-ways-active-military-members-utilize-storquest-self-storage>) offers its 10% military discount across its network.

Many independent facilities near bases also offer discounts or waive the admin fee for service members, even if they do not advertise it. Ask at the counter. ## When does the military pay for your storage?

The government covers storage in several situations, so check your entitlements before you pay out of pocket. Your local Personal Property Office or transportation office confirms what applies to your orders.

- **Storage in transit (SIT):** when you ship household goods, you are entitled to 90 days of temporary storage, according to the [NAVSUP household goods FAQ](<https://www.navsup.navy.mil/NAVSUP-Household-Goods/FAQ>). You can request an extra 90 days, but extensions are reviewed closely and are not approved for personal convenience.

- **Non-temporary storage (NTS):** long-term storage for belongings you are not shipping to your new duty station. [Military OneSource](<https://militaryonesource.mil/resources/millife-guides/preparing-to-move-or-pcs>) notes that NTS may be authorized for one year after retirement and 180 days after separation, after which the cost becomes yours.

- **Vehicle storage on overseas orders:** for OCONUS moves, the government may pay to store one privately owned vehicle during your tour.

A private self storage unit makes the most sense when your needs fall outside these programs: you want access to your things, you are between leases, your SIT time is running out, or you are storing items you chose not to ship. Our [guide to planning a move in the US](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/moving-home-in-the-us-costs-timing-and-how-to-plan-your-move>) walks through timing, costs, and when storage fits into a move.

## How does the SCRA protect your stored belongings?

The Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA) gives you strong protection if you fall behind on rent while serving. Under [50 U.S.C. § 3958](<https://law.justia.com/codes/us/2015/title-50/chapter-50/subchapter-iii/sec.-3958>), a storage facility cannot sell or otherwise enforce a lien on a service member's property during military service, or for 90 days after it ends, without first getting a court order.

What this means for you:

- A facility must go to court before it can auction your unit while you are serving.

- If military service affects your ability to pay, a court can pause the process or adjust what you owe.

- The protection covers storage liens and other liens on your property.

To use these protections, tell the facility you are on active duty when you sign the lease, keep a copy of your orders on file with them, and set up autopay so a missed payment during deployment never becomes an issue. If you have questions about a specific lease, your installation legal assistance office can help at no cost.

## What size storage unit do you need for a PCS or deployment?

Most single service members need a 5x10, and most families storing a full home need a 10x15 or 10x20. Here is how common sizes line up with military life, using typical US monthly rates before any discount.

| **Unit size** | **Typical monthly cost** | **Good fit for** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $35 to $55 | Uniforms, gear, documents, and a few boxes |
| 5x10 | $60 to $80 | A barracks room or studio apartment |
| 10x10 | $115 to $130 | A one or two bedroom apartment |
| 10x15 | $150 to $165 | A two or three bedroom home |
| 10x20 | $195 to $220 | A larger family home, or a car |

### Do you need climate controlled storage units for a long deployment?

For storage longer than a few months, climate control is usually worth it. It protects wood furniture, electronics, uniforms, photos, and important papers from heat and humidity. It typically adds about 17% to 30% to the base rent. Learn more in our guide to [climate controlled storage](<https://www.selfstorage.help/climate-controlled-storage>).

## How do you store a car during deployment?

You can store a car in a drive-up storage unit, a covered parking space, or an outdoor parking spot at most storage facilities. A 10x20 unit fits most cars and SUVs.

Before you leave:

- Fill the tank and add fuel stabilizer.

- Change the oil and top off all fluids.

- Inflate the tires to the recommended pressure.

- Connect a battery tender or disconnect the battery.

- Ask your insurer about reduced coverage for a stored vehicle, and keep your registration current.

Our [vehicle storage guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/vehicle-storage-in-the-us-how-to-store-a-car-rv-or-boat>) covers cars, RVs, and boats in more detail, and you can compare [car storage options](<https://www.selfstorage.help/car-storage>) near you.

## How do you choose self storage near your base?

Pick the facility that is easiest for you, or a trusted family member, to reach. Prices for the same unit size can differ a lot between cities and even between neighborhoods, so compare a few options before you commit.

- **Prices follow local rent:** coastal cities cost more. A 10x10 near the Navy and Marine Corps installations in San Diego often costs more than the same unit in a smaller inland town. Browse [storage units in San Diego](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/california/san-diego>) to compare.

- **Check towns just outside the gate:** near Hill Air Force Base in Utah, facilities in [Layton](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/utah/layton>) and [Ogden](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/utah/ogden>) give you options at different price points.

- **Think about who will visit:** if a spouse or relative will check on the unit, choose a location near them and add them as an authorized user.

- **Look for the right features:** gated access, video monitoring, good lighting, and online payments matter more when you are far away.

## Checklist before you deploy or PCS

Run through this list before you hand over the keys to your home.

- Confirm your SIT or NTS entitlements with your transportation office.

- Ask the facility about its military discount and bring your military ID.

- Give the facility a copy of your orders and note your active duty status on the lease.

- Set up autopay and confirm the card will not expire while you are away.

- Add a trusted person as an authorized contact with access.

- Check whether your renter or homeowner policy covers stored items. Our post on [whether renters insurance covers storage units](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/does-renters-insurance-cover-storage-units>) explains what to ask.

- Take photos and keep an inventory of what you store.

- Pack smart so you can find things later. Follow our [step-by-step guide to packing a storage unit](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/how-to-pack-and-organize-a-storage-unit-a-step-by-step-guide>).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do storage units give military discounts?** Many do. Extra Space Storage and CubeSmart offer 5% off monthly rent for active duty members, and StorQuest offers 10%. Terms vary by facility, so ask before you sign.

**Who qualifies for a military storage discount?** Most discounts are for active duty service members with a valid military ID. Some facilities also extend them to veterans or spouses, so ask your local location.

**Does the military pay for storage during a PCS?** Often, yes. If you ship household goods, you are entitled to 90 days of storage in transit, and non-temporary storage may be authorized for items you do not ship. Your transportation office confirms the details.

**Can a storage facility auction my unit while I am deployed?** Not without a court order. The SCRA blocks a facility from enforcing a lien on your property during military service and for 90 days afterward unless a court approves it first.

**What size storage unit do I need for a deployment?** A 5x10 holds the contents of a barracks room or studio, and a 10x10 fits a one or two bedroom apartment. Families storing a full home usually need a 10x15 or 10x20.

**Is climate controlled storage worth it for a deployment?** For stays longer than a few months, usually yes. It protects furniture, electronics, uniforms, and documents from heat and humidity for roughly 17% to 30% more per month.

**Can someone else access my storage unit while I am away?** Yes. Add a spouse, relative, or friend as an authorized user on your lease so they can visit, pay, or move items for you.
