**Introduction**

A moving container typically costs $225 to $830 for a local move and $2,000 to $7,700 or more for a long-distance move, depending on the size, the company, and how far you are going. A moving container is a large, weatherproof box that a company drops off at your home, that you pack at your own pace, and that the company then hauls to storage, to your new address, or both. This guide walks through what moving containers cost in 2026, how they actually work, which size fits your move, how they stack up against a storage unit or a rental truck, and how to pack and book one without overpaying.

## What is a moving container and how does it work?

A moving container is a portable, steel or heavy-duty-plastic box, usually 7 to 16 feet long, that gets delivered to your driveway so you can load it on your own schedule.

Here is the typical process from booking to drop-off:

- You book online or by phone and pick a container size and a delivery window.

- The company drops the container in your driveway or a legal parking spot.

- You load it yourself, or hire movers to load it for you, over hours or days.

- The company picks it up and either stores it at a secure facility or drives it to your new address.

- If you are moving long-distance, your container often travels by truck alongside other customers' containers rather than in a dedicated trip, which is part of what keeps the price down compared to full-service movers.

Unlike a rental truck, you never drive the container yourself. Unlike a self storage unit, the container comes to you instead of you driving boxes to a facility.

## How much do moving containers cost?

Moving containers cost more as the distance and the number of containers go up, and prices vary by company and season. Here is what to expect based on current 2026 pricing from major container companies.

| Move type | Typical cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local move, studio or 1 bedroom | $225 to $450 | Usually one small or medium container |
| Local move, 2 to 3 bedrooms | $350 to $650 | Often one large container |
| Local move, 3+ bedrooms | $530 to $830+ | May need two containers |
| Long-distance, under 500 miles | $2,000 to $3,000 | Price climbs with container count |
| Long-distance, around 1,200 miles | $2,700 to $5,000+ | Varies widely by company |
| Cross-country, 2,000+ miles | $4,900 to $7,700+ | Largest homes and longest routes |
| Storage-only, per month | $150 to $300+ | If you leave the container at a facility |

Quick take:

- A local move with one container averages a few hundred dollars all in.

- Long-distance pricing is the single biggest swing factor, more than container size.

- Keeping the container at a facility for a month or more adds a separate storage fee on top of the delivery and pickup charges.

## What size moving container do you need?

Match the container to your home size so you are not paying for two containers when one will do, or cramming a four-bedroom home into one box.

| Container size | Roughly fits | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 to 8 ft | A few rooms of boxes and small furniture | Studio apartments, decluttering, partial moves |
| 12 ft | A one to two-bedroom home | Most apartment moves |
| 16 ft | A three to four-bedroom home | Full house moves, often paired with a second container |

If you are unsure how much space your belongings take up, the same math used for storage unit sizing applies here. A one-bedroom apartment generally needs about the same footprint as a 10x10 storage unit, which lines up with a single 12 ft container.

## Moving containers vs. storage units: which is better?

Choose a moving container when you need your things to travel with you, and choose a traditional storage unit when you mainly need space and plan to access your items often.

|   | Moving container | Storage unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delivered to your door | Yes | No, you drive to the facility |
| Good for long-distance moves | Yes | No |
| Climate control available | Limited, varies by company | Common, widely available |
| Easy to access items mid-storage | No, once picked up it is sealed away | Yes, most facilities offer regular access |
| Typical monthly cost | $150 to $300+ | $50 to $260, see storage unit pricing |
| Best for | Moving plus storage in one step | Long-term storage, frequent access, local flexibility |

If you need to store items for months and want to grab things from the unit occasionally, a standard storage facility usually works out cheaper and more convenient. If you are relocating across the state or the country and want to pack once without driving a truck yourself, a container is often the better trade-off.

## Moving containers vs. renting a truck: which saves more?

A rental truck is usually the cheapest option if you are willing to drive it yourself, while a moving container costs more but removes the driving and lets you load at your own pace.

- Truck rental: typically the lowest total cost, but you drive the truck, handle fuel and tolls, and load and unload in a single push. See how to choose the right truck size.

- Moving container: higher cost, but you load over several days, someone else drives, and you can add storage time if your new place is not ready.

- Full-service movers: the most expensive option, but movers handle the loading, driving, and unloading entirely.

A good rule of thumb: pick a truck if you want the lowest price and do not mind driving, pick a container if flexibility and not driving matter more than price, and pick full-service movers if your budget allows and you want to do none of the physical work.

## Moving container companies to compare

A handful of national companies dominate the moving container space, and pricing and container sizes differ enough between them to be worth a side-by-side look before you book.

- Size options: companies differ in whether they offer one standard size or several sizes, which affects how many containers your move needs.

- Service area: not every company serves every state or every rural zip code, so confirm availability before you commit to a date.

- Storage options: some companies store your container at their own secure facility, while others require you to arrange separate storage.

- Insurance and valuation coverage: coverage levels and costs vary, so ask what is included before assuming you are covered.

- Quote structure: get a written quote that includes delivery, pickup, mileage, and any fuel surcharge, since the headline price often leaves these out.

Request quotes from at least three companies for the same move details. Prices for the same route and size can differ by several hundred dollars.

## How to pack a moving container the right way

Pack a moving container the same way you would pack a truck: heaviest items on the bottom, weight distributed evenly, and nothing left loose that can shift during transport.

- Load heavy furniture and appliances first, against the walls, to keep the container's weight centered.

- Fill gaps with soft items like pillows and blankets so boxes cannot slide.

- Use furniture pads and straps or rope to secure large pieces to the container's built-in tie-down points.

- Save the last few feet near the door for anything you will need immediately at the new place.

- Take photos of how everything is packed in case you need them for an insurance claim.

For a full room-by-room packing approach, the same techniques in how to pack a moving truck carry over directly to a container.

## Pros and cons of moving containers

Weigh these trade-offs before you decide a container is the right call for your move.

**Pros:**

- Delivered to your door, so you skip driving a rental truck.

- Load and unload on your own schedule, often over several days.

- Doubles as temporary storage if your new home is not ready yet.

- Works well for long-distance and cross-country moves.

**Cons:**

- Costs more than a DIY truck rental for the same move.

- Not every company offers climate control.

- Requires space to park the container, which can be a problem in dense cities or with HOA rules.

- Once picked up, you generally cannot access your things until delivery.

## When a moving container makes the most sense

A moving container is usually the right call in these situations:

- Long-distance moves where you want to avoid driving a truck across the country.

- Overlap periods between move-out and move-in dates, when you need short-term storage built into the move.

- Partial moves, like sending furniture ahead while you finish packing the rest.

- Renovation projects, where you need belongings out of the house for a few weeks but want them close by.

- Decluttering before a sale, since staged homes show better with less furniture, and a container keeps items on-site but out of sight.

## How to save money on a moving container rental

- Book early. Rates rise as your date approaches, especially during the summer peak season.

- Compare at least three companies for the same size and route, since quotes can vary widely.

- Move in the off-season. Fall and winter rates are typically lower than the May through September rush.

- Right-size your container count. One larger container is often cheaper than two smaller ones for the same volume.

- Ask about current promotions. Many companies run seasonal discounts or waive the first month of storage.

- Pack it yourself. Hiring loaders adds cost on top of the container rental itself.

## Frequently asked questions

How much do moving containers cost? Local moves typically run $225 to $830 depending on home size, while long-distance moves range from about $2,000 to $7,700 or more depending on distance and container count.

How do moving containers work? A company delivers an empty container to your home, you load it on your own timeline, and the company picks it up and either stores it or drives it to your new address.

How long can I keep a moving container? Most companies allow you to keep a container for as long as you need for an ongoing monthly storage fee, whether at your home, a company facility, or in transit.

Are moving containers cheaper than a moving truck? No, a self-driven truck rental is typically the cheaper option. Containers cost more because someone else does the driving and you get more flexible loading time.

Can I pack a moving container myself? Yes, most people load and unload their own containers. You can also hire local loading help through the container company or a separate moving labor service.

Do moving containers come with climate control? Some do and some do not. Coverage varies by company, so ask directly if you are storing items sensitive to heat or humidity.

What size moving container do I need? A 7 to 8 ft container suits a studio or partial move, a 12 ft container fits most one to two-bedroom homes, and a 16 ft container handles a three to four-bedroom home.

Is a moving container better than a storage unit? It depends on your needs. Containers are better when your items need to travel with you during a move. Storage units are usually cheaper and easier to access if you mainly need space for months at a time.
