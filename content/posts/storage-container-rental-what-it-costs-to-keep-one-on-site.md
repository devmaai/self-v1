**Introduction**

Renting a storage container usually costs $75 to $300 per month, plus $100 to $400 each way for delivery and pickup. A 20-foot container, the most popular size, typically runs $100 to $200 a month. A storage container is a steel, weatherproof box (often a converted shipping container) that a company drops on your property and leaves there for as long as you need it. This guide covers current prices by size, how the rental process works, where you can legally place a container, how on-site container storage compares to a traditional storage unit, and when buying beats renting.

## How much does it cost to rent a storage container?

Most people pay $100 to $200 a month for a standard 20-foot storage container, before delivery and pickup. Your final price depends on the container size, how far you are from the supplier's yard, how long you rent, and any upgrades you add.

| Container size | Typical monthly rent | Delivery (each way) | Roughly what it holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 ft | $75 to $150 | $100 to $250 | One room of furniture, garage overflow, or tools |
| 20 ft | $100 to $200 | $125 to $300 | A two to three-bedroom home's furniture and boxes |
| 40 ft | $150 to $300 | $150 to $400 | A four to five-bedroom home, or business inventory |
| 40 ft high cube | $175 to $325 | $150 to $400 | Same footprint as 40 ft with an extra foot of height |

Quick take:

- Delivery and pickup can add $200 to $800 to the total, so they matter most on short rentals.

- Many suppliers bill a full month even if you only need the container for a week or two.

- Longer leases (six months or more) often bring the monthly rate down.

## What size storage container do you need?

Pick the size by what you need to store once it is stacked, plus how much room you have on your property. A 20-foot container fits most home and small business needs, while 40-foot containers suit businesses, farms, and job sites.

| Size | Exterior footprint | Interior space | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 ft | About 10 x 8 ft | About 560 cubic feet | Small projects, tight driveways, tools and equipment |
| 20 ft | About 20 x 8 ft | About 1,150 cubic feet | Home overflow, small business stock, seasonal gear |
| 40 ft | About 40 x 8 ft | About 2,350 cubic feet | Business stock, construction sites, large homes |
| 40 ft high cube | About 40 x 8 ft, 9.5 ft tall | About 2,700 cubic feet | Tall items, shelving, bulky equipment |

For comparison, a 20-foot container offers about the same floor space as a 10x20 storage unit. If you are unsure how much room your things take up, the storage size guide uses the same math.

## How does storage container rental work?

The supplier delivers an empty container to your property, you load it on your own schedule, and the container stays put until you call for pickup. Here is the usual process:

- Get quotes. Share your address, the size you want, your start date, and where the container will sit.

- Prepare the spot. Clear a level, firm area with room for the delivery truck to get in and tilt the container off.

- Delivery day. A tilt-bed or roll-off truck slides the container into place. The driver needs straight, open access with no low wires or branches.

- Load and lock. You fill the container at your own pace and secure it with a heavy-duty padlock, often through a lock box on the door.

- Pickup or purchase. When you are done, the supplier hauls the empty container away. Some companies offer a rent-to-own option if you decide to keep it.

The biggest difference from a moving container is that a storage container usually stays on your property the whole time. It is built for on-site storage, not for being trucked to a new address with your belongings inside.

## Where can you put a storage container? Space, permits, and HOA rules

You can usually place a storage container on your own driveway, yard, or commercial lot, as long as the ground is level and local rules allow it. Street placement almost always needs a permit.

**Space the delivery truck needs:**

- 10 or 20 ft container: roughly 60 to 75 feet of straight-line clearance and about 12 feet of width.

- 40 ft container: roughly 100 to 120 feet of straight-line clearance.

- Overhead: about 14 feet of open height, clear of power lines and tree branches.

- Ground: firm and level surfaces like concrete, asphalt, or compacted gravel. Soft grass can sink or rut under the weight.

**Rules to check before you book:**

- City permits: many cities require a permit for containers on the street or sidewalk, and some limit how long a container can sit on residential property.

- HOA rules: homeowner associations often restrict containers or cap them at a set number of days.

- Rental properties: if you rent your home, get written approval from your landlord first.

A quick call to your city's permit office and your HOA can save you from fines or a forced early pickup.

## Storage container rental vs. self storage unit: which is better?

Choose a storage container when you want your belongings close by on your own property. Choose a self storage unit when you have no space for a container, need climate control, or plan to store for many months.

|   | On-site storage container | Self storage unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Location | Stays on your property | At a facility |
| Typical monthly cost | $75 to $300 + delivery | $50 to $260 |
| Access | Anytime, right outside | During facility hours, or 24/7 at some sites |
| Climate control | Rare, insulated options cost more | Widely available |
| Space needed at home | A lot | None |
| Best for | Job sites, business overflow, farms | Long-term or climate-sensitive storage |

If you need storage for a few weeks while you sort out a move or a project, compare both options in our guide to short-term storage.

## When does an on-site storage container make sense?

A storage container is a great fit when you want easy, daily access to your things without driving anywhere. Common uses include:

- Events and seasonal businesses: keep tents, booths, and seasonal stock where you use them, without a trip to a facility.

- Construction and job sites: keep tools and materials locked up on-site overnight.

- Business overflow: store seasonal inventory, retail fixtures, or equipment without leasing a warehouse.

- Farms and ranches: protect feed, tack, and equipment from weather and pests.

- Schools and nonprofits: store sports equipment, supplies, and event materials securely on site.

- Disaster recovery: store salvageable belongings after a flood or fire while repairs happen.

It is usually a weaker fit if you live in an apartment or townhome with no driveway, need climate control for wood furniture or electronics, or plan to store for a year or more. In those cases a storage unit is often cheaper and simpler.

## What extra fees and features should you ask about?

The monthly rate is only part of the bill. Ask each supplier about these before you sign:

| Fee or feature | Typical cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delivery and pickup | $100 to $400 each way | Rises with distance from the supplier's yard |
| Damage waiver or insurance | $10 to $30 a month | Check whether your home or business policy already covers it |
| Lock box | $10 to $50, one time | Protects the padlock from bolt cutters |
| Ground prep or blocks | $0 to $100 | Some sites need wood blocks or gravel under the container |
| Relocation on-site | $75 to $200 | If you need the container moved on your property |
| Insulation or vents | Varies | Helps limit condensation and heat buildup |
| Taxes and permits | Varies by city | Permits are usually your responsibility |

Also ask about the condition of the container. Most rentals are "cargo-worthy" or "wind and watertight" used shipping containers, which is fine for storage. Ask for one with working door seals and no rust holes.

## Should you rent or buy a storage container?

Rent a storage container if you need it for less than a year, and consider buying a used one if you need storage for two years or more. Here is how to decide.

- Rent for under a year. Monthly rent of $100 to $200 on a 20-foot container adds up to the price of a used one, about $2,000 to $4,500 delivered, within roughly one to three years.

- Buy for two years or more. Owning usually costs less over a long stretch, and a used container keeps much of its value if you sell it later.

- Inspect before you buy. Check the doors, seals, and floor in person or ask for recent photos. One-trip containers cost the most, while cargo-worthy units are cheaper and fine for storage.

- Check permit rules first. Some cities treat a container kept for months as an accessory structure, which can bring a permit fee, setback rules, or a time limit.

- Ask about rent-to-own. Some suppliers credit part of your rent toward buying the container, which helps if you are not sure how long you need it.

- Get delivery quotes either way. Distance to the supplier's yard drives the delivery fee, so a nearby yard can save you hundreds whether you rent or buy.

- Compare against a storage unit. For long stays, a local facility with a first month free deal can beat container pricing.

## Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to rent a storage container per month? Most storage containers rent for $75 to $300 a month depending on size. A 20-foot container usually costs $100 to $200 a month, plus delivery and pickup fees.

How much are delivery and pickup for a storage container? Expect about $100 to $400 each way, depending on how far you are from the supplier and the container size. Some companies offer a flat round-trip price.

Can I rent a storage container for one week? Some suppliers offer short rentals, but many charge a full month minimum. For a week or two, ask about daily rates or compare a short-term storage unit.

Do I need a permit for a storage container in my driveway? It depends on your city and HOA. Driveway placement on private property often needs no permit, but street placement almost always does, and some HOAs limit how long a container can stay.

Is it cheaper to rent a storage container or a storage unit? For a few months on your own property, costs are often similar once delivery is included. For long-term storage, a self storage unit is usually cheaper and offers climate control options.

Are storage containers waterproof? Most rental containers are wind and watertight steel boxes that keep rain out. Condensation can still build up inside, so use pallets, moisture absorbers, and vents for sensitive items.

What size storage container is most popular? The 20-foot container is the most rented size. It holds the furniture and boxes from a two to three-bedroom home and fits on most driveways.

Is a storage container secure? Steel walls and lockable doors make containers hard to break into. Add a lock box and a heavy-duty padlock, and place the container where neighbors or staff can see it.
