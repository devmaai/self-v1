**Introduction**

To store clothes in a storage unit safely, wash and fully dry every item, pack it in clean plastic bins or wardrobe boxes with acid-free tissue, keep the containers off the floor, and choose a climate controlled unit if you are storing for more than a few months. A small 5x5 unit, which typically costs about $40 to $80 a month, holds the clothing from a full closet or two. Below you will find a step-by-step packing method, the best containers for each type of fabric, how long clothes can stay in storage, what size unit you need and how to keep moths, mildew and odors away.

## How do you store clothes in a storage unit?

You store clothes in a storage unit by cleaning them, sorting them by fabric and season, packing them in breathable or airtight containers that suit each fabric, and placing them on shelves or pallets inside a dry, clean unit. Follow this order and your clothes will come out looking the way they went in.

- Wash or dry clean everything. Body oils, perfume and food spots attract insects and set into stains over time.

- Dry items completely. Even slight dampness can lead to mildew inside a closed bin.

- Sort by fabric and season. Group wool, cotton, silk, synthetics and formalwear so each gets the right packing.

- Pack loosely. Overstuffed bins crease fabric and trap moisture.

- Label every container. List the contents and the season on at least two sides.

- Raise containers off the floor. Use shelving or pallets so air can circulate.

## What is the best way to store clothes long term?

The best way to store clothes long term is in clean, sealed plastic bins or acid-free boxes, layered with acid-free tissue, inside a climate controlled storage unit. This combination protects against dust, light, pests and humidity, which are the four main causes of fabric damage.

Here is how the common container options compare:

| Container | Best for | Pros | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plastic bins with lids | Everyday clothing, cotton, synthetics, kids' clothes | Stackable, waterproof, keep out pests | Pack only fully dry items to avoid trapped moisture |
| Wardrobe boxes | Suits, dresses, coats on hangers | Keep garments hanging and wrinkle free | Cardboard can absorb moisture in humid areas |
| Acid-free archival boxes | Wedding dresses, vintage pieces, silk | Gentle on delicate fibers, prevent yellowing | Cost more than standard boxes |
| Breathable cotton garment bags | Wool coats, formalwear | Let fabric breathe while blocking dust | Offer less pest protection than sealed bins |
| Vacuum seal bags | Synthetics, bedding, bulky jackets | Save a lot of space | Can crush natural fibers and down over time |
| Regular cardboard boxes | Short stays only | Cheap and easy to find | Attract pests and break down with humidity |

## How to pack clothes for storage by fabric type

Each fabric needs slightly different care, so pack by material instead of by owner or room.

- Cotton and linen: Fold loosely and stack in plastic bins. Place tissue between folds to limit permanent creases.

- Wool and cashmere: Fold, never hang, since hanging stretches the shoulders. Add cedar blocks or lavender sachets to deter moths.

- Silk: Wrap in acid-free tissue and store flat in an archival box away from light.

- Leather and suede: Condition first, then store in a breathable cotton bag. Plastic can trap moisture and cause cracking.

- Down jackets: Store loosely in a large bin or breathable bag so the filling keeps its loft.

- Formalwear and wedding dresses: Have them professionally cleaned, then store in an acid-free box or a breathable garment bag.

- Shoes: Clean them, stuff with acid-free paper to hold their shape and keep them in their own boxes.

If you are storing other fabric items, such as quilts or upholstered pieces, our guide on how to store furniture in a storage unit without damage covers wrapping and placement in more detail.

## Do you need a climate controlled unit for clothes?

You need a climate controlled unit for clothes if you are storing them for longer than a season, if they include natural fibers or delicate fabrics, or if you live somewhere with hot summers, cold winters or high humidity. Climate controlled self storage units usually stay between about 55 and 80 degrees and keep humidity steady, which prevents mildew, yellowing and brittle fibers.

Expect to pay roughly 20% to 30% more than a standard unit of the same size. A standard unit can work well for:

- Durable synthetics or work clothes stored for a few months

- Short stays in a mild, dry climate

- Clothing packed in sealed plastic bins with moisture absorbers

## How long can clothes stay in a storage unit?

Clothes can stay in a storage unit for several years when they are cleaned, packed in the right containers and kept in a climate controlled space. In a standard unit, plan to check on them at least every three to six months, especially through summer.

At each visit:

- Open a few bins and check for musty smells, discoloration or insect activity.

- Replace silica gel packs or moisture absorbers.

- Refold delicate items along new lines to prevent permanent creases.

## What size storage unit do you need for clothes?

Most people need a 5x5 or 5x10 storage unit for clothing, depending on how much else they are storing. A 5x5 fits roughly 10 to 15 medium bins plus a few wardrobe boxes, while a 5x10 adds room for shelving, larger wardrobe boxes and seasonal items.

## How to protect stored clothes from moths, mildew and odors

You protect stored clothes by keeping them clean, dry and sealed, and by using natural deterrents instead of strong chemicals.

### Keep pests out

- Pack only freshly cleaned clothing, since moth larvae feed on body oils and food residue.

- Use cedar blocks, cedar rings or lavender sachets in bins with wool and cashmere.

- Choose sealed plastic bins over cardboard boxes, which pests can chew through.

### Stop mildew before it starts

- Add silica gel packs or moisture absorbers to every bin.

- Leave a small gap between containers and the walls so air can move.

- Keep bins on shelves or pallets in case of a minor leak or condensation.

### Avoid odors and discoloration

- Skip mothballs. They leave a strong smell and are hard to air out of fabric.

- Avoid dry cleaner plastic bags, which trap moisture and can cause yellowing.

## How to organize clothes in self storage units

You organize clothes in a self storage unit by placing the items you will need first near the door and building a clear walkway to everything else.

- Front: Next season's clothing and anything you may need on short notice.

- Middle: Labeled bins on freestanding shelves, heaviest at the bottom.

- Back: Long-term items such as formalwear, keepsakes and outgrown kids' clothes.

- Hanging section: Wardrobe boxes or a sturdy garment rack along one wall.

Keep a simple inventory on your phone with a photo of each bin's contents and its label. The same approach works well for paperwork, as explained in our guide to document storage units.

## How to choose a storage facility for your clothes

The right facility for clothing is clean, dry, secure and close enough that you can visit easily when the seasons change. Before you sign, look for:

- Climate controlled units if you plan to store for more than a few months

- A clean, well-sealed building with no signs of leaks or pests

- Gated access, cameras and good lighting

- Access hours that match when you will swap seasonal clothes

## Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to store clothes in a storage unit? Yes. Clothes stay in good condition in a storage unit when they are cleaned, fully dried, packed in sealed bins or breathable bags and kept off the floor. A climate controlled unit adds the most protection for long stays.

Should I use plastic bins or cardboard boxes for clothes? Plastic bins are the better choice for storage units because they keep out moisture, dust and pests. Cardboard works for short stays but can absorb humidity and attract insects.

Can I vacuum seal clothes for long-term storage? You can vacuum seal synthetics, bedding and casual wear to save space. Avoid it for wool, silk, leather and down, since long compression can damage the fibers and flatten the filling.

How do I keep clothes from smelling musty in storage? Make sure every item is clean and dry, add silica gel packs or moisture absorbers to each bin and store in a climate controlled unit. Checking and airing items every few months also helps.

Do I need climate control to store clothes? Climate control is recommended for storage longer than a season, for natural or delicate fabrics and in hot, cold or humid areas. For a few months in a mild climate, a standard unit with sealed bins can be enough.

What should I not put in storage with my clothes? Avoid storing dirty or damp items, food, scented products that attract pests and anything flammable. Keep leather goods out of airtight plastic so they can breathe.

How much does it cost to store clothes in a storage unit? Most people spend about $40 to $120 a month on a 5x5 or 5x10 unit for clothing. Climate control usually adds 20% to 30% to the monthly rate.

How often should I check on clothes in storage? Plan to check every three to six months in a standard unit and at least twice a year in a climate controlled unit. Look for odors, discoloration and signs of pests.
