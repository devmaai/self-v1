**Introduction**

A heated storage unit keeps your belongings above freezing through winter, usually at a minimum of around 50 degrees, and it typically costs about 15% to 30% more than a standard unit of the same size. Heated storage is a smart pick when you live somewhere with cold winters and you are storing furniture, electronics, or anything with liquids that could freeze. In this guide you will see how heated units differ from full climate control, what they cost by size, which items need them, and how to choose the right facility.

## What is a heated storage unit?

A heated storage unit is a self storage space with a heating system that stops the temperature from dropping too low in cold months. Most heated units sit inside an enclosed building, and the facility sets a minimum temperature, commonly around 50 degrees, so the space never freezes.

Here is what heated storage usually includes and leaves out:

- **Included:** heat in fall and winter, an enclosed building, and protection from frost and freezing.

- **Often not included:** air conditioning in summer and active humidity control.

- **Access:** usually indoor hallways, so you load through a building entrance instead of driving up to the door.

Because the term varies from one operator to another, ask each facility what its heated units actually do and what temperature it keeps them at year round.

## Heated vs climate controlled storage: what is the difference?

Heated storage controls only the low end of the temperature range, while climate controlled storage keeps the space within a set band all year and may also manage humidity. Climate controlled units typically hold somewhere between 50 and 85 degrees, depending on the operator, and some also regulate moisture.

| **Feature** | **Standard unit** | **Heated unit** | **Climate controlled unit** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Winter protection | None | Yes, kept above freezing | Yes |
| Summer cooling | None | Usually none | Yes |
| Humidity control | None | Usually none | Often, depending on the facility |
| Typical price vs standard | Base rate | About 15% to 30% more | About 15% to 40% more |
| Best for | Tools, metal, patio gear | Furniture, electronics, liquids in cold regions | Documents, photos, wine, art, long stays |

If you live where winters are harsh but summers are mild, heated storage often covers what you need. If your area is hot and humid for much of the year, full climate control does more to protect sensitive items. ## How much do heated storage units cost?

Heated storage units usually cost about 15% to 30% more than standard storage units of the same size. Exact prices depend on your city, the facility, and the season you book.

| **Unit size** | **Typical standard rate** | **Typical heated rate** | **Good for** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5x5 | $35 to $55 | $40 to $70 | Boxes, small electronics, seasonal items |
| 5x10 | $60 to $80 | $70 to $105 | A studio apartment or a few furniture pieces |
| 10x10 | $115 to $130 | $130 to $170 | One to two bedrooms of furniture |
| 10x15 | $150 to $165 | $170 to $215 | Two to three bedrooms, appliances |
| 10x20 | $195 to $220 | $225 to $285 | A full home's contents |

A few things push the price up or down:

- **Location:** big-city and coastal markets cost the most.

- **Size:** larger units cost more, so check our [storage size guide](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-size-guide>) before you book.

- **Season:** fall and winter rates and move-in deals are often better than summer rates.

- **Floor:** ground-floor units near the entrance can cost more than upper-floor units.

## What should you store in heated self storage units?

Store anything in a heated self storage unit that can crack, warp, split, or freeze when the temperature drops. Cold air causes wood to shrink, makes some plastics brittle, and can damage batteries and screens.

Items that benefit most from heated storage:

- **Wood and upholstered furniture**, which can crack or warp in freezing temperatures. See our tips on [how to store furniture in a storage unit](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/how-to-store-furniture-in-a-storage-unit-without-damage>).

- **Electronics** such as TVs, computers, and gaming consoles, since extreme cold can harm components and screens.

- **Mattresses and bedding**, which stay fresher when kept away from frost. Our guide on [storing a mattress](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/how-to-store-a-mattress-in-a-storage-unit-without-damage>) covers wrapping and placement.

- **Musical instruments**, especially pianos, guitars, and violins.

- **Clothing and fabrics**, which you can protect further with the steps in our guide on [how to store clothes in a storage unit](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/how-to-store-clothes-in-a-storage-unit-a-long-term-guide>).

- **Liquids and paints**, where the facility allows them, since freezing can burst containers.

Items that usually do fine in a standard unit include metal tools, garden equipment, patio furniture, and plastic bins of sturdy household goods. Paper records, photos, and wine need steady humidity as well as warmth, so full climate control is the better fit for those. Our guide to [document storage units](<https://www.selfstorage.help/blog/document-storage-units-how-to-store-paperwork-and-records-safely>) explains why.

## Do you need heated storage in winter?

You need heated storage in winter if your area regularly drops below freezing and you are storing temperature-sensitive items for more than a few weeks. In milder regions, a standard unit is often enough for sturdy belongings.

Heated storage makes the most sense in places with long, cold winters. For example:

- **Salt Lake City, Utah:** cold, snowy winters make heat worth it for furniture and electronics. Browse options in [Salt Lake City storage](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/utah/salt-lake-city>).

- **Buffalo, New York:** heavy lake-effect snow and long freezes mean heated units are in steady demand. Compare units in [Buffalo storage](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/new-york/buffalo>).

- **Kansas City, Missouri:** winter cold snaps and humid summers make heated or fully climate controlled units a practical choice. See [Kansas City storage](<https://www.selfstorage.help/storage-search/missouri/kansas-city>).

Prices also differ from city to city. Heated and climate controlled units in large, expensive metros cost more than the same size in smaller markets, so compare several nearby facilities before you decide. Choose a facility close to home or work if you plan to visit often, since a short drive matters more in snowy weather.

## How to choose a heated self storage facility

Choose a heated self storage facility by confirming its temperature settings, building access, and security before you sign. A few questions save you from surprises later.

Ask each facility:

- What minimum temperature do your heated units keep in winter?

- Do the units also have cooling or humidity control in summer?

- Is the building fully enclosed, with indoor hallways?

- Are carts, elevators, and covered loading areas available?

- What are the access hours, and is the lot cleared of snow?

- What security features are in place, such as gated entry and cameras?

- How often does the rate change after the move-in promotion?

Large national brands such as [Public Storage](<https://www.publicstorage.com/>), [Extra Space Storage](<https://www.extraspace.com/>), [CubeSmart](<https://www.cubesmart.com/>), and [U-Haul](<https://www.uhaul.com/>) offer temperature-controlled units at many locations, and local operators often do too. Each lists its unit features online, which makes it easy to compare.

## How to prepare your items for heated storage

Prepare your items for heated storage by cleaning them, wrapping them in breathable covers, and keeping them off the floor. Heat protects against freezing, but good packing protects against dust and moisture.

- **Clean and dry everything** before it goes in, since trapped moisture can lead to mildew.

- **Use breathable covers** such as cotton sheets or furniture pads instead of tight plastic wrap.

- **Raise items on pallets or shelves** to keep them away from cold floors and any melting snow tracked in.

- **Leave a walkway** so air moves around your belongings and you can reach what you need.

- **Add moisture absorbers** if your heated unit has no humidity control. The [EPA recommends](<https://www.epa.gov/mold>) keeping indoor humidity below 60%, ideally between 30% and 50%, to limit mold.

- **Remove batteries** from electronics and store them separately.

## Frequently asked questions

**What temperature is a heated storage unit?** Most heated storage units are kept at a minimum of around 50 degrees in winter. The exact setting varies by facility, so ask before you rent.

**Is heated storage the same as climate controlled storage?** No. Heated storage keeps your unit warm in cold months, while climate controlled storage holds a set temperature range all year and may also control humidity.

**How much more does a heated storage unit cost?** A heated unit usually costs about 15% to 30% more than a standard unit of the same size. A heated 10x10 typically runs around $130 to $170 a month.

**Do I need heated storage for furniture?** If you live where winters drop below freezing, heated storage helps keep wood and upholstered furniture from cracking or warping. In mild climates, a standard unit is often fine for short stays.

**Can electronics be stored in an unheated unit in winter?** It is risky. Extreme cold can damage screens, batteries, and internal parts, so a heated or climate controlled unit is the safer choice for electronics.

**Are heated self storage units available near me?** Many facilities in cold-winter areas offer heated or temperature-controlled units. Search by your city and filter for climate or temperature control to compare options.

**When is the best time to rent a heated storage unit?** Fall and winter are often the best times to book, since moving demand is lower and many facilities run move-in deals.
